Types of questions asked in JEE Main 2019–2025 (86 questions)

Key message: these 86 questions fall into only 7 repeating types. The three biggest types (Types 1, 2, 3) account for 42 questions (49%). Titration questions (Types 1, 2 and 3) alone are 42 questions (49%). About 43% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Redox titration calculations (KMnO₄, K₂Cr₂O₇, hypo) – 14 questions

What is asked: Find a molarity, a volume, a strength in g/L or the purity of a sample from the volume of an oxidising agent used up in a titration.

What to master: The n-factor of each species; equivalents on both sides are equal at the end point; the reactions of MnO₄⁻, Cr₂O₇²⁻, S₂O₃²⁻ and I⁻; the molar mass of Mohr's salt and oxalic acid dihydrate.

Forms that appear: KMnO₄ against ferrous sulphate or oxalic acid, 15 mL of Fe²⁺ with 0.03 M dichromate, ferrous oxalate with K₂Cr₂O₇, purity of H₂O₂ with KMnO₄, Cu²⁺ or MnO₂ determined by hypo, a mixture of four iron salts oxidised by KMnO₄.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (2), 2021 (5), 2022 (4), 2024 (1), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

KMnO₄ titrations: Mohr's salt, oxalic acid, H₂O₂, FeSO₄ and mixtures of Fe(II) salts: 6 questions

K₂Cr₂O₇ and other oxidants with Fe²⁺, ferrous oxalate and thiosulphate: 5 questions

Iodometry: hypo with liberated iodine, and iodide–iodate reactions: 3 questions

Type 2: Acid–base neutralisation and volumetric calculations – 14 questions

What is asked: Find the volume or amount of an acid or base needed for neutralisation, the basicity of an acid, or the composition of a mixture from titration data.

What to master: The n-factor (basicity or acidity) of the acid and base; moles of H⁺ and OH⁻ before and after mixing; behaviour of Na₂CO₃ with phenolphthalein and methyl orange; the Kjeldahl method.

Forms that appear: HBr with Ba(OH)₂, unreacted HCl after adding NaOH, oxalic acid against NaOH, H₃PO₃ and H₃PO₂ with NaOH, a mixture of NaOH and Na₂CO₃, nitrogen by Kjeldahl, CO₂ passed into Ca(OH)₂, a monobasic acid of given density.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2021 (5), 2022 (3), 2023 (3), 2024 (1), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Neutralisation: volume or amount of acid or base, basicity, unreacted acid: 8 questions

Mixtures, back titration, double indicator and burette readings: 6 questions

Type 3: Titration theory: indicators, primary standards and conditions – 14 questions

What is asked: Choose correct statements about indicators, primary standards, titration curves and the conditions used in permanganate titrations.

What to master: Colours and pH range of phenolphthalein and methyl orange; properties of a primary standard; why HCl is avoided with KMnO₄; heating in the oxalic acid titration; shape of pH and conductometric curves.

Forms that appear: statements on redox versus acid–base indicators, which compounds are not primary standards, heating of oxalic acid with KMnO₄, potassium hydrogen phthalate with phenolphthalein, assertion–reason on KOH and KMnO₄, graph of pH when NaOH is added to HCl.

Asked in: 2020 (3), 2022 (3), 2023 (3), 2024 (3), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Indicators, pH changes and titration curves: 7 questions

Primary standards and conditions for permanganate titrations: 7 questions

Type 4: Balancing redox equations and electron change – 12 questions

What is asked: Balance an equation to find a coefficient, the sum of coefficients or the number of molecules of water, or find the electrons exchanged in a half-reaction.

What to master: The ion-electron method in acidic and basic medium; standard half-reactions of MnO₄⁻ and Cr₂O₇²⁻; change in oxidation state × number of atoms = electrons.

Forms that appear: IO₃⁻ with I⁻, Cl₂ in alkali, Cr₂O₇²⁻ with Fe²⁺, MnO₄⁻ with C₂O₄²⁻, MnO₄⁻ with I⁻ to MnO₂, S₈ in alkali, water produced from KMnO₄ and ferrous ammonium sulphate, the chromyl chloride reaction chain.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (2), 2023 (5), 2024 (3)

Which form was asked:

Balancing a redox equation and finding coefficients or atom counts: 9 questions

Electrons gained or lost and change in oxidation state in a half-reaction: 3 questions

Type 5: Types of redox reactions and oxidising or reducing agents – 12 questions

What is asked: Name the type of a redox reaction, or pick the oxidising or reducing agent and compare their strength.

What to master: Combination, decomposition, displacement and disproportionation; species with highest, lowest and intermediate oxidation states; comparing E° values.

Forms that appear: Au(CN)₂⁻ displaced by zinc, match the reaction with its type, H₂O₂ with HOCl or with I₂, thiosulphate with I₂ and with Br₂, Cu²⁺ with KI, E° of O₂, S₂O₈²⁻, Br₂ and Au³⁺.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (2), 2021 (2), 2023 (2), 2024 (3), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Classifying a reaction (combination, decomposition, displacement, redox): 4 questions

Oxidising and reducing agents, and their strength: 8 questions

Type 6: Disproportionation reactions – 11 questions

What is asked: Identify which species or reactions undergo disproportionation, or find the oxidation states of the products.

What to master: A species disproportionates only if its element has an intermediate oxidation state and the higher and lower states are stable; known examples in acidic and alkaline medium.

Forms that appear: Which of Cl₂, Br₂, I₂ and F₂, a list of nine species, S₈ in alkali, ClO₄⁻, MnO₄²⁻ in acid, Cu⁺, the difference in oxidation states of the two ions formed by Mn(VI).

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (3), 2024 (4), 2025 (1)

Type 7: Oxidation number of an element – 9 questions

What is asked: Find the oxidation number of an element in a compound, ion or reaction product.

What to master: Rules for oxidation numbers; special cases of peroxides, superoxides and polythionic acids; the product of a given redox reaction.

Forms that appear: N in NO, NO₂, N₂O and NO₃⁻; metals in K₂Cr₂O₇, KMnO₄ and K₂FeO₄; P in H₄P₂O₇, H₄P₂O₅ and H₄P₂O₆; K in K₂O, K₂O₂ and KO₂; S in polythionic acids; Cr after base; S after oxidation by MnO₄⁻.

Asked in: 2020 (2), 2021 (6), 2024 (1)

Which form was asked:

Oxidation number of an element in a compound or ion: 6 questions

Oxidation state of the element in a product or from its position: 3 questions

5. Suggested study order for students

Start with titrations (Types 1, 2 and 3): 42 questions (49%). The n-factor method solves nearly all of them.

Then balancing and electron change (Type 4) and reaction types and agents (Type 5): 24 questions (28%).

Then disproportionation and oxidation numbers (Types 6 and 7): short, quick marks.

6. A routine to follow for every question

Write the half-reactions (or the species and the products) and note the medium: acidic, basic or neutral.

Find the n-factor of every species from the change in oxidation number × number of atoms.

At the end point, equivalents of oxidant = equivalents of reductant: write n × M × V on both sides.

For a statement or identification question, check the oxidation numbers: intermediate state means both oxidising and reducing.

Check the unit, the rounding asked (nearest integer) and that the answer is sensible.
