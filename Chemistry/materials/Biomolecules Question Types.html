JEE Main Chemistry – Biomolecules

Types of questions asked in JEE Main 2019–2025 (150 questions)

Key message: these 150 questions fall into only 7 repeating types. The three biggest types (Types 1, 2, 3) account for 83 questions (55%). The two carbohydrate types (Types 1 and 2) are 61 questions (41%). About 17% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Disaccharides and polysaccharides: linkages, hydrolysis and reducing property – 31 questions

What is asked: Name the monomers or the glycosidic linkage of a disaccharide or polysaccharide, say whether it is reducing, or give its hydrolysis products.

What to master: Maltose, lactose, sucrose; starch, amylose, amylopectin, cellulose and glycogen; α and β linkages; invert sugar; reducing and non-reducing sugars; structures and statements on them.

Forms that appear: the two monomers of maltose, the linkage in sucrose and lactose, which structure is the α-anomer of maltose, which sugar does not give Fehling's test, what amylopectin is made of.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (4), 2021 (10), 2022 (6), 2024 (2), 2025 (5)

Which form was asked:

Monomers, glycosidic linkage and structure of maltose, lactose and sucrose: 12 questions

Reducing and non-reducing sugars, Fehling's and Tollens' tests: 4 questions

Hydrolysis products, invert sugar and enzymes: 6 questions

Starch, amylose, amylopectin, cellulose and glycogen: 9 questions

Type 2: Monosaccharides: structure, configuration, reactions and tests – 30 questions

What is asked: Choose the structure (Fischer, Haworth) of a monosaccharide, or give the product of a reaction or the result of a test on glucose and fructose.

What to master: D and L sugars; open-chain and cyclic (pyranose, furanose) forms; anomers and epimers; reducing and non-reducing structures; Br₂ water, HNO₃, HI, acetic anhydride, HCN; Molisch, Barfoed, Seliwanoff, Fehling and Tollens tests.

Forms that appear: structure of L-glucose and L-fructose, pyranose form of a given sugar, number of stereocentres in glucose, product of glucose with HNO₃ or HI, how to tell glucose from fructose.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (5), 2021 (2), 2022 (6), 2023 (4), 2024 (5), 2025 (5)

Which form was asked:

D/L configuration and open-chain (Fischer) structure of glucose and fructose: 9 questions

Pyranose and furanose (Haworth) structures, anomers and ring structures: 6 questions

Reactions of glucose and fructose: 9 questions

Chemical tests for carbohydrates: 6 questions

Type 3: Proteins: structure levels, denaturation and identification tests – 22 questions

What is asked: Name the level of protein structure or the forces that stabilise it, say what denaturation changes, or choose the test for a protein or amino acid.

What to master: Primary to quaternary structure; α-helix and β-sheet; fibrous and globular proteins; denaturation; Biuret, ninhydrin and xanthoproteic tests; Ruhemann's purple.

Forms that appear: which structure remains intact on boiling an egg, what stabilises the α-helix, how many of the given compounds give a positive Biuret test, the structure of Ruhemann's purple.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2021 (7), 2022 (4), 2023 (2), 2024 (3), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary structure; denaturation: 12 questions

Tests for proteins and amino acids: 10 questions

Type 4: Amino acids: classification, structure, zwitterions and pKa – 20 questions

What is asked: Identify the essential, acidic or basic amino acid, choose or analyse a structure, or state the charge or form of an amino acid at a given pH.

What to master: Essential amino acids; one-letter codes; sulphur-containing and basic amino acids; chiral centres; zwitterion; charges at a given pH; pKa order; solubility and melting point.

Forms that appear: which are essential amino acids, one-letter code of arginine, form of valine at pH 2 and 10, structure of histidine at pH 2, negative charge on a tetrapeptide at pH 12.5.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (2), 2021 (2), 2023 (7), 2024 (3), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Classification: essential amino acids, one-letter codes, acidic and basic: 9 questions

Structure, chirality, zwitterions, pKa and charge of amino acids: 11 questions

Type 5: Peptides: linkage, sequence and counting – 18 questions

What is asked: Count the peptide bonds or the possible peptides, find the sequence or the product, or calculate the molar mass of a protein.

What to master: Peptide bond; n! sequences; cyclic peptides; hydrolysis products; acylation of a dipeptide; sp² carbons and chiral carbons in a peptide; molar mass from percentage composition and osmotic pressure.

Forms that appear: number of tripeptides from three different amino acids, peptide linkages in a peptide of 7 residues, product of Asn–Ser with excess acetic anhydride, glycine units in a protein from osmotic pressure.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (2), 2021 (1), 2022 (4), 2023 (5), 2024 (1), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Counting peptide bonds and possible peptides: 9 questions

Sequence, formation and structure of a given peptide: 7 questions

Molar mass of a protein from composition or osmotic pressure: 2 questions

Type 6: Nucleic acids: bases, nucleotides and base pairing – 14 questions

What is asked: Name or choose the structure of a base, give the complementary strand or the number of hydrogen bonds, or say what DNA and RNA contain.

What to master: Purines and pyrimidines; adenine, guanine, cytosine, thymine and uracil; A–T and G–C pairing; nucleotide and sugar; phosphodiester link; functions of DNA and RNA.

Forms that appear: which is the incorrect base structure, complementary base of thymine, hydrogen bonds in a DNA strand of 13 bases, oxygen atoms in a uracil nucleotide, sugar of DNA and RNA.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2021 (3), 2022 (2), 2023 (1), 2024 (4), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Bases, base pairing and complementary strands, hydrogen bonds: 9 questions

Nucleotides, sugars and general facts about DNA and RNA: 5 questions

Type 7: Vitamins: classification, deficiency diseases and structure – 12 questions

Forms that appear: fat-soluble vitamins, how many vitamins can be stored in the body, match vitamins with deficiency diseases, which vitamin is not excreted easily, vitamin that helps blood clotting.

5. Suggested study order for students

Start with carbohydrates (Types 1 and 2): 61 questions (41%). The linkages of the three disaccharides and the Fischer and Haworth structures of glucose are the base.

Then proteins and amino acids (Types 3, 4 and 5): 60 questions (40%); learn the tests, the essential amino acids and the counting rules.

Then nucleic acids and vitamins (Types 6 and 7): 26 questions (17%); mostly recall of tables.

6. A routine to follow for every question

Read what is asked: a name, a structure, a count, a match or a statement; note whether the options are pictures.

For a sugar structure decide D or L from the last stereocentre first, then the ring size and α or β from the anomeric OH.

For statement and match questions mark the sure facts first and eliminate options (for example sucrose is non-reducing, cellulose is β).

For counting questions (peptides, bonds, chiral centres, hydrogen bonds) write the structure or sequence out and count item by item.

Memorise the short tables: essential amino acids, one-letter codes, vitamins with their diseases, tests and what they detect.
