Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• A lanthanoid or actinoid atom or ion is given and its configuration or number of f electrons is asked.

• 'Which ion is the strongest oxidising or reducing agent?' or 'which lanthanoid shows +2 or +4?'

• Colourless, diamagnetic or coloured Ln ions, or a statement on lanthanoid contraction.

2. Formulae and laws

Ln atom

[Xe]4fn5d0 or 16s2

Ln​3+ ion

4fn (remove 6s2 and 5d1)

Stable f shells

f0: La​3+, Ce​4+; f7: Eu​2+, Gd​3+, Tb​4+; f14: Yb​2+, Lu​3+

Spin-only moment

μ=n(n+2) BM; Gd​3+: 63=7.9 BM

3. Concepts and skills

Why is Ce​4+ an oxidant?

It reverts to the common +3 state; E∘(Ce4+/Ce3+)=+1.74 V.

Which divalent Ln ion is the most stable?

Eu​2+ (f7); Yb​2+ (f14) and Sm​2+ are strong reductants.

Which Ln ions are colourless?

Those with f0 or f14 (La​3+, Lu​3+, Ce​4+); ions with unpaired f electrons (Eu​3+, Sm​3+, Nd​3+) are coloured.

What is lanthanoid contraction?

Atomic and ionic radii decrease across the series because 4f electrons shield poorly; it makes 4d and 5d radii nearly equal (Mo, W; Ag, Au).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Counting only 4f electrons when the question asks for 'f electrons in the ground state' of an actinoid (Np has 4f145f4, total 18).

• Forgetting that Gd keeps a 5d1 electron, so Gd​2+ is 4f75d1.

• Calling Eu​3+ colourless.

• Mixing up lanthanoid and actinoid contraction.

Completed notes – Type 2

1. How do I spot this type?

• A metal ion is named and μ or the number of unpaired electrons is asked (often to the nearest integer).

• The ion must first be found: 'the strongest oxidising agent among...', 'the most basic oxide of vanadium', 'the metal with highest enthalpy of atomisation'.

• The product of a KMnO​4 or chromite reaction is found and its μ is added or subtracted.

2. Formulae and laws

Spin-only magnetic moment

μ=n(n+2) BM

Values

n=1:1.73; 2:2.83; 3:3.87; 4:4.90; 5:5.92 BM

3d ions

M​2+ = 3dn (Ti​2+ d2, V​2+ d3, Cr​2+ d4, Mn​2+ d5, Fe​2+ d6, Co​2+ d7, Ni​2+ d8)

d0 and d10

μ=0: MnO​4−, CrO​42−, VO​2+, Zn​2+, Cu​+

3. Concepts and skills

Which oxidation state is the strongest oxidant?

The highest one, or an ion that gains stability: Mn​3+ (d4→d5), Co​3+, Mn​4+ in MnF​4.

Which 3d metal ions liberate H​2 from dilute acid?

Ti​2+, V​2+, Cr​2+ (negative E∘ for M​3+/M​2+); Co​3+ cannot.

Maximum μ among d-block complexes?

5.92 BM (d5, five unpaired).

Which oxide is most basic?

The lowest oxidation state: V​2O​3 (V​3+, d2, 2.83 BM).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Removing 3d electrons before 4s when forming the ion.

• Using n (unpaired electrons) instead of n(n+2).

• Rounding the sum of two moments (add first, then round).

• Taking the wrong species as 'strongest oxidant' (use oxidation state and the stability of the product).

3. Concepts and skills

Alloy compositions?

Brass Cu+Zn; bronze Cu+Sn; German silver Cu+Zn+Ni; gun metal Cu+Sn+Zn; stainless steel Fe+Cr+Ni+C.

Why is dilute H​2SO​4 added to Mohr's salt?

To prevent hydrolysis of Fe​2+ (and oxidation on prolonged heating).

Which metal gives H​2 with both acid and alkali?

Zinc (amphoteric).

Which catalyst for which process?

V​2O​5 contact; Ni hydrogenation; ZSM-5 cracking; CuCl​2 Deacon; Pd/BaSO​4 or Pd/CaCO​3 partially poisoned Lindlar.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Putting nickel in brass instead of zinc.

• Writing Fehling's active reagent as Cu(I) (it is Cu(II)).

• Confusing the brown-ring test (nitrate) with the acetate test (red colour with neutral FeCl​3).

• Counting the H-bonded protons in Ni(dmg)​2 among those 'not involved in H-bonding'.

2. Formulae and laws

3. Concepts and skills

What does KMnO​4 give on heating at 513 K?

K2MnO4 (green, paramagnetic) + MnO2 + O2.

What happens to K​2MnO​4 in neutral or acidic solution?

It disproportionates to MnO​4− and MnO​2.

Magnetic character?

MnO​4− is diamagnetic (d0); MnO​42− is paramagnetic (d1).

Why is HCl not used in permanganate titrations?

MnO​4− oxidises Cl​− to Cl​2.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Writing MnO​2 as the product in acidic medium.

• Giving I​2 instead of IO​3− in neutral or alkaline medium.

• Taking the change in Mn oxidation state as 5 for every medium.

• Calling manganate diamagnetic.

Completed notes – Type 5

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'The metal with the highest … enthalpy ...' or 'the correct order of ...'.

• Which ion or metal is the strongest reducing or oxidising agent, or liberates H​2.

• Several statements on Zn, Cd, Hg, or on Cr, Mo and W.

2. Formulae and laws

Highest IE​3

Mn (Mn2+=3d5 is stable)

Enthalpy of atomisation

highest V (about 515 kJ/mol), lowest Zn

E∘(M2+/M)

positive only for Cu; most negative for Mn and Zn

Density

Zn < Cr < Fe < Co < Cu

3. Concepts and skills

Why does Mn​2+ have the lowest hydration enthalpy?

d5 spherical distribution, weaker ligand-field stabilisation.

Why are Mo and W radii equal?

Lanthanoid contraction offsets the extra shell of W.

Why is Zn, Cd, Hg 'soft'?

d10: no unpaired d electrons, weak metallic bonding, low melting points.

Which is more stable down the group?

The higher oxidation state (W(VI) over Cr(VI)), unlike the p-block.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Comparing IE of different oxidation states (IE of M​+ with IE of M​2+ gives the expected order, but Mn​2+ and Fe​2+ reverse).

• Saying Cu liberates H​2.

• Treating Zn as a transition metal when counting atomisation enthalpy.

• The chromyl chloride test and the colour of the solution, CrO​5 or the chromium oxidation state.

• K​2Cr​2O​7 in acidic solution and the colour change, or a fact about its structure.

CrO2Cl2+4NaOH→Na2CrO4+2NaCl+2H2O

3. Concepts and skills

Why does K​2Cr​2O​7 paper turn green with SO​2?

Cr(VI) is reduced to Cr​3+ (green).

What is the oxidation state of Cr in CrO​5?

+6 (one oxo and two peroxide groups, not +10).

Structure of dichromate?

Two tetrahedral CrO​4 sharing one O: non-linear symmetrical Cr–O–Cr; 8 Cr–O bonds (chromate has 4).

Why is K​2Cr​2O​7 a primary standard?

It is not hygroscopic; Na​2Cr​2O​7 is.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking CrO​5 as +10.

• Using Na​2Cr​2O​7 as the primary standard.

• Saying chromate is square planar.

• Giving the colour of Cr(VI) as green (Cr​3+ is green).

Which element shows only one oxidation state?

Which element has the maximum number of oxidation states?

Mn (+2 to +7).

Common positive oxidation states of Cr?

+2 to +6.

Which elements have d10 in the ground state?

Cu, Zn, Cd, Ag and Pd.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Writing the Cr and Cu configurations as 4s2.

• Counting Ni and Pd as the same (4d10 for Pd, not Ni).

• Forgetting that the highest oxidation state is limited to the number of valence electrons.

Completed notes – Type 8

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'Both ions are colourless' or 'the set of ions coloured and paramagnetic'.

• The colour of Fe​2+ and Fe​3+ or the origin of the colour of K​2Cr​2O​7.

• The nature of an oxide of V, Cr or Mn.

2. Formulae and laws

Colourless

d0 (Sc​3+, Ti​4+) and d10 (Zn​2+, Cu​+)

Colour of aqueous ions

Cu​2+ blue, Cr​3+ violet, Ni​2+ green, Fe​2+ pale green, Fe​3+ yellow, Mn​2+ pale pink

Oxides of vanadium

V​2O​3 basic; V​2O​4; V​2O​5 amphoteric

Oxides of chromium and manganese

CrO basic; Cr​2O​3 amphoteric; CrO​3 and Mn​2O​7 acidic

3. Concepts and skills

What causes the colour of KMnO​4 and K​2Cr​2O​7?

Charge transfer (O→M), not d–d transitions.

What causes the colour of CuSO​4⋅5H​2O?

d–d transitions (d9).

Why does acidity rise with oxidation state?

A higher charge polarises the M–O bond more, so the oxide is more covalent and acidic.

What colours ruby?

Cr​3+ in Al​2O​3.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Calling Cr​3+ green in water (it is violet; green is the colour of its chloro complexes).

• Saying d–d transitions give colour in KMnO​4 (Mn(VII) is d0).

• Treating V​2O​5 as purely acidic (it is amphoteric).
