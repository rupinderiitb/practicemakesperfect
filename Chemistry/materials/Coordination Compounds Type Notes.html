Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• A complex is named and μ (in BM) or the number of unpaired electrons is asked, often to the nearest integer.

• 'Arrange the complexes in increasing order of spin-only magnetic moment', or 'which pair has the same μ?'

• 'The number of paramagnetic (or diamagnetic) complexes among...' or 'the diamagnetic octahedral complex is'.

2. Formulae and laws

Spin-only moment

μ=n(n+2) BM

Values

n=1:1.73; 2:2.83; 3:3.87; 4:4.90; 5:5.92 BM

Strong-field ligand

CN​−, CO, en, NO​2−, NH​3: pairing, low spin (Δ0>P)

Weak-field ligand

F​−, Cl​−, Br​−, H​2O, SCN​−: high spin (Δ0<P)

3. Concepts and skills

How many unpaired electrons in a d6 octahedral complex?

Low spin (t2g6): 0, e.g. [Fe(CN)6]4−, [Co(NH3)6]3+. High spin (t2g4eg2): 4, e.g. [FeF6]4−, [CoF6]3−.

Which d8 complexes are diamagnetic?

Square planar ones with a strong-field ligand: [Ni(CN)4]2−, [Pt(NH3)2Cl2]. [NiCl4]2− is tetrahedral and has 2 unpaired electrons.

What is the μ of [Mn(CN)6]3−?

Mn​3+ is d4, low spin t2g4: 2 unpaired electrons, μ=2.83 BM.

Do d10 and d0 ions have a magnetic moment?

No: Zn​2+, Cu​+ and Sc​3+ are diamagnetic.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the metal's atomic configuration instead of that of the ion (remove 4s electrons first).

• Treating every [Fe(CN)6]3− as high spin (CN​− is a strong-field ligand: 1 unpaired electron).

• Assuming all Ni(II) complexes are square planar: [NiCl4]2− is tetrahedral.

• Rounding before adding two moments.

Completed notes – Type 2

1. How do I spot this type?

• The d-electron configuration (t2gxegy) of a complex or the number of electrons in t2 or eg is asked.

• The CFSE of a complex is asked, or complexes are put in the order of CFSE.

• A statement is judged: 'which cannot be explained by crystal field theory', 'which distort (Jahn–Teller)', 'difference in unpaired electrons between high spin and low spin'.

2. Formulae and laws

Octahedral CFSE

(−0.4x+0.6y)Δ0+ pairing energy (x electrons in t2g, y in eg)

Tetrahedral splitting

Δt=4/9Δ0; CFSE =(−0.6x+0.4y)Δt with x in e, y in t2

High spin or low spin

Δ0<P: high spin; Δ0>P: low spin (possible only for d4 to d7)

Zero CFSE

d0, d10 and high-spin d5

3. Concepts and skills

What is the configuration of [Co(en)3]3+?

Co​3+ is d6, en is strong-field: t2g6eg0.

Which complex has the highest CFSE among d6, d5, d10 ions?

The low-spin d6 complex: −2.4Δ0+2P.

Why do d9 complexes distort?

Uneven filling of eg (eg3) lifts the degeneracy (Jahn–Teller); high-spin d4 also distorts.

Which d orbitals point at the ligands in an octahedral complex?

dx2−y2 and dz2 (eg set); dxy, dyz, dxz (t2g) lie between the axes.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Forgetting the pairing energy when comparing a low-spin and a high-spin complex.

• Using −0.4 and +0.6 for a tetrahedral complex (it is −0.6 for e and +0.4 for t2, times Δt).

• Writing eg for the lower set in an octahedral field (t2g is lower).

• Calling a d3 or d8 complex 'high spin' or 'low spin': there is only one arrangement.

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'The number of ambidentate ligands', 'the denticity of EDTA, biuret or the ligand in Fehling's reagent', 'which complex is homoleptic'.

• The oxidation state or the coordination number of the metal is asked, often as a sum for two complexes.

• Moles of AgCl precipitated, the number of ions, or the primary and secondary valency of a CoCl​3⋅xNH​3 type compound.

2. Formulae and laws

Oxidation state

charge of complex − sum of charges of the ligands

Coordination number

number of donor atoms attached to the metal (en=2, ox =2, EDTA =6)

Primary and secondary valency

Primary = ionisable charge; secondary = coordination number

AgNO​3 test

moles of AgCl = number of Cl​− outside the bracket

3. Concepts and skills

Which ligands are ambidentate?

NO​2− (N or O), SCN​− (S or N), CN​− (C or N); CO32− and C2O42− are not.

What is a homoleptic complex?

One with a single kind of ligand, e.g. [Co(NH3)6]3+, [Cr(CO)6].

Why is a chelate more stable?

The chelate effect: a polydentate ligand gains entropy when it displaces several monodentate ligands.

How many ions does [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2 give?

3 ions (1 complex cation and 2 Cl​−); only the 2 Cl​− outside give AgCl.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Counting a Cl​− inside the bracket as precipitated by AgNO​3.

• Taking the denticity as the number of atoms in the ligand instead of the number of donor atoms.

• Using the charge of the ligand wrongly (NO is +1 in nitroprusside, CO and NH​3 are neutral).

• Forgetting that coordination number and oxidation state are different (en counts 2 in coordination number).

Completed notes – Type 4

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'Which complex shows facial–meridional isomerism' or 'the number of geometrical isomers of ... is'.

• 'The number of optical isomers (or stereoisomers) of [Cr(ox)2Cl2]3−...' or 'the complex that can show optical activity'.

• 'Total number of isomers' of a square planar or octahedral complex, with linkage or ionisation isomers included.

2. Formulae and laws

[MA2B2] square planar, [MA4B2] octahedral

2 geometrical isomers (cis, trans)

[MA3B3]

2 geometrical isomers (fac, mer)

[M(AA)3] and cis-[M(AA)2B2]

2 optical isomers each (chiral)

[MABCD] square planar

3 geometrical isomers

3. Concepts and skills

Which complexes show no geometrical isomerism?

Tetrahedral complexes, [MA5B], [M(AA)3] and [MA2B2] tetrahedral.

How many stereoisomers does [Co(ox)2BrCl]3− have?

Count the geometrical forms, then add 2 optical isomers for every chiral form.

When is a complex optically active?

When it has neither a plane nor a centre of symmetry.

What kinds of structural isomerism occur?

Ionisation, hydrate, linkage (ambidentate ligands) and coordination isomerism.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Counting a pair of enantiomers as one isomer.

• Giving a square planar [MABCD] only 2 isomers (it has 3).

• Calling trans-[M(AA)2B2] optically active (it has a plane of symmetry).

• Forgetting linkage isomers when the ligand is ambidentate.

Completed notes – Type 5

1. How do I spot this type?

• The hybridisation of the metal in a given complex is asked, or complexes are matched with d2sp3, sp3d2, dsp2, sp3.

• The hybridisation and magnetic behaviour of [MnCl6]3−, [Mn(CN)6]3−, [Ni(CN)4]2− are asked together.

• A shape is asked: square pyramidal, trigonal bipyramidal, or the geometry around a metal.

2. Formulae and laws

Inner-orbital complex

strong-field ligand, d2sp3, usually low spin

Outer-orbital complex

weak-field ligand, sp3d2, high spin

Four-coordinate

tetrahedral sp3; square planar dsp2

Five-coordinate

trigonal bipyramidal dsp3 or square pyramidal

3. Concepts and skills

What is the hybridisation of [Ni(CN)4]2−?

dsp2 (square planar, diamagnetic); [NiCl4]2− is sp3 (tetrahedral, paramagnetic).

Which is the inner-orbital complex: [FeF6]3− or [Fe(CN)6]3−?

[Fe(CN)6]3− (d2sp3, 1 unpaired electron).

Why does [CrF6]3− use d2sp3?

Cr​3+ (d3) already has two empty 3d orbitals, so no pairing is needed.

What is the geometry of [Al(H2O)6]3+?

Octahedral.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking sp3d2 for every weak-field complex (d3 octahedral complexes such as [CrF6]3− are d2sp3; d8 octahedral ones such as [Ni(NH3)6]2+ are sp3d2).

• Mixing up sp3d2 (outer) and d2sp3 (inner).

• Assuming Ni(II) is always square planar.

Completed notes – Type 6

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'The correct order of ligands in increasing field strength' or 'the complex with highest crystal field splitting'.

• The wavelength absorbed by one complex is given and that of another is asked, or colours are ordered.

• An absorption spectrum or the colour of a solution is described and the ligand strength is deduced.

2. Formulae and laws

Spectrochemical series

I​−< Br​−< SCN​−< Cl​−< F​−< OH​−< C​2O​42−< H​2O < NH​3< en < bpy < NO​2−< CN​−< CO

Δ0 and the metal

Δ0 rises with the oxidation state of the metal and down a group

Energy absorbed

E=Δ0=hcλ: larger Δ0, shorter λ

Colour seen

the complementary colour of the light absorbed

3. Concepts and skills

Which absorbs at the shortest wavelength?

The complex with the largest Δ0: strongest ligand and highest oxidation state.

Why is [Zn(H2O)6]2+ colourless?

d10: no d–d transition is possible.

What colour change occurs when en replaces H​2O in [Ni(H2O)6]2+?

Green to blue and then violet, as Δ0 increases and the absorption shifts to shorter wavelength.

Why do [CoCl(NH3)5]2+ and [Co(CN)6]3− differ in colour?

CN​− is stronger than Cl​−, so Δ0 is larger and the absorption is at shorter wavelength.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Reversing the series (I​− is the weakest ligand, CO and CN​− the strongest).

• Confusing the colour absorbed with the colour seen.

• Ordering Δ0 by ligand only and ignoring a change of metal ion.

Why does AgCl dissolve in aqueous NH​3?

It forms the soluble complex [Ag(NH3)2]+.

Which catalyst is a rhodium complex?

Wilkinson catalyst [RhCl(PPh3)3].

What colour is [Co(H2O)6]2+?

Pink; with conc. HCl it forms blue tetrahedral [CoCl4]2−.

Why is EDTA used in lead poisoning?

It chelates Pb​2+ into a soluble complex that is excreted.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Matching Mg with haemoglobin (it is Fe; Mg is in chlorophyll).

• Calling Ni–DMG a 6-membered chelate ring (the rings are 5-membered).

• Mixing up the colour of [CoCl4]2− (blue) and [Co(H2O)6]2+ (pink).

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'The number of bridging CO ligands in Co2(CO)8, Mn2(CO)10 or W(CO)6'.

2. Formulae and laws

Synergic bonding

M←CO σ donation plus M→CO π back donation

Co2(CO)8

2 bridging CO, 1 Co–Co bond, 6 terminal CO

Mn2(CO)10

no bridging CO, 1 Mn–Mn bond, each Mn octahedral

Fe2(CO)9

3. Concepts and skills

Why are low oxidation states stabilised in carbonyls?

The empty π* orbitals of CO accept electron density from the metal (back bonding).

Why is Mn2(CO)10 organometallic?

It contains metal–carbon bonds (M–C of CO).

What happens to the C–O bond on back bonding?

It lengthens and weakens (lower stretching frequency).

Do Cr(CO)6 and W(CO)6 have bridging CO?

No: all 6 CO are terminal.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Counting terminal CO as bridging.

• Forgetting the metal–metal bond in Mn2(CO)10.

• Saying the CO ligand is a weak π donor (it is a strong π acceptor).

1. How do I spot this type?

• A formula is given and the IUPAC name is asked, or the reverse.

• The name of an anionic complex such as K2MnO4 or K3[Co(C2O4)3].

• A complex with mixed ligands, where the order of ligands matters.

2. Formulae and laws

Order

ligands in alphabetical order (prefixes di, tri ignored), then the metal

Anionic complex

the metal ends in -ate: ferrate, cobaltate, platinate

Anionic ligands

end in -o: chlorido, bromido, oxalato, nitrito-N

Oxidation state

Roman numeral in brackets after the metal

3. Concepts and skills

How is K3[Co(C2O4)3] named?

Potassium trioxalatocobaltate(III).

Which comes first in the name?

The cation, then the anion.

How are ligands such as en or ox counted?

With bis-, tris- if the name already has a prefix, e.g. bis(ethane-1,2-diamine).

What is the oxidation state of Mn in K2MnO4?

+6: potassium tetraoxidomanganate(VI).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Listing ligands by charge instead of alphabetically.

• Writing '-ate' for the metal in a cationic complex.

• Forgetting the oxidation state or giving the wrong Roman numeral.
