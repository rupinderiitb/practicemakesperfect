3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Isomerism: structural, geometrical, optical and conformational – 49 questions

What is asked: Count or identify isomers of a compound, decide whether a compound shows geometrical or optical isomerism, or compare conformations.

What to master: Chain, position, functional group and metamerism; cis–trans and E–Z; chiral centres and 2ⁿ; Newman projections of ethane and butane; enol content.

Forms that appear: number of stereoisomers of 5-phenylpent-4-en-2-ol, optical isomers of 2-chlorobutane, geometrical isomers of a given structure, chain isomers of C₇H₁₆, staggered and eclipsed conformers, highest enol content.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (3), 2021 (9), 2022 (8), 2023 (4), 2024 (14), 2025 (8)

Which form was asked:

Structural isomerism (chain, position, functional group, metamerism) and enol content: 16 questions

Geometrical (cis–trans, E–Z) isomerism: 8 questions

Optical isomerism: chiral centres and counting stereoisomers: 17 questions

Conformations of ethane and n-butane (Newman projections): 8 questions

Type 2: IUPAC naming, structures from names, classification and bond counting – 49 questions

What is asked: Write the IUPAC name of a drawn compound, draw a compound from its name, or classify it and count its σ and π bonds and hybridisation.

What to master: Principal functional group and priority order; longest chain and lowest locants; common names (catechol, mesityl oxide); σ/π counting; sp³/sp²/sp.

Forms that appear: name of a compound with −CHO, −Br, −NO₂ and a ring, structure of 4-methylpent-2-enal, σ and π bonds in 2-oxohex-4-ynoic acid, hybridisation of carbons a, b and c.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (3), 2021 (5), 2022 (7), 2023 (1), 2024 (23), 2025 (6)

Which form was asked:

Writing the IUPAC name of a given structure: 24 questions

Drawing a structure from its name; common names and priority order: 9 questions

Classification, hybridisation and counting σ and π bonds: 16 questions

Type 3: Purification of organic compounds: chromatography and separation methods – 46 questions

What is asked: Choose the correct purification method for a mixture, interpret a chromatogram, or calculate Rf.

What to master: Principle of each method: distillation, fractional distillation, steam distillation, vacuum distillation, crystallisation, extraction, sublimation, TLC, column and paper chromatography.

Forms that appear: Rf from a TLC plate, order of elution on a column, steam volatile compounds, separating glycerol from spent lye, separating aniline and chloroform, match-the-list of mixtures and techniques.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (3), 2021 (6), 2022 (7), 2023 (8), 2024 (13), 2025 (5)

Which form was asked:

Chromatography: TLC, column, paper, Rf value: 25 questions

Choosing a purification method (distillation, steam distillation, crystallisation, extraction, sublimation): 21 questions

Type 4: Quantitative analysis: Dumas, Kjeldahl, Carius and Sulphur estimation – 41 questions

What is asked: Find the percentage of N, halogen or S from the experimental data, or answer a theory question about the method.

What to master: Dumas and Kjeldahl for nitrogen (volume at STP, normality); Carius for halogens and sulphur (AgX, BaSO₄); which compounds Kjeldahl cannot be used for.

Forms that appear: percentage of bromine from 0.45 g compound giving 0.36 g AgBr, nitrogen from 30 mL N₂ at 287 K and 758 mm Hg, sulphur from BaSO₄, number of moles of CuO in Dumas method.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (5), 2021 (11), 2022 (7), 2023 (4), 2024 (3), 2025 (10)

Which form was asked:

Nitrogen estimation: Dumas and Kjeldahl methods: 24 questions

Halogen and sulphur estimation: Carius method: 17 questions

Type 5: Acidity and basicity orders – 32 questions

What is asked: Arrange compounds in order of acid or base strength, or pick the strongest acid or base, from the structures given.

What to master: Stability of the conjugate base; inductive and resonance effects; hybridisation and acidity; amine and aniline basicity; pKa and pKb.

Forms that appear: order of acidity of OH in alcohols and phenols, α-hydrogen acidity, strongest acid among CH(CN)₃, CHI₃, CHBr₃, CHCl₃, pKb of amines, acidity of carboxylic acids.

Asked in: 2019 (5), 2020 (7), 2021 (4), 2022 (2), 2023 (7), 2024 (7)

Which form was asked:

Acidity orders (pKa, alcohols, carboxylic acids, C–H and O–H acids): 24 questions

Basicity orders and pKb of amines and conjugate bases: 8 questions

Type 6: Aromaticity, resonance and reactivity of conjugated systems – 31 questions

What is asked: Decide whether a species is aromatic, compare resonance structures, or compare reactivity towards substitution.

What to master: Hückel's rule (4n + 2); planarity and conjugation; stability of contributing structures; effect of substituents on electrophilic and nucleophilic substitution.

Forms that appear: number of aromatic compounds among cyclic structures, tropolone, [6] and [8] annulene, stability order of resonance structures, rate of nucleophilic aromatic substitution with OH⁻.

Asked in: 2019 (5), 2021 (8), 2022 (3), 2023 (7), 2024 (6), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Identifying aromatic, non-aromatic and anti-aromatic species: 17 questions

Resonance structures: stability and validity: 7 questions

Reactivity towards electrophilic and nucleophilic substitution: 7 questions

Type 7: Stability of carbocations, carbanions and radicals – 23 questions

What is asked: Arrange reactive intermediates in order of stability or pick the most stable one.

What to master: Hyperconjugation, +I, resonance, adjacent lone pairs; s-character for carbanions; benzyl and allyl systems.

Forms that appear: most stable carbocation among substituted benzyl cations, decreasing hydride affinity, stability of carbanions, most stable radical on homolytic cleavage.

Asked in: 2021 (3), 2022 (5), 2023 (2), 2024 (7), 2025 (6)

Which form was asked:

Stability order of carbocations: 14 questions

Stability of carbanions, free radicals and other intermediates: 9 questions

Type 8: Qualitative analysis: Lassaigne's test – 15 questions

What is asked: Name the element, reagent or colour in the sodium fusion test, or say which compound gives a particular test.

What to master: Formation of NaCN, Na₂S, NaX; Prussian blue, blood red, purple, black; why HNO₃ is added before AgNO₃.

Forms that appear: which nitrogen compound does not give Lassaigne's test, colour with sodium nitroprusside, test for phosphorus, match the element with its reagent.

Asked in: 2021 (4), 2022 (2), 2023 (3), 2024 (4), 2025 (2)

Type 9: Electronic effects: inductive, mesomeric, hyperconjugation and directing groups – 14 questions

What is asked: Identify the electronic effect, compare bond lengths or dipole moments, or classify groups as activating or directing.

What to master: Permanent versus temporary effects; −I, +I, −M, +M; hyperconjugation; ortho/para and meta directing groups.

Forms that appear: how many compounds show inductive, mesomeric and hyperconjugation effects, shortest C–Cl bond, set of meta directing groups, which group shows negative resonance effect.

Asked in: 2020 (4), 2021 (1), 2024 (7), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Inductive, mesomeric and hyperconjugation effects (statements): 8 questions

Directing and activating groups; bond length and dipole effects: 6 questions

Type 10: Nucleophiles and electrophiles (attacking reagents) – 7 questions

What is asked: Identify nucleophiles or electrophiles, compare nucleophilicity, or recognise ambident nucleophiles.

What to master: Lone pair and negative charge; electron-deficient species; ambident nucleophiles (CN⁻, NO₂⁻).

Forms that appear: increasing order of nucleophilicity, total number of nucleophiles in a list, match carbocation, free radical and nucleophile, pair of ambident nucleophiles.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (2), 2025 (2)

5. Suggested study order for students

Start with isomerism and nomenclature (Types 1 and 2): 98 questions (32%). Drawing structures correctly is the skill behind both.

Then purification and quantitative analysis (Types 3 and 4): 87 questions (28%), mostly theory and formula-based marks.

Then the electronic-effects group (Types 5, 6, 7 and 9): 100 questions (33%); one clear order of stability or acidity solves many of them.

6. A routine to follow for every question

Read what is asked: name, structure, order, count or percentage, and note whether the options are pictures.

For an order question compare the species pair by pair using one effect at a time: inductive, resonance, hyperconjugation, s-character.

For counting questions (isomers, σ/π, chiral centres) draw the structure explicitly and mark each case; do not count mentally.

For analysis numericals write the formula for the percentage first, then substitute the mass of the precipitate or the volume at STP.

Eliminate options using one sure fact (for example the more stable carbocation) before checking the rest.
