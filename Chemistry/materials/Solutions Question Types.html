JEE Main Chemistry – Solutions

Types of questions asked in JEE Main 2019–2025 (159 questions)

Key message: these 159 questions fall into only 9 repeating types. The three biggest types (Types 1, 2, 3) account for 85 questions (53%). The colligative-property types (1, 2, 4 and 5) are 94 questions (59%). About 59% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: van't Hoff factor, dissociation and association: colligative properties of electrolytes – 34 questions

What is asked: Use i to find the degree of ionisation or association, the boiling or freezing point, the Ka of a weak acid or a molar mass.

What to master: i = 1 + (n−1)α; i = 1 − α/2 for dimerisation; ΔT = i·K·m; particles counted for each solute.

Forms that appear: MX₃ and MX₂, A₂B and A₂B₃, HA dimerising and dissociating, benzoic acid in water, acetic acid and chloroacetic acid, K₄Fe(CN)₆, MgCl₂ and NaCl in sea water, Pb(NO₃)₂ with NaCl, CrCl₃·xNH₃.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (2), 2021 (10), 2022 (4), 2023 (7), 2024 (3), 2025 (5)

Which form was asked:

Degree of ionisation or association from i, and i from the degree: 17 questions

Weak acids: Ka, degree of dissociation and i from a colligative property: 7 questions

Mixtures, several electrolytes and combined problems (complexes, Ksp, titration): 10 questions

Type 2: Boiling-point elevation and freezing-point depression of non-electrolytes – 27 questions

What is asked: Find a molar mass, a mass, Kb or Kf, the boiling or freezing point, or the ice that separates.

What to master: ΔTb = Kb·m and ΔTf = Kf·m; molality from mass and the kg of solvent; ratios of ΔT.

Forms that appear: urea, glucose and ethylene glycol in water, 2.5 g solute in 75 g water, AB and AB₂ in water, two solvents with Kb in the ratio 1:8, ethylene glycol and ice separated, ΔTf on a graph.

Asked in: 2019 (5), 2021 (5), 2022 (8), 2023 (2), 2024 (3), 2025 (4)

Which form was asked:

ΔTb or ΔTf to find a molar mass or a mass of solute: 10 questions

Ratios and relations between Kb, Kf and ΔT: 9 questions

Two solutes, mixtures of solvents, ice separated and graphs of ΔT: 8 questions

Type 3: Concentration terms and conversions – 24 questions

What is asked: Find molarity, molality, mole fraction, mass percent or ppm of a solution, or convert between them.

What to master: Definitions of M, m, x, w/w % and ppm; density to convert between volume and mass; molar mass of hydrates.

Forms that appear: NaCl in 500 mL, ethyl alcohol in water, sodium sulphate, glucose in blood, urea 4.44 m, H₂SO₄ 31.4%, sea water 6 M, CuSO₄·5H₂O, DCM in chloroform.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (1), 2021 (4), 2022 (1), 2023 (5), 2024 (8), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Molarity, molality, mole fraction and mass % from given data: 14 questions

ppm, hydrates, dilution and gas volumes: 5 questions

Which concentration term changes with temperature, and statements: 5 questions

Type 4: Osmosis and osmotic pressure – 18 questions

What is asked: Find π, a molar mass or a concentration from π, compare isotonic solutions, or decide the direction of osmosis and reverse osmosis.

What to master: π = iCRT; units of R; equal iC for isotonic solutions; the membrane is semipermeable.

Forms that appear: protein in 300 cm³, blood and glucose, 400 Pa for 2.5 g, a biopolymer, PVC graph, NaCl and glucose mixed, artificial cell in NaCl, raw mango, reverse osmosis.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (4), 2021 (1), 2022 (2), 2023 (4), 2024 (3), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Osmotic pressure calculations (π = iCRT) and isotonic solutions: 13 questions

Direction of osmosis, reverse osmosis and the net osmotic pressure: 5 questions

Type 5: Comparing solutions: order of properties, isotonic pairs and statements – 15 questions

What is asked: Arrange solutions by boiling or freezing point, pick the lowest freezing point, count isotonic pairs, or judge statements.

What to master: The number of ions per formula; i × C as the measure of the colligative effect.

Forms that appear: 10⁻⁴ M NaCl and urea in order, 0.06 M Al₂(SO₄)₃, KHSO₄ against glycine, acetic acid in water and in benzene, isotonic pairs of NaCl, CaCl₂, AlCl₃, five pairs with K₄[Fe(CN)₆].

Asked in: 2020 (1), 2021 (3), 2023 (5), 2024 (3), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Order of boiling point, freezing point or vapour pressure for several solutions: 8 questions

Isotonic pairs, statements and match-the-list: 7 questions

Type 6: Ideal solutions: Raoult's law, vapour pressure and vapour composition – 14 questions

What is asked: Find the total vapour pressure, a pure vapour pressure or the vapour composition of an ideal mixture of two volatile liquids.

What to master: pA = xA·pA°; Dalton's law; yA = pA/P; two equations for two unknowns.

Forms that appear: benzene and toluene, A and B at 25 °C, hexane and heptane, P = 350 and 410 mm Hg at two compositions, 1/x against 1/y plot.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (1), 2021 (3), 2022 (2), 2023 (1), 2024 (1), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Total vapour pressure and vapour composition of an ideal mixture: 11 questions

Relations between xA, yA and the plot of 1/x against 1/y: 3 questions

Type 7: Lowering of vapour pressure by a non-volatile solute – 10 questions

What is asked: Find the lowering, the moles or mass of solute or the vapour pressure of the solution.

What to master: Relative lowering equals the mole fraction of the solute; i for an electrolyte.

Forms that appear: urea in 1000 g of water, glucose and 0.20 mm Hg, 30% w/v glucose, MgCl₂ 80% dissociated, SO₂ passed through NaOH, two open beakers in a closed container.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (1), 2022 (1), 2023 (4), 2024 (1), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Relative lowering of vapour pressure (Raoult's law for a non-volatile solute): 8 questions

Lowering of vapour pressure with an electrolyte: 2 questions

Type 8: Henry's law and the solubility of gases – 8 questions

What is asked: Find the amount of gas that dissolves or choose the correct statement, table or KH graph.

What to master: p = KH·x; 55.5 mol of water per kg; KH rises with temperature.

Forms that appear: O₂ at 20 kPa, O₂ at 0.920 bar, CO₂ at 0.835 bar in soda water, a table of KH for four gases, KH of He, N₂, O₂ and CH₄ against temperature.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (2), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Solubility of a gas from Henry's law: p = KH·x: 4 questions

Statements, the table of KH and graphs of KH against temperature: 4 questions

6. Suggested study order for students

Start with the colligative properties of dissolved solutes: ΔTb and ΔTf (Type 2) and the van't Hoff factor (Type 1): 61 questions (38%). One formula, ΔT = i·K·m, covers both.

Then concentration terms (Type 3, 24 questions) and osmotic pressure with comparing solutions (Types 4 and 5): 33 questions (21%).

Then vapour pressure: Raoult's law, lowering, Henry's law and deviations (Types 6, 7, 8, 9): 39 questions (25%).

7. A routine to follow for every question

Write what is given and what is asked, with the units.

Decide the colligative property (or the concentration term) and whether the solute is an electrolyte (find i) or associates.

Convert the data: mass to moles, volume of solvent to mass with the density, mL to L, Pa to bar.

Apply the formula once: ΔT = i·K·m, π = iCRT, (p° − p)/p° = x(solute), p = x·p°.

Check the answer: i between its limits, mole fraction below 1, and the rounding asked (nearest integer).
