How to use these notes

1. Do these pages BEFORE the practice sheet, one type at a time.

2. Close all books and notes. Fill the blanks from memory; leave a gap if you cannot recall.

3. Then press “Show completed notes” under that type, correct your answers in a different colour (or your own notes), and note what you missed.

4. Now do that type's questions on the sheet. Come back to the notes after the sheet to add anything new.

5. Re-fill the same page a week later with the prompts covered – this is what makes it stick.

Typing in the notes boxes
  • Your answers are saved on this device only. Use “Save my notes to a file” to keep a copy or move to another device.
  • Tap a box and use the bar at the bottom of the screen.
  • Subscript or superscript: select the text and press x₂ or xⁿ, or press the button, type, and press it again.
  • Back to normal text: press Aa, or press the right arrow at the end of the subscript or superscript.
  • Fraction: press a/b, type the top, press Tab, type the bottom, press Tab again to continue.

Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• A resistance, a cell constant or a resistivity is given and κ or Λm is asked (answer to the nearest integer).

• The limiting molar conductivities of three electrolytes are given and that of a fourth, or the degree of dissociation of a weak acid, is asked.

• A graph of Λm against c for two electrolytes, or statements on strong and weak electrolytes.

2. Formulae and laws

Conductivity

κ=1R⋅lA=cell constantR

Molar conductivity

Λm=κc=1000κM with κ in Scm−1, M in molL−1

Kohlrausch's law

Λm∘=ν+λ+∘+ν−λ−∘

Strong electrolyte

Λm=Λm∘−Ac

Weak electrolyte

α=ΛmΛm∘; Ka=Cα21−α

Saturated solution of a salt

Λm∘=κS with S in molm−3

3. Concepts and skills

What happens to κ and Λm on dilution?

κ always falls (fewer ions per volume); Λm always rises.

Why is Λm∘ of a weak electrolyte found by Kohlrausch's law?

Its Λm rises too steeply near zero concentration for extrapolation.

Which ions conduct best?

H+ and OH− (Grotthuss mechanism).

Which factor does NOT affect conductance?

The nature of the electrode; the electrolyte, its concentration, the solvent and the temperature do.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Mixing cm and m: κ in Sm−1 needs c in molm−3.

• Forgetting the factor 1000 when M is in molL−1.

• Taking Λm to fall on dilution.

• Using Λm∘ of the salt, not the ions, in α=Λm/Λm∘.

Completed notes – Type 2

1. How do I spot this type?

• A cell with concentrations given and the cell potential, a concentration or n is asked.

• A half-cell at a given pH or gas pressure (for example MnO4−/Mn2+ or H+/H2) and its potential is asked.

• A cell whose second concept is hidden (hydrolysis, Ksp, photoelectric effect) and E comes first.

2. Formulae and laws

Nernst equation at 298 K

E=E∘−0.059nlogQ

Reaction quotient

Q=[products][reactants] (solids and pure liquids omitted; gases as bar)

Hydrogen electrode

E=−0.059pH−0.0592logpH2

MnO4−+8H++5e−

E=E∘−0.0595log[Mn2+][MnO4−][H+]8

Concentration cell

E=0.059nlogccathodecanode

Hydroxide

[M2+]=Ksp[OH−]2

3. Concepts and skills

How is n found?

Balance the cell reaction; n is the number of electrons that cancel.

Why does E=0 at equilibrium?

Q=K, so E∘=0.059nlogK and E=0.

Does the potential depend on the stoichiometric coefficients?

E∘ does not; E does through Q and n.

Does a concentration cell have E∘?

E∘=0; the potential comes only from the concentration ratio.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Putting Q upside down (reactants over products).

• Using 0.059 without dividing by n.

• Forgetting that [H+]8 or [H+]14 enters the logarithm.

• Including the concentration of a solid electrode.

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• Two half-cell potentials are given and ΔG∘, spontaneity or the best cathode is asked.

• 'K for the reaction is 10x; find x' or 'E∘ if K=1020'.

• A ladder such as FeO42−→Fe3+→Fe2+→Fe and the potential between two non-adjacent species.

2. Formulae and laws

Cell potential

Ecell∘=Ecathode∘−Eanode∘

Gibbs energy

ΔG∘=−nFE∘; ΔG∘=−RTlnK

Equilibrium constant

logK=nE∘0.059

Combining half-reactions

E∘=n1E1∘+n2E2∘n1+n2

Temperature coefficient

ΔS=nFdEdT; ΔH=−nFE+nFTdEdT

3. Concepts and skills

Why are potentials not added directly?

E is intensive; ΔG (which carries n) is additive.

How do you pick the cathode with the largest ΔG change per electron?

Compare Ecell∘/n if the question says 'per electron'.

What sign does Ecell∘ have for a spontaneous reaction?

Positive; then ΔG∘<0 and K>1.

Is E∘ changed by multiplying the equation?

No; ΔG∘ and n change together.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Subtracting the wrong way (Eanode−Ecathode).

• Adding potentials of half-reactions with different n.

• Using F=96500 in kJ without converting.

• Forgetting that n is the electrons in the balanced reaction.

Completed notes – Type 4

1. How do I spot this type?

• A current and a time are given and the mass of a metal or the volume of a gas is asked.

• 'How many faradays are needed to reduce one mole of Cr2O72− (or obtain one mole of Fe from Fe3O4)?'

• Only part of the current is used (efficiency) and the time or the current is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Faraday's first law

m=Mn⋅It96500

Charge

Q=It; 1F=96500C=1 mol of electrons

Efficiency

t=QneededI×(efficiency)

Gases from water

anode O2: 4 F per mole; cathode H2: 2 F per mole

Gas volume at STP

V=moles×22.7L (1 bar) or 22.4 L (1 atm)

3. Concepts and skills

How many electrons per mole of product?

The change in oxidation number: Cr2O72−→2Cr3+ is 6; Fe3O4→Fe is 8/3 per Fe.

Same charge through two cells in series?

Equivalents of every substance liberated are equal.

How is the pH of the solution related?

Moles of OH− formed = moles of electrons passed.

What is one coulomb worth?

One electrochemical equivalent of the substance.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the wrong n (for Cr3+ from dichromate, 6 per Cr2O72−, not per Cr).

• Minutes not converted to seconds.

• Forgetting the efficiency factor.

• Using molar mass of the salt instead of the metal.

2. Formulae and laws

Zn→Zn2++2e−

Mercury cell

Zn(Hg)+HgO→ZnO+Hg

Fuel cell

2H2+O2→2H2O, about 70% efficient

Rusting

anode Fe→Fe2++2e−; cathode O2+4H++4e−→2H2O

3. Concepts and skills

Which cell is used in hearing aids and watches?

The mercury cell (steady 1.35 V, no ion in the electrolyte changes).

Which plate is the anode of the lead battery?

The lead plate on discharge; the roles reverse on charging.

Does tin protect iron when scratched?

No; iron is more active than tin and rusts faster.

What type of electrode is the calomel electrode?

Metal – insoluble salt – anion.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Calling a fuel cell a storage cell.

• Mixing the discharge and charging reactions of the lead battery.

• Thinking rusting is faster in alkaline medium.

• Treating tin plating as sacrificial protection.

Completed notes – Type 6

1. How do I spot this type?

• Ions or couples with E∘ values are given and an order, the strongest agent or the species that liberates H2 is asked.

• 'The number of metals oxidised by Cr2O72− (or NO3−)' with a list of couples.

• Which cell notation matches a given reaction.

2. Formulae and laws

Oxidising power

larger E∘ (reduction) ⇒ stronger oxidising agent

Reducing power

smaller E∘ ⇒ stronger reducing agent

Oxidation of a metal M by an oxidant

possible if Eoxidant∘>EMn+/M∘

Liberating H2 from acid

needs E∘(Mn+/M)<0

3. Concepts and skills

Which side of a cell is the anode?

The left; electrons flow from the anode to the cathode.

Is Ecell intensive?

Yes; it does not depend on the amount of substance.

What does a negative E∘ for M+/M tell you?

The metal is a stronger reducing agent than H2.

Does E∘(M+/M) depend on the sublimation enthalpy?

Yes (with ionisation of the gaseous atom and hydration of the ion).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Reading the order of reducing power as the order of E∘.

• Including couples with E∘ above the oxidant's.

• Mixing the anode and cathode in the cell notation.

Completed notes – Type 7

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'Which product is not obtained in the electrolysis of brine?' or statements on brine.

• A mixture of ions is electrolysed and the order of deposition is asked.

• A galvanic cell is connected to an external source: what happens at each electrode?

2. Formulae and laws

Brine

cathode H2 and OH−; anode Cl2; product NaOH

Cathode order

the couple with the larger E∘ is reduced first

External voltage on a Daniell cell

if Eext>1.1 V the cell reverses; if Eext<1.1 V it works as a galvanic cell

3. Concepts and skills

Why is Mg not deposited from water?

E∘(Mg2+/Mg) is so low that water (H2) is reduced instead.

What is formed at a Pt anode in dilute H2SO4?

O2; in concentrated H2SO4 the persulphate forms.

Active and inert electrodes?

An active anode (Ag, Cu) dissolves; an inert one (Pt) lets water or the anion discharge.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Putting Cl2 at the cathode in brine.

• Expecting a reactive metal to deposit from water.

• Ignoring the electrode material (silver anode dissolves).
