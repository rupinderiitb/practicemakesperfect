Types of questions asked in JEE Main 2019–2025 (268 questions)

Key message: these 268 questions fall into only 9 repeating types. The three biggest types (Types 1, 2, 3) account for 145 questions (54%). Magnetic moment and crystal field theory (Types 1 and 2) are 105 questions (39%). About 24% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Spin-only magnetic moment and unpaired electrons in complexes – 62 questions

What is asked: Find μ, the number of unpaired electrons, or the order of magnetic moments of given complexes; identify paramagnetic and diamagnetic species.

What to master: μ = √[n(n+2)] BM; d configuration of the metal; strong-field ligand → low spin; Δ₀ < P → high spin.

Forms that appear: [Co(CN)₆]³⁻, [MnBr₆]⁴⁻, [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻, [FeF₆]³⁻, [CoF₆]³⁻, Fe(CO)₄, orders of μ, diamagnetic octahedral complexes.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (6), 2021 (12), 2022 (10), 2023 (13), 2024 (8), 2025 (10)

Which form was asked:

Spin-only magnetic moment of a given complex or ion: 19 questions

Order or match complexes by magnetic moment or unpaired electrons: 20 questions

Paramagnetic or diamagnetic complexes, count of unpaired electrons: 23 questions

Type 2: Crystal field theory: t₂g/e_g configuration, CFSE and high or low spin – 43 questions

What is asked: Write the t₂g/e_g configuration, compute the CFSE, or judge a statement on crystal field theory, Jahn–Teller distortion and high or low spin.

What to master: CFSE = (−0.4 x + 0.6 y)Δ₀ + pairing; Δₜ = 4/9 Δ₀; Δ₀ < P high spin; Δ₀ > P low spin.

Forms that appear: Configuration of [Co(en)₃]³⁺, [CoF₆]³⁻, [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻, CFSE of d⁶ ions, order of CFSE of Co complexes, Mn(II) and Ni(II) complexes, Jahn–Teller distortion.

Asked in: 2019 (7), 2020 (8), 2021 (2), 2022 (4), 2023 (7), 2024 (5), 2025 (10)

Which form was asked:

d-electron configuration t₂g/e_g of a complex or ion: 20 questions

CFSE, crystal field stabilisation energy: 15 questions

Crystal field theory: statements, Jahn–Teller, high and low spin: 8 questions

Type 3: Ligands, denticity, oxidation state, coordination number and Werner's theory – 40 questions

What is asked: Count ambidentate ligands, give the denticity, the oxidation state or the coordination number, or use AgNO₃ precipitation to find the formula.

What to master: Oxidation state = charge − ligand charges; coordination number = donor atoms; ions outside the bracket precipitate with AgNO₃.

Forms that appear: Ambidentate NO₂⁻, SCN⁻, denticity of EDTA, biuret, Fehling's ligand, oxidation state of Fe in nitroprusside, [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]Cl₂, double salts, CoCl₃·xNH₃ with AgNO₃.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (3), 2021 (9), 2022 (4), 2023 (11), 2024 (4), 2025 (5)

Which form was asked:

Ligands: type, denticity, ambidentate and homoleptic complexes: 12 questions

Oxidation state, coordination number and effective atomic number: 9 questions

Werner's theory: primary and secondary valency, precipitation with AgNO₃, conductivity: 16 questions

Stability of complexes and stability constants: 3 questions

Type 4: Isomerism in coordination compounds: geometrical, optical and total isomers – 35 questions

What is asked: Count the geometrical, optical or total isomers of a complex, or choose the complex that shows fac–mer, cis–trans or optical isomerism.

What to master: [MA₂B₂] and [MA₄B₂]: cis–trans; [MA₃B₃]: fac–mer; cis-[M(AA)₂B₂] and [M(AA)₃] are chiral.

Forms that appear: fac–mer in [Co(NH₃)₃Cl₃], isomers of [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺, [Pt(en)(NO₂)₂], [Cr(ox)₃]³⁻, [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺, [Pd(F)(Cl)(Br)(I)]²⁻, chiral complexes.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (9), 2021 (7), 2022 (2), 2023 (6), 2024 (3), 2025 (4)

Which form was asked:

Geometrical isomerism: cis–trans and fac–mer: 18 questions

Optical isomerism and number of stereoisomers: 11 questions

Total isomers: structural plus stereoisomers: 6 questions

Type 5: Valence bond theory: hybridisation, magnetic nature and shape – 27 questions

What is asked: Give the hybridisation, shape and magnetic behaviour of a complex or ion; match complexes with hybridisation.

What to master: Strong-field ligand: d²sp³ (octahedral), dsp² (square planar), low spin; weak-field: sp³d², sp³, high spin.

Forms that appear: [Mn(CN)₆]³⁻, [MnCl₆]³⁻, [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻, [NiCl₄]²⁻, [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻, [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺, [CoF₆]³⁻, PF₅, SF₆, Ni(CO)₄, shape of [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (4), 2022 (3), 2023 (2), 2024 (8), 2025 (7)

Which form was asked:

Hybridisation of the central metal (valence bond theory): 12 questions

Hybridisation together with magnetic nature of a complex: 11 questions

Shape and geometry of complexes and ions: 4 questions

Type 6: Spectrochemical series, crystal field splitting and colour of complexes – 22 questions

What is asked: Order ligands or complexes by field strength or Δ₀, or relate colour, absorbed wavelength and Δ₀.

What to master: I⁻ < Br⁻ < Cl⁻ < F⁻ < OH⁻ < H₂O < NH₃ < en < CN⁻ < CO; larger Δ₀ means absorption at shorter wavelength.

Forms that appear: Order of field strength of ligands, highest Δ₀ among [Co(CN)₆]³⁻, [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ and similar, [Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺ absorption at 498 nm, [Cu(H₂O)₄]²⁺ and en, colour of Co(III) complexes.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (3), 2023 (5), 2024 (4), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Spectrochemical series: order of ligand field strength and Δ₀: 9 questions

Colour and absorption: wavelength, energy of light absorbed: 13 questions

Type 7: Applications and tests: biological and medicinal complexes, DMG, Prussian blue – 19 questions

What is asked: Match a metal with its biological role or catalyst, or identify the complex by its colour or test.

What to master: Chlorophyll Mg; vitamin B₁₂ Co; haemoglobin Fe; carbonic anhydrase Zn; Wilkinson catalyst Rh; cisplatin Pt; Ni–DMG red ppt.

Forms that appear: Anti-tumour compounds, EDTA in lead poisoning, Wilkinson catalyst, Ni–dmg, Prussian blue, [Fe(SCN)]²⁺, colour of Co²⁺ complexes, AgCl in NH₃.

Asked in: 2019 (5), 2021 (3), 2022 (2), 2023 (5), 2024 (3), 2025 (1)

Type 8: Metal carbonyls and organometallic compounds – 11 questions

What is asked: Count the bridging CO, the metal–metal bonds or the geometry in a metal carbonyl, or explain synergic bonding.

What to master: M←CO σ bond and M→CO π back bonding; Co₂(CO)₈ has 2 bridging CO; Mn₂(CO)₁₀ has none.

Forms that appear: Bridging CO in Co₂(CO)₈, Mn₂(CO)₁₀, W(CO)₆, Fe₂(CO)₉; geometry around Mn; synergic bonding; low oxidation states of metals.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (1), 2022 (3), 2023 (2), 2024 (2)

Type 9: IUPAC naming of coordination compounds – 5 questions

Forms that appear: [PtBr₂(PMe₃)₂], K₂MnO₄, K₃[Co(C₂O₄)₃], [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl(NH₂CH₃)]Cl.

5. Suggested study order for students

Start with the magnetic moment (Type 1): 62 questions (23%) and crystal field theory (Type 2): 43 questions (16%). Learn μ = √[n(n+2)] and the t₂g/e_g filling for d¹ to d¹⁰.

Then ligands and Werner theory (Type 3) and isomerism (Type 4): 75 questions (28%). Practise the isomer counts for [MA₂B₂], [MA₄B₂], [MA₃B₃] and [M(AA)₃].

Then hybridisation and the spectrochemical series (Types 5 and 6): 49 questions (18%), and applications, carbonyls and naming (Types 7, 8, 9): 35 questions (13%).

6. A routine to follow for every question

Write the oxidation state of the metal and its d configuration (remove the 4s electrons first).

Decide strong or weak field from the ligand (CN⁻, CO, en, NO₂⁻ strong; F⁻, Cl⁻, H₂O weak) and fill the t₂g and e_g orbitals.

Count the unpaired electrons and give μ = √[n(n+2)] BM or the CFSE.

For hybridisation, shape and isomers, draw the geometry (octahedral, tetrahedral, square planar) with the ligands.

Check the answer: Ni²⁺ with CN⁻ is square planar, and Ni²⁺ with Cl⁻ is tetrahedral.
