Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• Two rate constants at two temperatures (or a rise of 10 K that doubles k) and Ea or a new k is asked.

• An equation such as lnk=a−b/T, a plot of lnk against 1/T, or statements on the Arrhenius equation are given.

• A catalyst, a reversible step, a three-step energy diagram or a mechanism with a slow step is given, and Ea, ΔH, the number of intermediates or the order is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Arrhenius equation

k=Ae−Ea/RT, so lnk=lnA−EaRT

Two temperatures

logk2k1=Ea2.303R(1T1−1T2)

Forward and backward

ΔH=Ea,f−Ea,b; a catalyst lowers both by the same amount and leaves ΔH unchanged

Composite constants

k=k1k2k3⇒Ea=Ea1+Ea2−Ea3; k=k1k3k2⇒Ea=1/2(Ea1+Ea3−Ea2)

3. Concepts and skills

What do the slope and intercept of lnk against 1/T give?

Slope =−Ea/R and intercept =lnA; with logk the slope is −Ea/2.303R.

Why does a catalyst speed up a reaction?

It gives a path of lower Ea for both directions; ΔH, ΔG and K are unchanged.

How do you pick the rate-determining step in an energy diagram?

It is the step with the highest peak; peaks are activated complexes and valleys are intermediates.

How does the rate law follow from a mechanism?

Use the slow step and replace any intermediate with the reactants through the fast equilibrium before it.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using Ea in kJ with R=8.314 J K​−1 mol​−1 without converting.

• Forgetting to change °C to K.

• Saying that a catalyst changes ΔH or the equilibrium constant.

• Counting the first and the last valley (reactant and product) as intermediates.

Completed notes – Type 2

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'Time for 99.9% (or 90%) completion is x times the half life', or 'time to go from 50% to 75%'.

• A rate constant, a half life or the amount left after a time is given for a reaction of the form A→ products.

• A radioactive isotope, a drug that loses potency, or the ​14C ratio in wood is given.

2. Formulae and laws

Integrated law

k=2.303tlog[A]0[A]

Half life

t1/2=0.693k, independent of [A]0

Fraction left

[A][A]0=(1/2)t/t1/2

Completion times

t75%=2t1/2; t87.5%=3t1/2; t90%=3.32t1/2; t99.9%≈10t1/2

3. Concepts and skills

How do you handle A→2B or A→B+C?

Work with the amount of A left; only A appears in the first order law, so the coefficient of the product does not matter.

What is the rate at a given time?

rate=k[A] at that time; two rates at two times give k from their ratio.

Why is radioactive decay first order?

The decay rate is proportional to the number of nuclei present, N=N0e−kt.

How do you find the time to go from 50% to 87.5%?

Count half lives: 50%→25%→12.5% is two half lives.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Putting the amount reacted instead of the amount left inside the logarithm.

• Mixing ln and log: k=1tln[A]0[A] or 2.303tlog[A]0[A].

• Using percentage completed where percentage left is needed (90% completion leaves 10%).

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• A table of experiments with concentrations and initial rates is given; the order, k or a missing entry is asked.

• 'If the concentration of A is doubled and B is halved, the rate becomes...'.

• The volume of an elementary reaction mixture is reduced to a fraction of its value.

2. Formulae and laws

Order from two rows

r2r1=(c2c1)n with only one concentration changing

Overall order

n=m+n′ for rate =k[A]m[B]n′

Volume reduced to 1/x

every concentration becomes x times; rate becomes xn times

Elementary reaction

order equals molecularity; rate =k[A]a[B]b with the coefficients as powers

3. Concepts and skills

Which two rows do you compare?

Two rows in which only one concentration changes; if none exist, use three rows and solve for the orders together.

What does a zero order in B mean?

The rate does not change when [B] changes.

Can the order be a fraction or zero?

Yes, for complex reactions; the molecularity of an elementary step is a whole number.

How is k found after the order?

Substitute any one row into the rate law and solve for k; the units follow the order.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Comparing rows in which two concentrations change together.

• Using the coefficients as the order for a reaction that is not elementary.

• Giving the order of one reactant when the overall order is asked.

Completed notes – Type 4

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'Which graph represents a zero order reaction?' or a graph of rate against concentration is shown.

• A population grows as N0et and an antibiotic then removes it at a rate proportional to N2.

• Several lines of logk against 1/T are drawn and the order of the activation energies is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Zero order

[A]=[A]0−kt: a straight line of slope −k; rate is constant

First order

ln[A]=ln[A]0−kt: a straight line of slope −k; rate against [A] is a line through the origin

Arrhenius plot

logk against 1/T: slope =−Ea2.303R, so a steeper line means larger Ea

k against T

k rises exponentially with T for any reaction obeying the Arrhenius equation

3. Concepts and skills

How do you tell the order from a rate-concentration graph?

Horizontal line: zero order; straight line through the origin: first order; parabola: second order.

Why is the logk against 1/T line falling?

Higher T (smaller 1/T) gives larger k, so the slope is negative.

How is growth related to decay?

Both are first order: N=N0e±kt; growth has a positive exponent.

What does the half life against [A]0 plot show?

A horizontal line means first order; a rising line through the origin means zero order.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking the slope of lnk against 1/T as +Ea/R.

• Reading a falling rate-time curve as zero order.

• Ignoring the unit of the axis (logk or lnk) when finding Ea.

Completed notes – Type 5

1. How do I spot this type?

• A reaction is stated to be of zero order and a half life, time or concentration is asked.

• Half lives are given at two initial concentrations or pressures and the order is asked.

• The unit of k for order n, or the order from the unit of k, is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Zero order law

[A]=[A]0−kt; t1/2=[A]02k

Half life and order

t1/2∝[A]01−n, so t1t2=(a2a1)n−1

Unit of k

(mol L−1)1−ntime−1

Common units

n=0: mol L​−1 s​−1; n=1: s​−1; n=2: L mol​−1 s​−1

3. Concepts and skills

What happens to successive half lives of a zero order reaction?

Each is half of the previous, since t1/2∝[A]0.

How do you get the order from t1/2 at two pressures?

Write t1/t2=(p2/p1)n−1 and solve for n.

What is the time for complete reaction of zero order?

t=[A]0/k, twice the half life.

What does a t1/2 that halves when [A]0 doubles show?

Second order, n=2.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the first order half life formula 0.693/k for a zero order reaction.

• Taking the order from the stoichiometry instead of from the data.

• Writing the unit of k for the wrong order.

Completed notes – Type 6

1. How do I spot this type?

• Two substances decay by first order with different half lives and the time at which their amounts have a given ratio is asked.

• For A→B→C, 'the net rate of formation of B is zero' and [B] is asked.

• Two reversible reactions are written and the relation between their equilibrium constants is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Each decay

[X]=[X]0(1/2)t/t1/2 with its own half life

Ratio condition

Set [A][B]=[A]0[B]02t/tB−t/tA equal to the ratio asked

Steady state

d[B]dt=k1[A]−k2[B]=0⇒[B]=k1k2[A]

Reversible step

K=kfkb; reversing and multiplying a reaction by 3 gives K2=K1−3

3. Concepts and skills

When are the concentrations of A and B equal for equal starting amounts?

When t/tA=t/tB in the exponent, which needs the same number of half lives or a ratio condition solved for t.

Why can the net rate of B be zero?

The intermediate is used up as fast as it forms, so its concentration stays small and constant.

How do the two-reaction K values combine?

Reverse the reaction: K→1/K; multiply by n: K→Kn.

Do parallel decays affect each other?

No; each follows its own first order law.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Adding the half lives of independent decays.

• Writing the ratio of the two amounts the wrong way round.

• Forgetting that doubling the coefficients cubes or squares K.

Completed notes – Type 7

1. How do I spot this type?

• A balanced equation and the rate of change of one species are given; the rate of another species or of the reaction is asked.

• A reactant concentration falls by a given amount in a given time and the average rate is asked.

• The ratio of two rates is given as a logarithm and the coefficients are asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Rate of reaction

r=−1ad[A]dt=−1bd[B]dt=1cd[C]dt

Average rate

Δ[A]Δt over the interval, divided by the coefficient

Ratio of rates

(−d[A]/dt)(−d[B]/dt)=ab

Moles

dnAdt follows the same ratios as d[A]dt in a fixed volume

3. Concepts and skills

Why divide by the coefficient?

So that the rate of reaction is the same whichever species is followed.

What does log(−d[A]/dt)(−d[B]/dt)=0.301 mean?

The ratio is 2, so a:b=2:1.

Does the sign matter?

Reactants have a negative rate of change and products a positive one; the rate of reaction is always positive.

What if the equation has fractional coefficients?

Use them as written: 2A+3B+3/2C gives −1/2d[A]dt and 2/3d[C]dt.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the coefficient as a multiplier instead of a divisor.

• Giving the rate of formation of a product as the rate of reaction without dividing by its coefficient.

• Dropping the factor of 2 in 2N2O5→4NO2+O2.

Completed notes – Type 8

1. How do I spot this type?

• A gas A decomposes to gaseous products and the total pressure is given at a time and at infinity.

• The options give k in terms of Pi, Pt or P∞.

• A table of time against total pressure at constant volume is given and k is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

A→B+C

PA=2Pi−Pt and P∞=2Pi; k=1tlnPi2Pi−Pt=1tlnP∞2(P∞−Pt)

A→2B+C

PA=3Pi−Pt2 and P∞=3Pi

2N2O5→2N2O4+O2

Pt=3Pi−PA2 and P∞=3/2Pi

First order law

k=2.303tlogPiPA

3. Concepts and skills

Why can total pressure be used?

At constant volume and temperature, pressure is proportional to moles of gas.

How do you find PA at time t?

Let x be the pressure of A that has reacted; write Pt in terms of Pi and x, then PA=Pi−x.

What does P∞ represent?

The total pressure when A has completely decomposed.

What is Pt after one half life for A→B+C?

Half of A has gone, so Pt=Pi+0.5Pi=1.5Pi.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using Pt in place of PA in the first order law.

• Using the wrong stoichiometric factor (1 for A→B+C, 2 for A→2B+C).

• Forgetting that P∞ is 2Pi or 3Pi, not Pi.
