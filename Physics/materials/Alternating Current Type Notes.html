1. How do I spot this type?

• 'An inductor of 0.5 mH, a capacitor of 200μF and a resistor are in series: the frequency at which the current is in phase with the emf.'

• 'A series LCR circuit with R=5Ω, L=20 mH, C=0.5μF on a 250 V supply: the power dissipated at resonance.'

• 'The value of R is doubled: the current amplitude at resonance.'

• 'R=100Ω, L=1 H, C=6.25μF: the quality factor.'

2. Formulae and laws

Resonant frequency

ω0=1LC, f0=12πLC

At resonance

XL=XC, Z=R, I=VR, P=V2R

Quality factor

Q=ω0LR=1RLC

Bandwidth

Δω=RL, Q=ω0Δω

Maximum current of an LC circuit

Imax=VC/L

3. Concepts and skills

Does the resonant frequency depend on R?

No. Only L and C set it; R sets the height and width of the peak.

What is the phase at resonance?

Zero: the current is in phase with the emf and the power factor is 1.

What happens to the bandwidth when R increases?

It increases and the quality factor falls: the resonance is less sharp.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Forgetting to convert μF, nF and mH to farads and henries.

• Using f in 1/LC (that gives ω).

• Using the peak voltage in P=V2/R instead of the rms value.

• Thinking the resonant frequency changes when R is changed.

2. Formulae and laws

Reactances

XL=ωL, XC=1ωC, ω=2πf

Impedance

Z=R2+(XL−XC)2

Current

I=VZ (rms with rms, peak with peak)

Phase angle

tanϕ=XL−XCR

Very high frequency

capacitor → short circuit, inductor → open circuit

3. Concepts and skills

Does the current lead or lag?

It lags the voltage when XL>XC and leads it when XC>XL.

How do I get the voltage across one element?

Multiply the current by its resistance or reactance; the element voltages add as phasors, not as numbers.

How do I treat two parallel branches?

Find the current and phase of each branch from its own impedance, then add them as phasors.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Adding R, XL and XC directly instead of using the square root.

• Using the peak voltage with the formula for the rms current.

• Taking ω=f.

• Giving the wrong sign of the phase when XC>XL.

2. Formulae and laws

Phase of the current

R: 0; L: lags by π2; C: leads by π2

Series circuit

Z≥R always, Imax=V/R at resonance

Reactance against frequency

XL∝f (straight line), XC∝1f (hyperbola)

Power of a pure L or C

P=0 since cosϕ=0

3. Concepts and skills

Which side of the resonance peak is capacitive?

Below ω0, where XC>XL.

Does a pure inductor or capacitor consume power?

No; it stores and returns the energy, the average power is zero.

Can an LC circuit alone have zero reactance?

Yes, at resonance the reactances cancel.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Reversing the lead and lag of the capacitor and inductor.

• Saying that a circuit with a capacitor or an inductor alone can be at resonance.

• Taking the average power of a pure L or C as non-zero.

• Mixing up the curves of XL and XC.

Sine plus cosine

3. Concepts and skills

What does an AC ammeter read?

The rms value, which gives the same heating as a direct current.

Why is the mean of the square used?

The mean of the current is zero over a cycle, but the heat depends on i2.

How do I find the time between two values?

Find the phase ωt for each value and divide the difference by ω.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Dividing by 2 the constant (DC) part of the current.

• Using ω=f in the time.

• Taking the average of i instead of the rms.

• Using the peak current for the heat in a resistor.

3. Concepts and skills

What is wattless current?

The current in a pure inductor or capacitor, 90∘ out of phase with the voltage, with zero average power.

What is the power factor at resonance?

1, since Z=R.

What changes when the frequency changes?

XL and XC change, so Z and cosϕ must be recomputed.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the peak values in VrmsIrmscosϕ.

• Taking the phase of the current as the phase of the voltage.

• Forgetting 1/2 when the amplitudes are given.

• Adding the reactances of L and C instead of subtracting.

3. Concepts and skills

What is the phase relation in an inductor?

The current lags the voltage by π/2, so the voltage leads the current.

What happens when the frequency is halved?

XL halves (the current doubles); XC doubles.

What is the displacement current in a capacitor?

The same as the current in the wires, flowing between the plates.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Writing the voltage equation with −90∘ instead of +90∘ for an inductor.

• Forgetting the unit prefixes of pF, μF and mH.

• Using ω=f.

• Using the peak voltage for an rms current.

3. Concepts and skills

How does an inductor behave just after closing?

As an open circuit: no current flows through it at t=0.

And a long time later?

As a plain wire (zero resistance), so it is replaced by a short circuit.

What does a diode do in a discharging circuit?

It blocks the discharge when it is reverse biased and the charge stays.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Leaving the inductor in the circuit in the steady state of a DC circuit.

• Mixing up the growth and decay formulas.

• Taking the energy fraction as the current fraction (U∝i2).

• Forgetting to redraw the circuit for t=0 and t=∞.

Ideal transformer

Load resistance

R=V2P on each side

Step up / step down

3. Concepts and skills

Is the power the same on both sides?

For an ideal transformer yes; otherwise Pout=ηPin.

Which side has the larger current?

The side with fewer turns (the low-voltage side).

Does a transformer work on DC?

No; it needs a changing flux.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Inverting the turns ratio.

• Using the load resistance of one side for the other.

• Forgetting the efficiency when the transformer is not ideal.

• Using the peak voltage with the rms current.
