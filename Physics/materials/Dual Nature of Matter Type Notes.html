Electron accelerated through V volts

3. Concepts and skills

Why is the wavelength of a heavier particle shorter at the same energy?

At fixed K the momentum p=2mK grows with the mass, and λ=h/p falls.

What does 'same de Broglie wavelength' tell you about momentum?

The momenta are equal, whatever the masses. The lighter particle then has the larger speed and the larger kinetic energy.

Does the charge matter when the kinetic energies are equal?

No: with the same K only the mass enters. With the same accelerating potential the charge enters through qV.

How are the wavelengths ordered for the same energy?

λe>λp>λd>λα, since me<mp<md<mα.

What is the wavelength of a neutron compared with a proton at the same energy?

Equal, because their masses are practically equal.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using λ∝1/m instead of 1/m for the same energy.

• Forgetting the charge (qα=2e) when the particles are accelerated through a potential.

• Taking the ratio of K as m1/m2 for the same wavelength; it is m2/m1.

• Using the electron mass for the proton or the number 1836 for the α-particle.

Einstein's photoelectric equation

Kmax=hν−ϕ=hcλ−ϕ=eV0

Two cases: eliminate ϕ

e(V01−V02)=hc(1λ1−1λ2)

Threshold wavelength

λ0=hcϕ; emission needs λ≤λ0

Maximum speed

v=2(E−ϕ)m, so v∝E−ϕ

3. Concepts and skills

Why can the work function be found from two readings?

Each reading gives K=hν−ϕ; subtracting removes ϕ and leaves h (or a combination of the given quantities).

If the frequency falls below the threshold and the intensity is doubled, how many electrons are emitted?

None. Intensity cannot make up for a photon energy below ϕ.

Does doubling the frequency double the kinetic energy?

No: K=hν−ϕ, so K more than doubles (the constant ϕ is subtracted once).

How are the speeds of photoelectrons compared?

Through E−ϕ, not through E itself.

What if the photon energy is exactly ϕ?

The electron just escapes with zero kinetic energy.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Comparing E instead of E−ϕ when finding the ratio of speeds.

• Writing the speed ratio as the ratio of kinetic energies, without the square root.

• Using λ2=3λ1 for λ1=3λ2, which reverses the answer.

• Forgetting that a stopping potential of V0 means Kmax=eV0 (not V0 in joules).

3. Concepts and skills

How is the stopping potential related to the kinetic energy?

eV0=Kmax: the retarding field removes all the kinetic energy of the fastest electron.

Why does a 10 W infra-red lamp and a 75 W infra-red lamp both fail below the threshold?

Photon energy depends on the frequency, not on the power; infra-red photons have energy below ϕ.

A light wave is given by sinωt. What is its photon energy?

ℏω with ω the coefficient of t (rad/s); using hω is a common error.

If two frequencies are present in the wave, which one decides Kmax?

The larger frequency.

What happens when λ>λ0?

No photoelectrons are emitted.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using hω instead of ℏω=hω/2π for angular frequency.

• Forgetting to convert Å to nm (or eV to J) before substituting.

• Taking the energy of the electron captured in a transition as its kinetic energy only; add the binding energy.

• Reading the wavelength straight from the phase of a wave: k=2π/λ.

3. Concepts and skills

Why does the wave theory fail for the photoelectric effect?

It predicts that Kmax grows with intensity, no threshold frequency and a time lag; none is observed.

Does increasing the intensity increase the stopping potential?

No. It increases the number of photons and so the saturation current.

Does a negative potential on the emitter suppress emission?

A sufficiently negative potential on the collector stops the current; the stopping potential varies linearly with frequency.

Does the number of photons rise with frequency at fixed power?

No, it falls: each photon carries more energy.

What is the minimum frequency for emission?

ν0=ϕ/h, independent of intensity.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Assuming a brighter light raises the stopping potential.

• Assuming a minimum intensity (not frequency) is needed.

• Reading 'not true' statements as 'true'.

• Confusing the number of photons with their energy.

How does λ change if the energy is quadrupled?

It halves.

How does λ change with temperature?

λ∝T−1/2: doubling T divides λ by 2.

How many wavelengths fit in the orbit of the n-th Bohr level?

Exactly n: 2πrn=nλ.

What is the first step for a microscope question?

Set λ equal to the resolution, then find K=h2/2mλ2.

Why does a lighter gas particle have a larger position uncertainty here?

Its wavelength λ∝1/m is longer.

• Taking n=2 for the second excited state (it is n=3).

• Using 1/2kT rather than 3/2kT (for λ in three dimensions).

• Forgetting that a 75% wavelength needs K′=169K (extra 79K).

3. Concepts and skills

Why does a magnetic field not change the wavelength?

The force is perpendicular to the velocity, so the speed and momentum magnitude stay constant.

Why are the wavelengths of the two fragments of a nucleus at rest equal?

Their momenta are equal and opposite, and λ=h/|p|.

What changes in an electric field?

The speed along the field, hence p and λ.

Is momentum conserved in an inelastic collision?

Yes, always; kinetic energy is not.

What direction matters when adding de Broglie momenta?

Momentum is a vector; use the direction of motion.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the kinetic-energy method instead of vector momentum conservation.

• Forgetting the sign when particles move in opposite directions.

• Adding the two speeds in an electric field perpendicular to the velocity.

• Treating the velocity as v0 when it is 2v0 for v0(î+ĵ).

3. Concepts and skills

Is the kinetic energy of a photon 1/2mv2?

No. A photon's energy is pc=hν.

If an electron and a photon have the same wavelength, which has more energy?

The photon, because Eph=pc and Ke=pv/2 with v<c.

If they have the same energy, which has the longer wavelength?

The photon (for energies of a few eV).

Why is E2=p2c2+m2c4 consistent with the photon?

For m=0 it gives E=pc.

Does the medium matter?

Use the speed and wavelength in the medium given in the question.

• Using p2/2m for the photon.

• Using E=hν with λe for the electron.

• Dropping c in the ratio λe/λph.

• Picking E2=pc2+… with the wrong powers.

Two metals give two parallel lines. What differs?

The intercepts (work functions), not the slope.

Which metal gives the larger Kmax at the same frequency?

No.

How do you read ϕ from a V0–ν plot?

Multiply the ν-intercept by h, or take the V0 intercept times e.

In an I–V plot, what do intensities change?

Only the saturation current.

• Saying the slope depends on the metal.

3. Concepts and skills

Why does a reflecting surface feel twice the force?

The photon momentum reverses, so the change is 2p instead of p.

Does the distance from a point source matter?

Yes: the intensity falls as 1/r2, so the pressure falls in the same way.

Why is F=P/2c for the hemisphere and not 2P/c?

Only half the power reaches it, and the force is the sum of the axial components, not their magnitudes.

Does the surface area matter for the momentum from a pulse?

Only through E=IAt.

How is the angle of a hanging mirror found?

Impulse gives v; energy conservation gives the height and the small angle.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using I/c for a reflecting surface.

• Forgetting the 4πr2 for a point source.

• Not converting minutes or nanoseconds to seconds.

• Adding the magnitudes of the forces on the hemisphere instead of the axial components.

3. Concepts and skills

What did Davisson and Germer observe?

Diffraction of electrons from a nickel crystal, with the wavelength given by h/p.

Why do electrons show interference?

Because they behave as waves of wavelength h/p.

Why is there a minimum wavelength in an X-ray tube?

The highest photon energy equals the full kinetic energy of the electron.

How are the work function and wavelength of photoelectrons linked?

Through Kmax, which fixes p and hence λe.

Which condition is used for a scattering maximum in this chapter?

The one fixed by the data given in the question (grating form dsinθ=λ unless planes are described).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using λ=hc/K for the electron's wavelength (that is the photon's).

• Confusing the Bragg and grating conditions.

• Forgetting to convert the energy to eV from J.

• Using the work function as the kinetic energy of the photoelectron.
