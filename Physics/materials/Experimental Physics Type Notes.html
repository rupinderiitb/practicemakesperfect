Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'n vernier divisions are equal to m main-scale divisions' is given and the least count or vernier constant is asked.

• The jaws are closed, the vernier zero is to the left or right of the main zero, and a division coincides; then an object is measured.

• A travelling microscope (for the refractive index of a glass slab) is described by its divisions per cm and vernier divisions.

2. Formulae and laws

Least count (vernier constant) in terms of the scales

LC=1MSD−1VSD

If N vernier divisions equal (N−1) main-scale divisions

LC=value of 1 MSDN

If N vernier divisions equal (N+1) main-scale divisions

LC=value of 1 MSDN (the vernier division is larger)

A vernier with 10 divisions equal to 9 main-scale divisions, 1 MSD =1mm

LC=0.1mm

Travelling microscope: 20 divisions per cm on the main scale and 50 vernier divisions equal to 49 main-scale divisions

LC=(1/20)cm50=0.001cm

Observed reading

MSR+VSR×LC (VSR = coinciding vernier division)

Zero error when the vernier zero is to the right of the main zero (positive)

+n×LC, n = coinciding division with the jaws closed

Zero error when the vernier zero is to the left of the main zero (negative)

−n×LC with n the coinciding division as given in the question

Corrected reading

observed reading−zero error (add the magnitude for a negative error)

Number of main-scale divisions that coincide with N vernier divisions when LC=120N cm and 1 MSD =1mm

Set NVSD=xMSD, so LC=(1−xN)MSD, and solve for x

3. Concepts and skills

Why is a vernier division slightly smaller than a main-scale division?

So that the difference of the two lengths is a small fixed quantity: the least count. Each vernier division lags a little behind the main-scale lines.

How do you read a vernier measurement?

Note the main-scale reading just to the left of the vernier zero, then the number of the vernier division that coincides with a main-scale line; multiply this number by LC and add.

What does a positive zero error mean for later readings?

All readings come out too large by the zero error, so it is subtracted.

How is the least count of a vernier of N divisions found when only the main-scale value is given?

Divide the value of the smallest main-scale division by the number of vernier divisions: LC=1 MSDN.

Same principle in a travelling microscope?

Yes. Find the value of 1 MSD from the divisions per cm, then divide by the number of vernier divisions.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the wrong unit for 1 MSD (cm in the question, mm in the answer).

• Subtracting a negative zero error instead of adding its magnitude.

• Taking the vernier division number as the main-scale reading.

• Reading 'divisions per cm' as the value of one division (1 MSD =1divisions per cm cm).

3. Concepts and skills

What is the pitch, and what is the least count?

The pitch is the distance the screw advances in one full turn; the least count is the pitch divided by the number of divisions on the circular scale (the smallest length it can measure).

What does the ratchet do?

It limits the pressure so the object is not squeezed and the reading stays the same from one measurement to another.

How do you find the true reading when there is a zero error?

Take the observed reading and subtract the zero error with its sign: positive error is subtracted, negative error is added.

Which quantities can a spherometer measure?

Radius of curvature of a spherical surface and the thickness of a thin plate, not the volume or diameter of a wire.

Why is the zero error found with the jaws closed?

It is the reading when the true length is zero, so it is the offset in every later measurement.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking the pitch as the least count.

• Adding a positive zero error (it must be subtracted).

• Forgetting to count the 'visible' main-scale divisions in MSR (for example 4 divisions of 0.5 mm is 2 mm).

• Mixing mm and cm between the pitch and the final answer.

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• Several readings of the same quantity are given and the percentage error, mean or the correct way to record the result is asked.

• The accuracy or error in g, a density or an area is asked from the errors in the measured length, time or sides.

• A meter bridge with a shunt, or a wire loaded in Searle's apparatus and immersed in a liquid.

3. Concepts and skills

What is the difference between absolute, mean and percentage error?

Absolute error is the size of the deviation of one reading; mean error is their average; percentage error is mean error divided by the mean value times 100.

Why are errors added, not subtracted, when two quantities are subtracted?

The worst case of each measurement acts in opposite directions, so the maximum error is the sum of the individual errors.

How many digits should a result have?

No more than the least count allows: the mean is rounded to the same decimal place as the error.

What does immersing the load in a liquid do in Searle's experiment?

The buoyant force reduces the pull on the wire, so the extension falls in the ratio of the apparent weight to the actual weight.

How does a shunt shift the null point of a meter bridge?

It lowers the resistance in that gap, so the null point moves towards the side of the gap with the smaller resistance.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the mean error where the question asks for the standard deviation, or the reverse.

• Forgetting the factor 2 for T2 in the error of g.

• Not converting 50 oscillations into the period before finding the error.

• Using the actual weight, not the apparent weight, after immersion.
