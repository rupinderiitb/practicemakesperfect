• 'In a ferromagnetic material below the Curie temperature, a domain is defined as ...'

• 'A paramagnetic sample shows a magnetisation of 6 A/m at 0.4 T and 4 K: the magnetisation at 0.3 T and 24 K.'

• 'The figure gives the B–H variation of a ferromagnetic material: the retentivity, coercivity and saturation.'

2. Formulae and laws

Susceptibility

χ=MH

Diamagnetic

−1≤χ<0, independent of T

Curie's law

χ∝1T, M∝BT

Retentivity, coercivity

B at H=0; H at B=0

3. Concepts and skills

Which materials move towards the stronger part of the field?

Paramagnetic and ferromagnetic ones; diamagnetic ones move to the weaker part.

What happens to a ferromagnet above the Curie temperature?

It becomes paramagnetic, because the domains are destroyed.

Why is soft iron used for an electromagnet?

It has high permeability and low retentivity, so it is magnetised and demagnetised easily.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Saying that diamagnetism depends on temperature.

• Calling paramagnetic attraction 'strong'; it is weak.

• Mixing up retentivity (on the B axis) and coercivity (on the H axis).

2. Formulae and laws

Permeability

μ=μ0(1+χ)=μ0μr

Percentage increase in B

100χ%

Magnetic intensity

H=nI=NIl

Magnetisation

M=mV, χ=MH

Energy of a solenoid

U=B22μAl

3. Concepts and skills

Does H depend on the material?

No, H=nI depends only on the current and the turns.

Why is the increase in B equal to χ?

Because B=μ0(1+χ)H against μ0H for air.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using μ=μ0χ instead of μ0(1+χ).

• Using the turns N instead of the turns per metre n in H=nI.

• Forgetting to convert turns per cm to turns per m.

Apparent dip

3. Concepts and skills

Why is the true dip never more than the apparent dip?

Because tanδ=tanδ′cosα≤tanδ′.

Which component controls the oscillation of a compass needle?

The horizontal component BH.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using Bsinδ for the oscillations.

• Taking the ratio of the frequencies instead of the ratio of their squares.

• Swapping tanδ and tanδ′ in the apparent dip relation.

3. Concepts and skills

Why does a magnet fall slowly in a copper tube?

Eddy currents in the tube oppose the motion, so the magnet reaches a constant speed.

Which component of the earth's field gives the emf of a horizontal rod moving horizontally?

The vertical component.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the horizontal component for a horizontal rod moving horizontally.

• Forgetting the factor 1/2 for a rotating blade.

• Adding the emfs of the blades; they are in parallel.
