1. How do I spot this type?

• 'E⃗ is along −z and B⃗ along +x at a point: the direction of propagation.'

• 'A 35 MHz plane wave travels along x with E⃗=9.6ĵ V/m: find B⃗.'

• 'B⃗=3×10−8sin(1.6×103x+48×1010t)ĵ T: write E⃗.'

• 'E⃗=E0(x̂+ŷ)sin(kz−ωt): find B⃗' or 'polarisation k̂, propagation (î+ĵ)/2: find B⃗'.

2. Formulae and laws

Direction of travel

Ê×B̂=ĉ (direction of propagation)

Amplitudes

E0=cB0, B0=E0c

Other field's direction

B̂=ĉ×Ê, Ê=B̂×ĉ

Wave along +x or −x

phase kx−ωt: +x; phase kx+ωt: −x

General relation

B⃗=1ωk⃗×E⃗

Wave speed from the equation

v=ωk

3. Concepts and skills

How do I get the direction of the other field quickly?

Put the right hand along E⃗, curl towards B⃗ and the thumb gives the direction of travel; or use the cross product ŷ×ẑ=x̂ and its cyclic forms.

What if the vector is not a unit vector?

Its magnitude multiplies the amplitude: for (4î−3ĵ) the amplitude is 5 times the scalar in front.

Do E⃗ and B⃗ have the same phase?

Yes. They rise and fall together; only the amplitudes (by c) and the directions differ.

What does +ωt in the phase tell me?

The wave travels towards the negative direction of the axis in kx±ωt.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Reversing the sign of the direction when the phase is kx+ωt.

• Forgetting that Ê×B̂ must point along the travel, so B⃗ is not simply along E⃗ rotated by 90∘ in either sense.

• Dropping the factor in the amplitude when the field is written with a non-unit vector.

• Using B0=cE0 (wrong way round).

• Taking the speed of the wave as c without checking ω/k in the given equation.

3. Concepts and skills

How is the energy shared between E⃗ and B⃗?

Equally: each carries half of the energy density, so ⟨uE⟩:⟨u⟩=1:2.

Which power do I use for a bulb?

Only the radiated light: efficiency times the rated power.

What is the intensity 'due to the electric field' of a source?

Half of the total intensity.

If the diameter of the cylinder is halved, what happens to the energy?

The volume falls to 1/4, so the field amplitude must double to hold the same energy.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the rated power of the bulb instead of the radiated power.

• Forgetting the factor 1/2 in I=1/2ε0cE02 or mixing rms and peak values.

• Using the area 4πr instead of 4πr2.

• Taking the electric energy density as the total.

• Not noting that c is needed when it is not given.

2. Formulae and laws

Order of wavelength (long to short)

radio > microwave > infrared > visible > UV > X-ray > gamma

Ranges

microwave 0.1m to 1mm; infrared 1mm to 700nm; UV 400 to 1nm; X-ray 1 to 10−3nm; gamma below 10−3 nm

Shortest X-ray wavelength

λmin(Å)=12400V(volt)

Photon energy

E=hcλ

Photons per second at equal power

n∝λ

3. Concepts and skills

Which waves come from a metal target hit by fast electrons?

X-rays (the continuous spectrum and the characteristic lines from inner shells).

Sources of the bands

Radio: aerials; microwaves: magnetron or Klystron; infrared: vibration of atoms and molecules; X-rays: inner-shell electrons; gamma: the nucleus.

Uses

Radar: microwaves; physiotherapy and greenhouse effect: infrared; sterilising and water purification: UV; diagnosis and crystal structure: X-rays; cancer treatment: gamma or X-rays.

Do all colours travel at the same speed in vacuum?

Yes. Red and blue differ in frequency and wavelength, not in speed in vacuum.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Reversing the order of wavelength when the list gives the energies.

• Mixing up the ranges of UV and X-rays, or of microwave and infrared.

• Using λmin=12400/V with the voltage in kilovolt.

• Counting photon number density as proportional to 1/λ at equal power.

• Placing radar in the radio band or mobile phones in the microwave band without reading the options.

2. Formulae and laws

Gauss, electric

∮E⃗⋅dA⃗=qε0

Gauss, magnetic

∮B⃗⋅dA⃗=0

Faraday

∮E⃗⋅dl⃗=−dΦBdt

Ampere–Maxwell

∮B⃗⋅dl⃗=μ0(ic+id), id=ε0dΦEdt

Displacement current in a capacitor

id=ic in the circuit; through a part of the area, id⋅A0A

AC across a capacitor

imax=ωCV0

3. Concepts and skills

Why did Maxwell add the displacement current?

A changing electric field acts like a current: it keeps Ampere's law consistent for a charging capacitor and gives the wave solution.

Which equation is new in time-varying conditions?

Faraday's law: ∮E⃗⋅dl⃗=0 for static fields, but equal to −dΦB/dt when B⃗ changes.

Do EM waves carry momentum?

Yes: energy U carries momentum U/c; the photon mass is zero.

Are EM waves deflected by electric or magnetic fields?

No, they carry no charge.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Writing E⃗⋅dA⃗=ε0q (inverted).

• Matching Gauss's law of magnetism with the electric one.

• Taking the whole 6 A as the displacement current through a small area.

• Calling the speed 1/μ0ε0 valid inside a medium.

• Judging the statements on 'direction of propagation along E⃗' without remembering that the wave is transverse.

3. Concepts and skills

Does the frequency matter when I find B from E?

No. The frequency only decides the wavelength.

What if the wave is not in vacuum?

Use E0=vB0 with v=ω/k read from the equation.

Is the argument (x−ct) a hint for the speed?

Yes: the coefficient of t over the coefficient of x gives the speed.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using B0=cE0.

• Using the frequency in the formula for B.

• Forgetting to convert MHz to Hz.

• Using c=3×108 when the equation gives a different speed.

3. Concepts and skills

Why is the time of exposure not needed?

Pressure is force per area at any instant; the time only enters if the question asks for the momentum or energy delivered.

Why is the pressure on a mirror double?

The momentum of the light is reversed, so the change in momentum is 2× the incident momentum.

Does the wave exert a force on a charge at rest?

Only the electric force qE, since the magnetic force needs a velocity.

What do I do with two waves?

Add the electric fields and the magnetic fields at the point, then take qE⃗+qv⃗×B⃗.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using I/c for a perfectly reflecting surface.

• Using the exposure time to divide the pressure.

• Forgetting the magnetic force or using it for a charge at rest.

• Mixing W/cm​2 and W/m​2.

• Taking v⃗×B⃗ with the wrong sign.

3. Concepts and skills

What changes when the wave enters a medium?

The speed and wavelength; the frequency stays the same.

How do I find εr from the speed?

εr=(c/v)2μr.

Why do E0 and B0 change in opposite ways at an interface?

The intensity is fixed (no loss), E0 falls by n and B0=nE0/c rises by n.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using εr alone for the index and ignoring μr.

• Keeping the vacuum wavelength in the medium.

• Dividing E0 by n instead of by n.

• Forgetting that v=c/μrεr needs both relative values.
