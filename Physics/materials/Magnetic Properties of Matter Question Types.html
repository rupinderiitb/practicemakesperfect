JEE Main Physics – Magnetic Properties of Matter

Types of questions asked in JEE Main 2019–2025 (55 questions)

Key message: these 55 questions fall into only 5 repeating types. The three biggest types (Types 1, 2, 3) account for 39 questions (71%). The material and solenoid questions (Types 1 and 2) are 30 (55%). About 15% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Dia-, para- and ferromagnetism: properties, domains, Curie's law and hysteresis – 16 questions

What is asked: Statements or short conceptual questions about the three kinds of magnetic material, the domains of a ferromagnet, the temperature dependence, the hysteresis loop and the choice of a core.

What to master: χ of dia-, para- and ferromagnets, the movement in a non-uniform field, the Curie temperature, Curie's law χ ∝ 1/T, retentivity, coercivity and saturation, and soft iron for electromagnets.

Forms that appear: two statements on the temperature dependence of diamagnetism; the definition of a domain below the Curie temperature; the statements on a paramagnetic substance (A to D); a diamagnetic sphere with a paramagnetic cavity; a sample with magnetisation 6 A/m at 0.4 T and 4 K moved to 0.3 T and 24 K; reading retentivity, coercivity and saturation from a B-H loop; the M-H and χ-T graphs of a diamagnet.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (3), 2021 (5), 2022 (2), 2023 (3), 2024 (1), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Dia-, para- and ferromagnetic materials: properties, domains and statements: 11 questions

Curie's law: magnetisation and susceptibility against temperature: 2 questions

Hysteresis loop, retentivity and coercivity: soft iron and electromagnets: 3 questions

Type 2: Susceptibility, permeability and the field in solenoids and toroids filled with a material – 14 questions

What is asked: A solenoid, a toroid or a coil is filled with a material; find the permeability, the percentage change in the field, the magnetic intensity, the demagnetising current or the energy.

What to master: μ = μ₀(1 + χ), μ_r = 1 + χ, the percentage increase 100χ, H = nI, I = Hl/N for the coercivity, χ = M/H and the energy density B²/2μ.

Forms that appear: χ = 99 or 499 for the permeability; a toroid filled with aluminium (χ = 2.2×10⁻⁵) or magnesium; a solenoid of 1200 turns on a 2 m tube; a magnet of coercivity 5×10³ A/m in a 30 cm solenoid of 150 turns; a solenoid with μ_r = 2 and its energy; a cube with a dipole moment of 20×10⁻⁶ J/T in H = 60×10³ A/m.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2021 (2), 2022 (2), 2023 (4), 2024 (1), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Susceptibility and permeability: μ = μ₀(1 + χ) and the percentage increase in B: 7 questions

Solenoid and toroid: magnetic intensity H = nI and the demagnetising current: 5 questions

Energy in a solenoid with a core and susceptibility from a dipole moment: 2 questions

Type 3: Earth's magnetic field: dip, apparent dip and the oscillation magnetometer – 9 questions

What is asked: A compass needle or a dip circle is used at one or two places; find the angle of dip, the total field or the ratio of the fields from the components, the dip angles and the oscillation counts.

What to master: B_H = B cos δ, B_V = B sin δ, tan δ = B_V/B_H, tan δ′ = tan δ / cos α, and N² ∝ B cos δ for oscillations.

Forms that appear: a needle at 45° with the meridian making 60° with the horizontal; apparent dip 45° at 30° to the meridian; a needle oscillating 20 times per minute at dip 30° and 10 times at dip 60°; B_V = 6×10⁻⁵ T with dip 37°; B_H = 0.5 G with dip 30°; the force to keep a needle of pole strength 1.8 A m horizontal.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2021 (2), 2022 (4), 2023 (1)

Type 4: Links to induction: motional emf in the earth's field, eddy currents and mutual flux – 9 questions

What is asked: Questions that use the earth's field or a magnet moving near a conductor: the emf in a fan, a rod or an aeroplane, the damping by eddy currents, or the flux and emf in coils.

What to master: ε = B_V l v, ε = ½B_Vωl², Lenz's law and eddy currents, Φ₂ = MI₁, and ε = −dΦ/dt with a current I = I₀t(1 − t).

Forms that appear: a ceiling fan of three 80 cm blades at 1200 rpm with dip 30°; a 20 cm rod moved east at 20 m/s with dip 45°; an aeroplane with a wing span of 10 m at 180 km/h; a magnet dropped in a copper tube; the core of a galvanometer; insulating and metallic balls dropped from the same height.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (1), 2022 (1), 2023 (4), 2024 (1)

Type 5: Bar magnet as a magnetic dipole: torque, work, oscillation and the neutral point – 6 questions

What is asked: A bar magnet or a needle is placed in a magnetic field; find the torque, the work in turning it, the field of the magnet at a point, or the field from a neutral point.

What to master: τ = MB sin θ, W = 2MB from stable to unstable equilibrium, T = 2π√(I/MB), B_eq = μ₀M/(4π(d² + l²)^{3/2}) and the uncertainty in B from the uncertainty in length.

Forms that appear: M = 0.5 A m² in 8×10⁻² T: the work from the most stable to the most unstable position; a small magnet at 30° experiencing 0.018 N m in 0.06 T; a needle with M = 9.85×10⁻² that makes 10 oscillations in 5 s; a neutral point at 18 cm from a 14 cm magnet with B_H = 0.4 G; a time varying field B cos ωt.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (2), 2024 (1), 2025 (1)

6. Suggested study order for students

Start with the properties of dia-, para- and ferromagnets (Type 1, 16 questions, 29%): they are statements from NCERT and can be learnt in one sitting.

Then the susceptibility and solenoid questions (Type 2, 14, 25%), which are single formulas.

The earth's field (Type 3, 9) is out of the syllabus now; do it only after the rest, together with the induction links (Type 4, 9).

Finish with the bar magnet as a dipole (Type 5, 6) and the one-off question.

7. A routine to follow for every question

Decide the family first: statements on materials, a solenoid or toroid with a core, the earth's field, a link to induction, or a bar magnet in a field.

For a material question write μ = μ₀(1 + χ) and H = nI; the field changes by the factor 1 + χ.

For the earth's field resolve it into B_H = B cos δ and B_V = B sin δ before anything else; a horizontal wire moving horizontally cuts only B_V.

For an oscillation question use N² ∝ B_H and take a ratio between the two cases; for a bar magnet use τ = MB sin θ and W = 2MB.
