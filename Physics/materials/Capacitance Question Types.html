JEE Main Physics – Capacitance

Types of questions asked in JEE Main 2019–2025 (123 questions)

Key message: these 123 questions fall into only 9 repeating types. The three biggest types (Types 1, 2, 3) account for 63 questions (51%). The charge-sharing and dielectric-insertion questions (Types 3 and 4) are 31 (25%). About 36% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Slabs, metal sheets and layered dielectrics: capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor – 27 questions

What is asked: Find the capacitance, or a constant or length in it, when a slab, a metal sheet, an air gap or several dielectrics fill the space between the plates.

What to master: C = ε₀A/(d − t + t/K), a metal sheet of thickness t, dielectrics in series (add d/K) and side by side (add K·A), the dielectric strength and voltage rating, and a leaky dielectric.

Forms that appear: a slab of constant 5 and thickness 1 mm in a 2 mm gap; a metal sheet of thickness 2d/3; a plate moved by 0.2 cm so that the capacitance is unchanged; layers of constants 3 and 5 with a 7.5 pF capacitor in parallel; three layers of constants K, 3K and 5K with thicknesses d, 2d and 3d; a leakage current through a dielectric of resistivity 200 Ω m.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2021 (6), 2022 (7), 2023 (5), 2024 (3), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Slab, metal sheet or air gap between the plates: capacitance: 13 questions

Layers in series and dielectrics side by side: 10 questions

Dielectric strength, voltage rating, leakage and scaling of C: 4 questions

Type 2: Series and parallel combinations: equivalent capacitance, charges and voltages – 19 questions

What is asked: Find the equivalent capacitance of a network, or the charge, the potential difference or the energy of one capacitor in a network connected to a battery.

What to master: Parallel C = C₁ + C₂, series 1/C = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂, equal charge in series and equal voltage in parallel, shorted capacitors, and the energy ratio of the same capacitors in parallel and in series.

Forms that appear: four capacitors of 16 μF in a bridge-like figure; six capacitors of 8 μF with shorting wires; seven capacitors of 2 μF to give 6/13 μF; the charge on the 15 μF capacitor of three in series on 13 V; the charge on the 4 μF capacitor in a mixed network; the ratio of the charges on three capacitors.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (3), 2021 (3), 2022 (3), 2023 (3), 2024 (1), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Equivalent capacitance of a network: 8 questions

Charge, potential difference and energy of capacitors in a chain: 11 questions

Type 3: Connecting charged capacitors: charge sharing and energy lost – 17 questions

What is asked: A charged capacitor is joined to another, charged or uncharged, and the question asks for the final charge, voltage or capacitance, or for the energy lost or the ratio of the energies.

What to master: Conservation of charge, the common potential V = (C₁V₁ ± C₂V₂)/(C₁ + C₂), and the loss C₁C₂(V₁ − V₂)²/(2(C₁ + C₂)).

Forms that appear: a 10 μF capacitor at 50 V joined to an uncharged capacitor that brings the voltage to 20 V; 5 μF charged to 5 μC; two capacitors C and 2C charged to V and 2V and joined with opposite polarity; the energy lost by 600 pF at 200 V joined to an identical capacitor; a charged capacitor joined in series to an uncharged one.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (5), 2021 (1), 2022 (3), 2023 (3), 2024 (2), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Final charge, voltage or capacitance after connecting: 7 questions

Energy lost or ratio of energies on connecting: 10 questions

Type 4: Dielectric inserted into a charged capacitor: charge, potential difference and energy – 14 questions

What is asked: A dielectric is inserted while the battery stays connected or after it has been removed; the question asks for the new charge, potential difference, energy, work done or force.

What to master: With the battery on V is fixed (C, Q and U increase), with the battery off Q is fixed (V and U decrease and the slab is pulled in), and the induced charge q(1 − 1/K).

Forms that appear: a 40 μF capacitor on 100 V filled with a dielectric of constant 2: the extra charge and the energy change; a 14 pF capacitor charged to 12 V and a porcelain plate inserted: the mechanical energy of the oscillating plate; two identical capacitors with the key opened before the dielectric is inserted; an induced charge of 4 × 10⁻⁶ C on a 5 × 10⁻⁶ C capacitor.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (4), 2022 (3), 2023 (1), 2024 (1), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Dielectric inserted with the battery connected: 4 questions

Dielectric inserted after the battery is removed: 8 questions

Induced (bound) charge on the dielectric: 2 questions

Type 5: Steady-state circuits with resistors and capacitors – 11 questions

What is asked: A capacitor sits in a circuit with a battery and resistors; find the charge on it, the ratio of the charges or its energy in steady state.

What to master: No current in a capacitor branch in steady state, the current in the resistor loop, the potential difference between the two nodes the capacitor joins, and the internal resistance of the battery.

Forms that appear: a 4 μF capacitor in series with a 6 Ω resistor and two 2 μF capacitors, a 5 V battery with 1 Ω internal resistance and a 4 Ω resistor; a bridge of 3, 4, 9 and 2 Ω with a 6 μF capacitor across it; a capacitor across the lowest of three 2 kΩ resistors.

Asked in: 2021 (2), 2022 (1), 2023 (2), 2024 (5), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Steady-state charge in a battery and resistor circuit: 6 questions

Bridge circuits and circuits with joined points: 5 questions

Type 6: Basic capacitor relations and energy stored – 11 questions

What is asked: Direct questions on charge, potential difference and capacitance of plates and spheres, and on the energy, energy density, force and work for a capacitor.

What to master: C = ε₀A/d, the potential difference (q₁ − q₂)/2C for two charged plates, 4πε₀R and 4πε₀R₁R₂/(R₂ − R₁) for spheres, U = ½CV² and u = ½ε₀E².

Forms that appear: a capacitor of 1 μF with plates charged +2 μC and +4 μC; the flux through a surface enclosing a plate; an isolated sphere enclosed by an earthed sphere; a charge that increases by 2 C to increase the energy by 44 %; the work to pull the plates apart; the energy density between plates 1 μm apart at 20 V.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2022 (4), 2023 (2), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Charge, potential and capacitance of plates and spheres: 6 questions

Energy, energy density, force and work: 5 questions

Type 7: Charging and discharging through a resistor, and graphs – 10 questions

What is asked: Find the time or the capacitance in an RC circuit, compare the times for the charge and the energy to fall in a discharge, or read a q–t, q–V or V–Q graph.

What to master: q = Q₀(1 − e^{−t/RC}), q = Q₀e^{−t/RC}, τ = RC, the capacitor as a wire at t = 0 and as an open circuit at t = ∞, and the output of an RC circuit for a pulse train.

Forms that appear: 100 V across 100 Ω and 1 μF: the time to reach 50 V; the potential difference across the capacitor that rises to 2 V in 1 μs; the time for the energy to fall to half compared with the charge falling to one eighth; the output of an RC circuit for a square wave.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (2), 2024 (1), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Charging and discharging through a resistor: 6 questions

Graphs: q–t, q–V and V–Q: 4 questions

Type 8: Non-uniform capacitors: stair plates, tilted plates and varying permittivity – 8 questions

What is asked: The plates are stepped or tilted, or the permittivity or the dielectric changes with position; find the capacitance by splitting the capacitor into parallel or series pieces or by integration.

What to master: Parallel pieces for steps, strips dx/(ε(x)A) in series for a varying permittivity, and the first-order expansion for small angles or small α.

Forms that appear: a flat plate and a stair-like plate with steps of A/3 and height d; four plates with the 2nd and 4th connected; the dielectric constant K₀(1 + αx); two plates at a small angle α; a triangular dielectric between square plates.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (2), 2021 (2), 2022 (1), 2023 (1), 2024 (1)

Which form was asked:

Stair plates and plate arrangements: 4 questions

Varying gap or permittivity: integration: 4 questions

Type 9: Displacement current, LC oscillations and capacitors in AC circuits – 6 questions

What is asked: Questions that join the capacitor with another chapter: the displacement current, the maximum current in an LC circuit, an AC bulb in series with a capacitor, or the energy in an electromagnetic wave.

What to master: I_d = C dV/dt = ε₀A dV/(d dt), I_max = V√(C/L), X_C = 1/ωC and the energy density ε₀E² of a wave.

Forms that appear: a displacement current of 4.425 μA when the voltage changes at 10⁶ V/s; 0.15 A charging a plate of radius 10 cm; a 100 μF capacitor at 12 V joined to 6.4 mH; a bulb and a capacitor in series on an AC supply when a dielectric is inserted.

Asked in: 2022 (1), 2023 (1), 2024 (2), 2025 (2)

5. Suggested study order for students

Start with the largest families: slabs and layered dielectrics (Type 1, 27 questions, 22%) and series and parallel networks (Type 2, 19, 15%).

Then charge sharing (Type 3, 17 questions) and a dielectric inserted into a charged capacitor (Type 4, 14).

Then steady-state circuits (Type 5, 11) and the basic relations and energy (Type 6, 11).

Finish with charging and discharging (Type 7, 10), non-uniform capacitors (Type 8, 8) and the links to displacement current, LC and AC (Type 9, 6).

6. A routine to follow for every question

Decide first which family the question belongs to: a network to reduce, charge sharing, a dielectric inserted (battery on or off), a steady-state circuit, an RC transient, or a non-uniform capacitor.

Write what is constant: the voltage when the battery is connected, the charge when it is removed or when the capacitors are isolated.

Convert the units first (μF, nF, pF, cm², mm) and write C = Kε₀A/d for each region.

Reduce a network from the far end: capacitors with both ends at one point drop out, then combine series and parallel.

In a steady-state circuit remove the capacitor branch, find the currents, and read the capacitor voltage from the nodes.
