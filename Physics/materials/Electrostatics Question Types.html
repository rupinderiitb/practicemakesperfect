Key message:

  • These 203 questions fall into only 7 repeating types (plus a small group of conceptual or one-off questions).
  • The three biggest types (Types 1, 2, 3) account for 101 questions (50%).
  • Gauss’s law fields and Coulomb’s law (Types 1 and 2) are 70 questions (34%);
  • Potential and dipole (Types 3 and 4) add 57 (28%).
  • About 88% of the questions are decided by applying a formula, 9% by a concept or reasoning, and the rest need both.

(How to read this: in Physics a “type” is the law or formula that unlocks the question. For each ‘type’ the document says what the question looks like, which laws and formulas you need, and how often it was asked)

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Gauss's law and fields of symmetric charge distributions – 37 questions

What is asked: Find the field of an infinite sheet, a combination of sheets, a charged sphere or shell, a cylinder or a line charge, sometimes with a density that changes with r.

What to master: Pick a Gaussian surface that matches the symmetry and count only the enclosed charge. Memorise the standard results: sheet σ/2ε₀, conductor surface σ/ε₀, shell kQ/r² outside and zero inside, solid sphere kQr/R³ inside, line λ/2πε₀r. For non-uniform ρ(r), integrate.

Formulas and laws: E = σ/2ε₀ (sheet); E = kQ/r² (outside sphere); E = kQr/R³ (inside solid sphere); E = λ/2πε₀r (line); ∮E·dA = q_in/ε₀

Forms that appear: two or three parallel sheets, shell inside a shell, solid sphere with ρ = k·r, long charged cylinder, charge hole (superposition), electron orbiting a line charge

Style: 9 of 37 ask for an integer answer; 34 are formula-based and 3 concept-based.

Asked in: 2019 (7), 2020 (7), 2021 (2), 2022 (4), 2023 (6), 2024 (7), 2025 (4)

Which form was asked:

Infinite sheets and plates: 14 questions

Gauss's law for spheres, shells, cylinders and line charges: 23 questions

Type 2: Coulomb's law, charge sharing and equilibrium of charges – 33 questions

What is asked: Force between point charges (in air or a medium), what happens when conducting spheres touch, where a charge is in equilibrium, and balls hanging from strings.

What to master: Coulomb's law with the dielectric constant; charge conservation and equal potential after contact (q ∝ R); the maximum-force condition for splitting a charge; free-body diagram of a hanging ball (T, mg, F).

Formulas and laws: F = kq₁q₂/(Kr²); q₁/q₂ = R₁/R₂ (joined spheres); T cos θ = mg, T sin θ = F

Forms that appear: identical spheres touched and separated, two spheres of radius R and 3R joined, charge divided so that force is largest, two hanging balls in air and in oil, three charges in a line

Style: 12 of 33 ask for an integer answer; 33 are formula-based and 0 concept-based.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (7), 2022 (8), 2023 (7), 2024 (5), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Contact and charge sharing between conducting spheres: 9 questions

Coulomb force, effect of a medium, equilibrium of point charges: 19 questions

Suspended charged balls: angle between strings, immersion in a liquid: 5 questions

Type 3: Electric potential and potential energy – 31 questions

What is asked: Potential at a point, potential energy of a group of charges, work done in moving a charge, potential of spheres and shells, drops that merge, and the link between V and E.

What to master: V is a scalar, so add it directly. Energy of a system = sum over pairs. Work = charge × potential difference. Connected conductors have equal potential. E = −dV/dx.

Formulas and laws: V = kq/r; U = Σ kq_iq_j/r_ij; W = qΔV; E = −dV/dr; U_merged = n^(5/3)U_drop

Forms that appear: potential at the centre of a ring or half ring, energy of charges at the corners of a square, 27 drops merging into one, work in moving a charge along a circle, V–r graph of a shell

Style: 8 of 31 ask for an integer answer; 28 are formula-based and 3 concept-based.

Asked in: 2019 (6), 2020 (4), 2021 (4), 2022 (3), 2023 (6), 2024 (4), 2025 (4)

Which form was asked:

Potential and potential energy of point-charge systems; work done: 13 questions

Potential of conducting spheres and shells; merging drops; spheres joined by a wire: 12 questions

Potential of rings/arcs and the relation between V and E: 6 questions

Type 4: Electric dipole – 26 questions

What is asked: Field and potential of a dipole at a point, or the torque, energy and motion of a dipole placed in an external field.

What to master: Dipole moment p = q·2a (a vector from − to +). Know the axial and equatorial fields and the potential formula; for a dipole in a field use τ = p × E and U = −p·E.

Formulas and laws: p = 2qa; E_axial = 2kp/r³; E_eq = kp/r³; V = kp cos θ/r²; τ = pE sin θ; U = −pE cos θ

Forms that appear: field ratio on axis and equator, dipole near a line charge or sheet, work to rotate a dipole by 180°, time period of small oscillations, two dipoles placed together

Style: 5 of 26 ask for an integer answer; 23 are formula-based and 1 concept-based.

Asked in: 2019 (5), 2020 (2), 2021 (2), 2022 (1), 2023 (5), 2024 (3), 2025 (8)

Which form was asked:

Field and potential of a dipole: 12 questions

Dipole in an external field: torque, energy, work, oscillation: 14 questions

Type 5: Electric flux – 25 questions

What is asked: Flux through a flat surface in a given field, through a cube or cuboid in a field that depends on position, or through a surface when a charge sits at a special place.

What to master: φ = E·A with the area vector; for a non-uniform field compute face by face. For a charge near a cube use Gauss's law with symmetry (corner: q/8ε₀ through the cube; a face receives a fixed fraction).

Formulas and laws: φ = EA cos θ; φ = q_in/ε₀; ∮E·dA for a cube

Forms that appear: field (2î + 4ĵ + 6k̂) through a face in the x–z plane, charge at a corner of a cube, charge at the centre of a face, hemisphere with charge at the centre, E = 4000x² through a cube

Style: 13 of 25 ask for an integer answer; 25 are formula-based and 0 concept-based.

Asked in: 2020 (1), 2021 (6), 2022 (2), 2023 (4), 2024 (7), 2025 (5)

Which form was asked:

Flux through a surface from E·A: 12 questions

Flux from enclosed charge (charge at a corner, on a face, centre of a cube, hemisphere): 13 questions

Type 6: Electric field of point charges and of rings, arcs, discs and wires – 23 questions

What is asked: Net field at a point because of several point charges (including a point of zero field) or of a continuous distribution such as a ring, arc, disc or finite wire.

What to master: Add field vectors using symmetry; for continuous charge take a small element, find its field, and integrate the components that survive. Learn the ring-on-axis and disc-on-axis formulas.

Formulas and laws: E = kq/r²; E_ring = kQx/(x²+R²)^(3/2); E_disc = (σ/2ε₀)(1 − x/√(x²+R²))

Forms that appear: field at the corner of a square, zero-field point of two charges, charged ring on its axis, field at the centre of a 120° arc, uniformly charged finite wire

Style: 3 of 23 ask for an integer answer; 23 are formula-based and 0 concept-based.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (2), 2021 (5), 2022 (2), 2023 (2), 2024 (3), 2025 (5)

Which form was asked:

Net field of point charges: null points and symmetric arrangements: 15 questions

Field of a ring, arc, disc or finite wire: 8 questions

Type 7: Motion of a charged particle in a uniform electric field – 11 questions

What is asked: A charged particle accelerates in a uniform field, enters between plates, or a charged pendulum sits in a horizontal field.

What to master: Treat it like projectile motion with acceleration a = qE/m. For a pendulum use the effective gravity g′. Equilibrium and energy methods both work.

Formulas and laws: a = qE/m; y = ½at²; qE = mg (balance); g′ = √(g² + (qE/m)²)

Forms that appear: electron entering parallel plates and leaving at an angle, charged bob in a horizontal field, drop held in balance by a vertical field

Style: 2 of 11 ask for an integer answer; 11 are formula-based and 0 concept-based.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (1), 2022 (3), 2023 (2), 2025 (2)

6. Suggested study order for students

Start with Gauss’s law and symmetric fields (Type 1), then Coulomb’s law and charge sharing (Type 2): 70 questions (34%).

Then potential and energy (Type 3) and flux (Type 5): 56 questions (28%).

Then the electric dipole (Type 4) and fields of rings, arcs and discs (Type 6).

Finish with a charged particle in a uniform field (Type 7) and the conceptual statements/graphs, which use the same ideas.

7. A routine to follow for every question

Draw the situation and mark every charge and its sign.

Decide what is wanted: force, field, potential, flux or energy. Force and field are vectors; potential and energy are scalars.

If the charge distribution is symmetric (sphere, cylinder, sheet) think of Gauss’s law first; otherwise add the contributions of the pieces.

Use the standard result for the shape and check units; for numerical answers keep k = 9×10⁹ N m²/C².

Check the answer with a limit (r → ∞, x ≫ R, or a symmetric point) before choosing the option.
