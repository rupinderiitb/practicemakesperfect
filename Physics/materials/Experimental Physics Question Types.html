3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Vernier callipers and travelling microscope: least count, zero error and reading – 21 questions

What is asked: Find the least count from a scale relation (n vernier divisions = m main-scale divisions), or find the correct reading after allowing for zero error.

What to master: Vernier constant = value of 1 MSD − value of 1 VSD; reading = MSR + VSR × LC; sign rules for positive and negative zero error.

Forms that appear: 10 VSD = 9 MSD, 50 VSD = 49 MSD, 20 VSD = 19 MSD; the zero of the vernier lies left or right of the main zero; a travelling microscope with 20 divisions per cm.

Asked in: 2020 (1), 2021 (3), 2022 (6), 2023 (1), 2024 (8), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Least count and scale relations of a vernier (also travelling microscope): 11 questions

Zero error and the corrected reading of a vernier caliper: 10 questions

Type 2: Screw gauge and spherometer: least count, zero error and reading – 17 questions

What is asked: Find the least count of a screw gauge from its pitch and number of circular divisions, or the corrected diameter when the instrument has a zero error.

What to master: LC = pitch / divisions; the zero error is positive when the circular zero is below the reference line; corrected reading = MSR + (CSR − zero error) × LC.

Forms that appear: pitch 0.5 mm with 50 or 100 divisions, six rotations moving 3 mm, zero of the circular scale 5 divisions below the line, a student's difference of readings, what a spherometer cannot measure.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (3), 2021 (4), 2022 (2), 2023 (1), 2024 (3), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Least count, pitch and instrument knowledge (screw gauge, spherometer): 6 questions

Zero error and the corrected diameter or reading of a screw gauge: 11 questions

Type 3: Errors in measurement and other experiments – 6 questions

What is asked: Find the percentage error or the correct way to record a result from several readings, or an experiment other than the two instruments (metre bridge, Searle's apparatus).

What to master: Mean and mean deviation; combination of errors; rounding to the least count; for the metre bridge, the balance condition; for Searle's apparatus, the extension of a wire and the effect of buoyancy.

Forms that appear: four readings of a wire's thickness, accuracy of g from a pendulum, mean and standard deviation of a pencil's diameter, a shunt across one gap of a metre bridge, a load immersed in a liquid.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2022 (1), 2023 (2), 2024 (1)

Which form was asked:

Errors: mean, standard deviation, least-count error and percentage error: 3 questions

Other experiments: meter bridge and Searle's apparatus: 3 questions

5. Suggested study order for students

Start with the two instruments (Types 1 and 2): 38 questions (86%). They need only two least-count formulas and the zero-error rule.

Practise the sign rule for zero error until it is automatic: it decides most of the 2 or 3 mark-losing mistakes.

Then do Type 3: errors and the two other experiments (6 questions).

6. A routine to follow for every question

Write down what each scale reading means: the value of 1 main-scale division and the number of divisions on the vernier or circular scale.

Find the least count first (vernier constant or pitch / divisions) and convert everything to the same unit.

Find the zero error with its sign, then subtract it from the observed reading.

For error questions, write mean, deviation and percentage error step by step, and keep the right number of decimal places.

Check that the answer is a multiple of the least count and that the unit is the one asked for.
