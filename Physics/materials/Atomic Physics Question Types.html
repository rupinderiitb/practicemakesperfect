The transition and series types (1, 3) are 56 questions (56%); the Bohr orbit type (2) is 27 (27%). About 33% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Transitions: photon energy, wavelength, frequency and number of spectral lines – 32 questions

What is asked: A hydrogen atom or a hydrogen-like ion jumps between levels, or is excited by a photon or an electron beam; the energy, wavelength or frequency of the photon, the level reached, the ion, or the number of spectral lines is asked.

What to master: E = 13.6 Z²(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²) eV; λ(nm) = 1240/E(eV); lines from level n: n(n − 1)/2; an atom absorbs only the exact gap between two levels, an electron may give less than its energy.

Forms that appear: the photon wavelength of a 4 → 1 transition, Z = 4 jumping from n = 4 to n = 2, a 12.5 eV electron beam on hydrogen, the transition that emits 124.1 nm in an energy-level figure, an excited Li⁺⁺ giving six lines, the minimum energy for a Balmer line, the Franck–Hertz experiment.

Asked in: 2019 (5), 2020 (2), 2021 (5), 2022 (3), 2023 (8), 2024 (5), 2025 (4)

Which form was asked:

Photon energy, wavelength or frequency of a given transition (including energy-level figures): 13 questions

Finding the quantum number or the ion from a photon energy or wavelength: 7 questions

Excitation by a photon or an electron, thresholds, Franck–Hertz and number of spectral lines: 12 questions

Type 2: Bohr orbits: radius, speed, energy and angular momentum – 27 questions

What is asked: Use the Bohr model to compare the radius, speed, period, energy or angular momentum of an electron in different orbits of hydrogen or a hydrogen-like ion.

What to master: r = 0.529 n²/Z Å; v = 2.19×10⁶ Z/n m s⁻¹; E = −13.6 Z²/n² eV; L = nh/2π; T ∝ n³/Z²; K = −E, U = 2E.

Forms that appear: the radius of the 5th orbit of Li⁺⁺ or the ratio for Li²⁺ and He⁺, the speed in the 3rd orbit from the 7th, the period of the first excited state, which level of C⁺ has the ground-state energy of hydrogen, the ratio K/U, the change of angular momentum, the orbital current.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (3), 2022 (4), 2023 (7), 2024 (7), 2025 (4)

Which form was asked:

Radius, speed, time period and frequency in the nth orbit: scaling with n and Z: 12 questions

Energy of hydrogen-like ions: equal energies, ionisation energy, K, U and E (and binding energy): 8 questions

Angular momentum, momentum, orbital current and magnetic moment of the electron: 7 questions

Type 3: Hydrogen spectrum series: wavelengths and ratios – 24 questions

What is asked: Find the longest or shortest wavelength of a spectral series, or the ratio of the wavelengths or frequencies of two lines of hydrogen or a hydrogen-like ion.

What to master: 1/λ = R Z²(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²); Lyman n₁ = 1, Balmer 2, Paschen 3; the longest wavelength is the first member and the shortest is the series limit.

Forms that appear: the longest wavelength of the Paschen series, the lowest wavelength of Balmer from that of Lyman, the ratio of Hα to Hβ, the third member of Lyman to the first member of Paschen, a figure with levels A, B, C and the ratio λ₁/λ₂.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (2), 2021 (4), 2022 (2), 2023 (5), 2024 (6), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Longest or shortest wavelength of a series, series limits and which series is visible: 9 questions

Ratios of wavelengths or frequencies of different lines, including level figures: 15 questions

Type 4: Atomic models, Rutherford scattering and Bohr's postulates – 8 questions

What is asked: Choose the correct statement about the Thomson, Rutherford and Bohr models and the nucleus, or use the scattering of α-particles: the graph, the impact parameter and the distance of closest approach.

What to master: Rutherford's nuclear atom; classical collapse; Bohr's postulates; nuclear volume ∝ A and constant density; N ∝ 1/sin⁴(θ/2); b ∝ cot(θ/2); r₀ = 2kZe²/K.

Forms that appear: an assertion about Bohr's model and electron repulsion, which statement is true of Rutherford and Thomson, the Rutherford gold-foil graph, d₁ = x d₂ for scattering angles of 60° and 90°, the closest approach to a Z = 80 nucleus.

Asked in: 2020 (1), 2022 (4), 2024 (2), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Statements and assertion–reason on the Thomson, Rutherford and Bohr models; nuclear size: 5 questions

Scattering graph, impact parameter and distance of closest approach: 3 questions

Type 5: Bohr quantisation in other situations – 5 questions

What is asked: Apply the Bohr quantisation condition to a different force or a different particle (a particle in a power-law potential, a muon, K X-rays), or combine a Bohr transition with another topic (photoelectrons in a magnetic field).

What to master: mvr = nh/2π together with the force law; E = K + U; the ionisation energy scales with the mass; the Kα photon equals the difference of two atomic levels.

Forms that appear: a particle in U = U₀r⁴ or U = ½kr², hydrogen with a muon instead of the electron, molybdenum Kα X-ray and the L-hole energy, the radiation of the 3 → 2 transition on gold in a magnetic field.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2021 (4)

Type 6: Recoil of the atom on photon emission – 3 questions

What is asked: Find the recoil speed of the hydrogen atom, or the recoil effect on the wavelength, when it emits a photon.

What to master: Momentum conservation: Mv = E/c. The recoil energy is a tiny fraction of the photon energy, so the effect on the wavelength is of order 10⁻⁹.

Forms that appear: the recoil speed for n = 2 → 1 and for n = 5 → 1, the percentage change of the wavelength for n = 3 → 2.

Asked in: 2021 (1), 2024 (2)

5. The few genuinely new questions

These do not fit a repeating pattern. See each once; they do not need heavy practice.

Mass defect of a reaction converted to energy: E = Δm c² in joules and then in kilowatt-hours (2024)

6. Suggested study order for students

Start with the transition and series types (Types 1 and 3): 56 questions (56%). One formula, E = 13.6 Z²(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²) eV, settles almost all of them.

Then the Bohr orbit scalings (Type 2): 27 questions (27%). Learn r ∝ n²/Z, v ∝ Z/n, E ∝ Z²/n².

Then the models and scattering questions (Type 4) and the Bohr quantisation in other systems (Type 5): 13 questions, mostly statements and short derivations.

Finish with recoil (Type 6, 3 questions) and the one-off mass-defect question.

7. A routine to follow for every question

Name the species and the levels: Z, the starting level n₁ and the final level n₂ (a first excited state is n = 2, the third excited state is n = 4).

Write the energy as 13.6 Z²(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²) eV, or the wave number as R Z²(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²); take ratios so that R and the units cancel.

Use scaling when two orbits or two ions are compared: r ∝ n²/Z, v ∝ Z/n, E ∝ Z²/n², T ∝ n³/Z².

Convert units: λ(nm) = 1240/E(eV), Å to nm, Hz to eV with h = 4.14×10⁻¹⁵ eV s.

Check by a limit: the Lyman limit is 912 Å, the first Balmer line is 6563 Å, and hydrogen needs 10.2 eV for its first excitation.
