1. How do I spot this type?

• 'A 5 V Zener is used on a 25 V supply with 400Ω in series; the Zener current is 4 times the load current. Find IL and RL.'

• 'A 8 V Zener with series resistance R across a 20 V supply, maximum Zener current 25 mA: the minimum R.'

2. Formulae and laws

Voltage across a conducting Zener

V=VZ (constant in breakdown)

Series resistor current

I=Vin−VZR

Load current

IL=VZRL

Zener current

IZ=I−IL

Maximum Zener current

IZmax=PVZ

Minimum series resistance (no load)

Rmin=Vin−VZIZmax

Power dissipated in the Zener

P=VZIZ

3. Concepts and skills

How do I know whether the Zener is in breakdown?

Assume it is and find the voltage it would have to hold; if the supply across it is below VZ it is off and acts as an open circuit.

Does the Zener current change when the load changes?

Yes. The total current through R stays fixed, so IZ falls by as much as IL rises.

What happens to a Zener in forward bias?

It acts like an ordinary diode and drops about 0 V (or its cut-in voltage).

Why is a series resistance needed?

To limit the current so that IZ stays below P/VZ and the Zener is not damaged.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the supply voltage across the load instead of VZ.

• Taking the whole current as the load current and forgetting that the Zener carries part of it.

• Using Vin in place of Vin−VZ when finding the current through R.

• Mixing mW with W in IZmax=P/VZ.

• Treating a Zener that is not in breakdown as a fixed-voltage source.

• 'Two ideal diodes (or diodes with forward resistance 25Ω, 30Ω or 50Ω) are in a network; find the current or the equivalent resistance between A and B.'

2. Formulae and laws

Forward bias

p side at the higher potential: diode conducts (wire plus cut-in drop)

Reverse bias

n side at the higher potential: no current (open circuit)

Real forward diode

Vdrop=0.3 V (Ge), 0.7 V (Si), or rf in series

Dynamic resistance

rd=ΔVΔI=1slope of the I-V curve

Current in a series circuit

I=V−∑VdropR

Rectifier output

half-wave: one half cycle; full-wave: both half cycles; a capacitor smooths the output

3. Concepts and skills

How do I treat an ideal diode?

Forward biased: a wire. Reverse biased: an open circuit.

What if I cannot tell which diodes conduct?

Assume a state, solve, and check that the current in each 'on' diode is positive and the voltage across each 'off' diode is reverse.

What is the difference between static and dynamic resistance?

Static: V/I at a point. Dynamic: ΔV/ΔI, the reciprocal of the slope at the point.

Can two back-to-back diodes work as a transistor?

No. The base of a transistor is a thin, lightly doped layer shared by both junctions.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Reading forward bias from the battery position without checking which side of the diode is at the higher potential.

• Adding the diode's forward resistance when the diode is reverse biased.

• Dropping 0.7 V across the Ge diode and 0.3 V across the Si diode (the other way round).

• Using V/I in place of ΔV/ΔI for the dynamic resistance.

• Keeping the negative half cycle in the output of a half-wave rectifier.

2. Formulae and laws

Current gain (CE)

β=ΔICΔIB

Current gain (CB)

α=ΔICΔIE=β1+β

Emitter current

IE=IB+IC

Voltage gain

AV=βRoutRin=ΔVoutΔVin

Power gain

AP=βAV=β2RoutRin; in dB: 10log10AP

Base current and output voltage

IB=VBB−VBERB, VCE=VCC−ICRC

Saturation

VCE≈0, IC=VCCRC, IB,min=ICβ

3. Concepts and skills

Which junctions are biased in an amplifier?

Emitter-base forward, collector-base reverse (active region).

When does a transistor act as a switch?

In cut-off (OFF, IC≈0) and saturation (ON, VCE≈0).

How do I read β from the output characteristic?

At constant VCE, take ΔIC between two neighbouring curves and divide by the step in IB.

Why is the base thin and lightly doped?

So that most of the carriers from the emitter reach the collector and few recombine.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using IC/IB (dc gain) when the question asks for the small-signal gain, or the other way round.

• Dividing by IE instead of IB for β.

• Forgetting that power gain is βAV (or β2Rout/Rin), not AV.

• Using VCC across the transistor in saturation instead of VCE≈0.

• Reading the current from the wrong curve (IB label) of the characteristic.

How do I find the output for given inputs?

Write the output of every gate on the figure, working from the inputs to Y.

How do I find the inputs for a given output?

Test the four rows 00, 01, 10, 11, or work backwards from the last gate.

What if an input is shared by two gates?

Use the same value (0 or 1) at both places.

How is a NAND or NOR gate drawn?

As AND or OR with a small circle (bubble) at the output.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Missing a small circle (NOT) on an input or output of a gate.

• Treating a NAND gate as an AND gate.

• Taking the output at the wrong point of the circuit.

• Reading an input value at the edge of an interval instead of inside it.

• Not changing the output at every change of either input.

3. Concepts and skills

What is a universal gate?

A gate from which every other gate can be built: NAND and NOR.

How do I identify the gate?

Find its output for 00, 01, 10 and 11 and compare with the standard columns.

What does a bar over a variable mean?

A NOT gate on that variable.

Are NAND with joined inputs and NOT the same?

Yes. A⋅A¯=A‾.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Mixing up the OR and AND symbols (flat back and pointed back).

• Forgetting the NOT on the input when applying De Morgan.

• Writing NAND as A‾⋅B‾ (this is NOR).

• Missing the small circle that turns AND into NAND, or OR into NOR.

• Not checking the answer with at least two input rows.

NOT

A=0→1, A=1→0

Rows to check

first and last rows are often enough to remove two or three options

3. Concepts and skills

How do I build a truth table?

Write the four rows of (A,B), add a column for every intermediate gate and fill them one by one.

How do I use the options quickly?

Compare the output for (0,0) and (1,1) with the options before filling the other rows.

What if the circuit has three inputs?

Write eight rows, from 000 to 111.

How do I read the circuit for a given table?

Identify the column (AND, OR, NAND, NOR, XOR) and look for the combination that gives it.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Writing the rows in a different order from the options.

• Missing a NOT at one input.

• Reading the column of the wrong gate in a two-gate combination.

• Stopping after two rows when two options agree on them.

• Mixing the NOR and NAND columns.

2. Formulae and laws

Carrier product

nenh=ni2

n-type

pentavalent donor (As, P); majority carriers electrons, Fermi level near the conduction band

p-type

trivalent acceptor (B, Al); majority carriers holes, Fermi level near the valence band

Electric field in the depletion layer

E=V0d

Photon energy

E(eV)=1242λ(nm)

Energy gain over a barrier

1/2mv′2=1/2mv2−eV0

3. Concepts and skills

Is the product nenh changed by doping?

No. It stays ni2 at a given temperature.

What does temperature do to a semiconductor?

More electrons reach the conduction band, so the resistance falls (the opposite of a metal).

What are the diffusion and drift currents at zero bias?

They are equal and opposite, so the net current is zero; under forward bias the diffusion current is larger.

How does light create a carrier?

A photon with hν≥Eg (or at least the acceptor-level gap) lifts an electron across the gap.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using ne=ni for the doped sample.

• Saying the resistance of a semiconductor rises with temperature.

• Using λ in metres with E=1242/λ (which needs nm).

• Taking the field as V0×d instead of V0/d.

• Confusing the donor and acceptor impurities (pentavalent / trivalent).

• 'The conductivity of a photodiode changes only for wavelengths below 660 nm; the band gap is X/8 eV.'

2. Formulae and laws

Photon energy

E=hcλ, E(eV)=1240λ(nm)=12400λ(Å)

LED

emits photons with hν≈Eg, forward biased, heavily doped

Photodiode

reverse biased; responds when hν≥Eg, so λmax=hcEg

Solar cell

no external bias, large junction area, converts light into electrical power

3. Concepts and skills

Why is a photodiode used in reverse bias?

The change in the small reverse current produced by light is easy to measure.

What decides the colour of an LED?

The band gap: a larger gap gives a shorter wavelength.

How is a solar cell different from a photodiode?

It has a larger junction area, works without external bias and delivers power.

Is an LED lightly or heavily doped?

Heavily doped, with the junction forward biased.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the maximum wavelength as a minimum (the photodiode needs a wavelength below the cut-off).

• Forgetting to convert Å or nm to metres when using hc/λ in joules.

• Calling the LED lightly doped or reverse biased.

• Saying the solar cell needs an external bias.

• Rounding 1240/λ too early.
