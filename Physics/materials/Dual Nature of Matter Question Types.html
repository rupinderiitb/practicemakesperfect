3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: de Broglie wavelength: comparing particles (same energy, potential, momentum or wavelength) – 26 questions

What is asked: Compare the de Broglie wavelengths (or kinetic energies, momenta, speeds) of electron, proton, neutron, deuteron and α-particle when they have the same energy, are accelerated through the same potential, or have the same wavelength.

What to master: λ = h/p = h/√(2mK) = h/√(2mqV). For the same K, λ ∝ 1/√m; for the same V, λ ∝ 1/√(mq); same λ means the same momentum, so K ∝ 1/m. Masses: m_p ≈ 1836 m_e, m_α ≈ 4 m_p, m_d ≈ 2 m_p, q_α = 2e.

Forms that appear: proton and electron with the same λ, α-particle and proton accelerated through 200 V, proton and deuteron with a given λ ratio, 'arrange λ_e, λ_p, λ_α in order', an α-particle and a carbon-12 atom with the same K.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (2), 2021 (8), 2022 (4), 2023 (7), 2024 (4)

Which form was asked:

Same kinetic energy: order and ratio of wavelengths: 8 questions

Accelerated through a potential: ratios of λ, momentum and energy: 8 questions

Same wavelength or momentum: ratio of K, velocity or mass: 10 questions

Type 2: Photoelectric effect with two conditions: ratios and unknowns from two wavelengths, frequencies or metals – 23 questions

What is asked: Two lights (or two metals) are used and a quantity such as the threshold wavelength, work function, stopping potential, kinetic energy or speed ratio of the photoelectrons is asked.

What to master: K_max = hν − φ = hc/λ − φ = eV₀ written twice; φ is eliminated by subtraction. Speed v ∝ √(hν − φ). Below threshold, no emission.

Forms that appear: stopping potential 8 V at λ and 2 V at 3λ, K doubles when λ changes from 800 nm to 500 nm, photon energies of 2φ and 10φ, 4 eV and 2.5 eV giving a speed ratio of 2, frequency halved and intensity doubled.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (3), 2021 (5), 2022 (6), 2023 (3), 2024 (2), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Two stopping potentials at two wavelengths: threshold wavelength and work function: 8 questions

Two kinetic energies at two wavelengths: work function or comparison of K: 6 questions

Ratio of photoelectron speeds for given photon energies, and one-line frequency or metal questions: 9 questions

Type 3: Photoelectric effect with one condition: maximum kinetic energy, threshold and stopping potential – 20 questions

What is asked: Find the maximum kinetic energy, stopping potential, work function, threshold frequency or wavelength from one set of data, or the K_max for light given as a wave or from an atomic transition.

What to master: K_max = hν − φ = eV₀; E(eV) = 1240/λ(nm); ν₀ = φ/h; the photon energy of an EM wave is ħω; for a transition E = 13.6(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²) eV; circular path r = √(2mK)/(eB).

Forms that appear: UV of 4.13 eV on a metal of φ = 3.13 eV, φ = 6.63 eV and ν₀, 380 nm threshold with 260 nm light, E = 200 sin(6×10¹⁵ t) + sin(9×10¹⁵ t), a photoelectron in a magnetic field bending into a 30 cm arc.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2021 (4), 2022 (2), 2023 (2), 2024 (5), 2025 (4)

Which form was asked:

K_max, stopping potential and work function from a given wavelength or photon energy: 7 questions

Threshold frequency and wavelength; will light of a given colour eject electrons?: 7 questions

Photoelectrons from a light-wave field, an atomic transition or a magnetic field: 6 questions

Type 4: Photoelectric effect: concepts and statements – 14 questions

What is asked: Choose the correct statement about stopping potential, intensity, frequency, threshold, time lag or the failure of the wave theory; assertion–reason and 'which of the following' questions.

What to master: Stopping potential and K_max depend on frequency and the metal only; intensity sets the number of photoelectrons; threshold frequency exists; emission is instantaneous.

Forms that appear: increasing the intensity of the light, 'stopping potential depends on…', Assertion–Reason on stopping potential and intensity, which phenomena are not explained by wave theory, statements A to E on saturation current and K_max.

Asked in: 2021 (3), 2022 (2), 2023 (3), 2024 (3), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Stopping potential, intensity and frequency: assertion–reason and statement questions: 8 questions

Which observations or phenomena are correct (multiple statements): 6 questions

Type 5: de Broglie wavelength of a single particle: dependence on energy, voltage and temperature – 14 questions

What is asked: Find how the de Broglie wavelength of one particle changes when its kinetic energy, accelerating voltage or temperature changes, or find its value for an electron in an atom or a microscope.

What to master: λ = h/p; λ ∝ 1/√K ∝ 1/√V; for a gas molecule λ = h/√(3mkT); Bohr model 2πr = nλ; electron λ = 12.27/√V Å.

Forms that appear: KE becomes E/4, voltage from 20 kV to 40 kV, λ increases by 50 %, λ reduced to 75 % by adding energy, the second excited state of hydrogen, resolving 7.5×10⁻¹² m in an electron microscope, an electron in a gas at 300 K.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (3), 2022 (1), 2023 (4), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

How λ changes when K, V or T changes: 7 questions

Numerical wavelength: atoms, electron microscope, thermal electrons: 7 questions

Type 6: Matter waves with motion: particles in fields, collisions and fission – 11 questions

What is asked: Find the de Broglie wavelength after time t of an electron moving in an electric or magnetic field, or after a collision, fission or disintegration where momentum is conserved.

What to master: Magnetic field: speed and λ are constant. Electric field: v² = v_x² + (eEt/m)². Momentum conservation as a vector; equal and opposite momenta give equal wavelengths.

Forms that appear: an electron entering a field with v₀ î and E = −E₀ k̂, a nucleus at rest splitting into M′/3 and 2M′/3, 4M breaking into M and 3M, an elastic collision of A (m/2) with B (m/3) at rest, a perfectly inelastic collision at right angles.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (3), 2021 (1), 2022 (2), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Electron in an electric or magnetic field: λ at a later time: 5 questions

Collisions, fission and disintegration: momentum conservation and de Broglie wavelength: 6 questions

Type 7: Photon versus matter wave: same energy or same wavelength – 10 questions

What is asked: Compare an electron (or proton) with a photon having the same energy or the same wavelength, or use the energy–momentum relation.

What to master: Particle: λ = h/√(2mE). Photon: λ = hc/E, p = E/c. Same λ means the same momentum. E² = p²c² + m²c⁴.

Forms that appear: an electron and a photon with the same energy E, same de Broglie wavelength with the electron at 25 % of c, ratio of kinetic energies, a photon of momentum 10⁻²⁷ kg m s⁻¹ against a particle of mass 9.1×10⁻³¹ kg.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (3), 2022 (2), 2024 (1), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Electron and photon of the same energy: 4 questions

Electron and photon of the same wavelength: 3 questions

Type 8: Photoelectric graphs: stopping potential, kinetic energy and current – 9 questions

What is asked: Read the work function or h from a graph, or choose the correct graph or the effect of changing intensity on it.

What to master: V₀–ν: slope h/e, intercepts ν₀ and −φ/e. K–ν: slope h. I–V: stopping potential independent of intensity, saturation current ∝ intensity. V₀–1/λ: slope hc/e.

Forms that appear: V₀ against ν for two metals, slope of the K_max–ν line, data points of an experiment, sodium emitter data, intensity I₁ < I₂ on an I–V graph.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (2), 2023 (2), 2024 (3), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

V₀–ν graphs: slope, intercept and work function: 6 questions

K–ν, I–V and V₀–1/λ graphs and the effect of intensity: 3 questions

Type 9: Photon number, power and intensity – 7 questions

What is asked: Find the number of photons emitted per second from a given power and wavelength, or the number of electrons ejected from an intensity and efficiency.

What to master: N = P/E = Pλ/hc; photon flux = I·A/(hν); electrons ejected = flux × efficiency; E = P/N fixes the spectrum region.

Forms that appear: a 2 mW laser at 500 nm, 15 kW with 10¹⁶ photons per second, 200 W sources at 300 nm and 500 nm, intensity 100 W m⁻² at 900 nm on 1 cm², one photon in 10³ ejecting an electron.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2022 (1), 2023 (1), 2024 (2)

Which form was asked:

Number of photons per second from power: 4 questions

Photon flux, intensity and the number of electrons emitted: 3 questions

Type 10: Radiation pressure and photon momentum – 7 questions

What is asked: Find the force, pressure or momentum delivered by light on an absorbing or reflecting surface.

What to master: Photon momentum p = E/c; absorbing: F = IA/c; reflecting: F = 2IA/c; I = P/(4πr²); momentum delivered = E/c (absorbing) or 2E/c (reflecting).

Forms that appear: a 450 W source 2 m from a reflecting surface, a 360 W cm⁻² beam on a non-reflecting surface, a laser pulse hitting a hanging mirror, a point source at the centre of a reflecting hemisphere, a pulse of 20 mW for 300 ns.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2023 (2), 2024 (2), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Force and pressure of light on a surface: 4 questions

Momentum delivered by a light pulse: 3 questions

Type 11: Davisson–Germer, X-ray cut-off and photoelectron wavelength – 5 questions

What is asked: Questions linking matter waves with other experiments: electron diffraction from a crystal, the cut-off wavelength in an X-ray tube, and the wavelength of photoelectrons.

What to master: Davisson–Germer verifies λ = h/p; diffraction condition for the first maximum; X-ray cut-off λ_min = hc/(eV); photoelectron λ = h/√(2mK_max).

Forms that appear: electrons scattered from a target of 1 Å spacing with the first maximum at 60°, an electron of wavelength λ striking an X-ray tube target, photoelectrons of two metals with λ_B = 2λ_A.

6. Suggested study order for students

Start with the matter-wave types (Types 1, 5, 6, 7, 11): 66 questions (45%). One formula, λ = h/√(2mK), settles almost all of them.

Then the photoelectric equation (Types 2, 3, 4, 8): 66 questions (45%). Learn K_max = hν − φ = eV₀ and the V₀–ν graph.

Then photon counts and radiation pressure (Types 9 and 10): 14 questions, only P = Nhν and p = E/c.

Finish with the one-off Millikan-type question.

7. A routine to follow for every question

Write down what is compared (same K, same V, same p or same λ) and the masses and charges of each particle.

For matter waves use λ = h/p and reduce it to the form that fits the data: h/√(2mK) or h/√(2mqV); take the ratio, not the values.

For the photoelectric effect write K_max = hν − φ = eV₀ for every case given; eliminate φ by subtraction, or use v ∝ √(E − φ) for speed ratios.

Convert units first: eV and nm with hc = 1240 eV nm, ħω for light waves, cm² to m², minutes to seconds.

Check the answer by a limit: no emission below threshold, V₀ independent of intensity, λ shorter for a heavier particle.
