JEE Main Physics – Ray Optics

Types of questions asked in JEE Main 2019–2025 (202 questions)

Key message: these 202 questions fall into only 8 repeating types. The three biggest types (Types 1, 2, 3) account for 98 questions (49%). The lens questions (Types 3, 4 and 7) are 80 (40%); the mirror questions (Type 2) are 32 (16%). About 29% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Refractive index, speed of light, apparent depth and slabs – 34 questions

What is asked: Use Snell's law and the refractive index to find an angle, the speed or wavelength of light in a medium, the apparent depth of an object under layers of liquid, or the emergent ray from a slab.

What to master: n = c/v, constant frequency, Snell's law, apparent depth = real depth / n, lateral shift of a slab, and the relation n = √(μ_rε_r).

Forms that appear: speed of light in two media of 1.5×10⁸ and 2.0×10⁸ m/s: relative index; reflected and refracted rays at 90°; a medium of relative permittivity 3 and permeability 4/3: critical angle; apparent depth of a pole or coin under two immiscible liquids; the lateral shift of a ray through a glass slab; the shift of the focus of a microscope when a liquid is poured in.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (3), 2021 (1), 2022 (9), 2023 (10), 2024 (3), 2025 (6)

Which form was asked:

Refractive index, speed, frequency and wavelength of light in a medium: 17 questions

Apparent depth, liquid layers and the shift of the image: 12 questions

Slab: emergent ray, lateral shift and the ray in a block: 5 questions

Type 2: Plane and spherical mirrors: images, magnification and motion – 32 questions

What is asked: Find the position, size or nature of the image formed by a plane or spherical mirror, count the images in two mirrors, or follow a ray or a moving object.

What to master: The mirror formula and magnification with signs, f = R/2 (unchanged in a liquid), reflection law for plane mirrors and mirrors at an angle, and speed of the image.

Forms that appear: reflected ray at 30° and the angle with the incident ray; two mirrors at right angles with a ray; the image of a point source in a mirror moving at some speed; a convex mirror forming an image half the size; an object at 15 cm and 25 cm from a concave mirror of radius 40 cm; magnification ½ at 40 cm; a graph of magnification against object distance; the focal length of a mirror found from an experiment.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (3), 2021 (7), 2022 (2), 2023 (7), 2024 (2), 2025 (8)

Which form was asked:

Plane mirrors: angle of reflection, images in one or two mirrors, motion of the source: 10 questions

Spherical mirrors: position, magnification and focal length: 16 questions

Mirror in a liquid, graphs, moving objects and experiments with a mirror: 6 questions

Type 3: Refraction at a spherical surface and the lens formula – 32 questions

What is asked: Find the image from a single refracting spherical surface, or from a thin lens: the position, magnification, nature, or the graph and sign conventions.

What to master: n₂/v − n₁/u = (n₂ − n₁)/R, 1/v − 1/u = 1/f, m = v/u, and the shape of the 1/v against 1/u line.

Forms that appear: a region of index 1 and 1.5 separated by a surface of radius 25 cm; light from a point source in air on a glass surface of radius 50 cm; an object at the focus of a concave lens; a lens with a real image of the same size at two positions; the distance between the object and a 3 times magnified virtual image; the graph of m against v.

Asked in: 2019 (6), 2020 (3), 2021 (4), 2022 (4), 2023 (2), 2024 (7), 2025 (6)

Which form was asked:

Refraction at a spherical surface: image position and nature: 10 questions

Thin lens: position, size and nature of the image: 15 questions

Graphs and sign conventions of lens formula, and experiments with a lens: 7 questions

Type 4: Focal length and power of a lens: lens maker's formula, lens in a liquid, lenses in contact – 32 questions

What is asked: Find the focal length or power of a lens from its radii and index, after it is put in a liquid, cut, silvered, or placed in contact with another lens.

What to master: Lens maker's formula, power P = 1/f, powers adding in contact, cut and silvered lenses, and the change in a liquid.

Forms that appear: radii of curvature 10 cm and 15 cm: the focal length; a convex lens of 18 cm in air immersed in water; a plano-convex lens in a liquid with the plane side silvered; a double convex lens cut into two halves; a convex lens in contact with a concave lens.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (2), 2021 (3), 2022 (3), 2023 (4), 2024 (3), 2025 (13)

Which form was asked:

Lens maker's formula: focal length and power from the radii and the index: 10 questions

Lens in a liquid: change in focal length and power: 8 questions

Lenses in contact, cut lenses, silvered or liquid lenses and lens with a liquid layer: 14 questions

Type 5: Prisms and dispersion – 24 questions

What is asked: Use the prism formula for the angle of minimum deviation or the refractive index, combine thin prisms, or judge statements on dispersion and the rainbow.

What to master: n = sin((A + δ_m)/2)/sin(A/2), δ = (n − 1)A for thin prisms, deviation and dispersion of combined prisms, and the nature of dispersion.

Forms that appear: n = cot(A/2): the angle of minimum deviation; a ray in a prism of index √3 at minimum deviation; two prisms of angle 4° and 6° combined to give no deviation; crown and flint prisms for an achromatic combination; the primary rainbow; the graph of the angle of deviation against the angle of incidence.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (1), 2021 (7), 2022 (3), 2023 (3), 2024 (4), 2025 (4)

Which form was asked:

Minimum deviation: angle of deviation, refractive index and prism angle: 12 questions

Statements, graph of deviation, dispersion and the rainbow: 7 questions

Thin prisms in combination: net deviation, angles and dispersive power: 5 questions

Type 6: Optical instruments: microscope, telescope, eye and resolving power – 17 questions

What is asked: Find the magnifying power, tube length or focal length of a microscope or a telescope, the resolving power or limit of resolution, or the correction for an eye defect.

What to master: Magnifying power of the compound microscope and the telescope, resolving power 1.22λ/D, the effect of a medium on resolution, and the lens for each eye defect.

Forms that appear: magnifying power 375 with a tube length of 150 mm and an objective of 5 mm; a telescope of tube length 60 cm and magnifying power 5; the limit of resolution of a 200 cm objective; a microscope immersed in oil; a friend who cannot see a window mesh clearly.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (4), 2021 (5), 2022 (3), 2023 (2)

Which form was asked:

Microscope: magnifying power, tube length and focal lengths: 8 questions

Telescope: tube length, magnifying power and mirrors: 3 questions

Resolving power, limit of resolution and eye defects: 6 questions

Type 7: Two-lens and lens–mirror systems – 16 questions

What is asked: Locate the final image formed by two lenses on an axis, or by a lens with a plane or curved mirror behind it; or find the equivalent focal length and power.

What to master: Image of the first element as the object of the next, the equivalent focal length formula, and the double pass through a lens when light is reflected back.

Forms that appear: two convex lenses of 20 cm each 60 cm apart for a distant object; a point object with a lens and a plane mirror at 1 m; the lens followed by a convex mirror of 15 cm; two concave refracting surfaces facing each other; a concave mirror at the bottom of a glass of water.

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (1), 2023 (4), 2024 (1), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Two lenses on a common axis: final image, equivalent focal length and power: 7 questions

Lens with a mirror, or a spherical surface and a mirror: the final image: 9 questions

Type 8: Critical angle and total internal reflection – 14 questions

What is asked: Find the critical angle or the refractive index from it, or decide whether and where a ray is totally reflected: in a prism, in an optical fibre, from a source under water or in a block.

What to master: sin C = n_rarer/n_denser, condition i > C, radius of the lit circle h tan C, and the acceptance angle of a fibre.

Forms that appear: critical angle 45°: ratio of the indices; speed in the denser medium; a small source at the bottom of a tank; the optical fibre of length 2 m and diameter 20 μm; a ray in a prism that undergoes total reflection at the second face.

Asked in: 2019 (2), 2020 (2), 2021 (3), 2022 (3), 2023 (1), 2024 (1), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Critical angle: its value, and the speed or ratio of indices from it: 6 questions

Total internal reflection: prisms, optical fibre, source under water and bodies of liquid: 8 questions

A transparent film of index 2.0 on glass of index 1.45: the minimum thickness for maximum transmission of green light of 550 nm (a thin-film interference question from wave optics, not ray optics) (2025)

6. Suggested study order for students

Start with the lens types (Types 3, 4 and 7: 80 questions, 40%): the lens formula, the lens maker's formula and the two-element systems carry the chapter.

Then mirrors (Type 2, 32 questions, 16%): the mirror formula with signs, and the plane mirror rules.

Then refraction (Type 1, 34 questions) and total internal reflection (Type 8, 14): Snell's law, apparent depth and the critical angle.

Finish with prisms (Type 5, 24), instruments (Type 6, 17) and the one-off question.

7. A routine to follow for every question

Decide first which family the question belongs to: a mirror, a refracting surface or a slab, total internal reflection, a lens or a lens system, a prism, or an instrument.

Write the sign convention before the formula (object on the left, distances along the incident light positive) and keep the signs in every step.

For a refraction question draw the ray and mark the angles: Snell's law at each surface, and check i > C wherever total reflection is possible.

For a lens in a liquid or a cut lens, find the new focal length from the lens maker's formula first, then use the lens formula.

For a system of two elements treat the image of the first as the object of the second, and for a mirror behind a lens count the lens twice.
