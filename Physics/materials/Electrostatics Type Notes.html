Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• The words 'infinite sheet', 'plate', 'long line / wire', 'cylinder', 'sphere', 'shell' appear.

• The charge density is given as σ (surface), λ (line) or ρ (volume), sometimes as ρ(r) that changes with r.

• A cavity is 'carved out' of a charged sphere, or a charge sits at the centre of a conducting shell.

2. Formulae and laws

Gauss's law: ∮E⃗⋅dA⃗=

qenclosedε0 (only the charge INSIDE the surface counts)

Field of an infinite non-conducting sheet with surface density σ

E=σ2ε0, away from a positive sheet, same on both sides

Field just outside the surface of a charged conductor

E=σε0 (perpendicular to the surface)

Two parallel sheets +σ and −σ: field between them, field outside

σε0 between; 0 outside

Thin spherical shell (charge Q, radius R): E for r>R, for r<R

r>R: kQr2=Q4πε0r2; r<R: 0

Uniformly charged solid sphere: E inside at distance r

E=kQrR3=ρr3ε0

Infinite line charge λ: E at distance r

E=λ2πε0r

Long solid cylinder, uniform ρ: E inside at distance r

E=ρr2ε0

Charge enclosed when the density varies as ρ(r)

qin=∫0rρ(r′)4πr′2dr′ (spherical shells of thickness dr′)

Sphere with a cavity: how do you find the field?

Superposition: full sphere of density +ρ plus a sphere of density −ρ filling the cavity

3. Concepts and skills

How do you choose the Gaussian surface?

A surface on which E is constant (or zero) and either parallel or perpendicular to dA⃗, matching the symmetry: sphere, coaxial cylinder or pillbox.

Why is E=0 inside a conductor, and where does extra charge sit?

Free charges rearrange until the net field inside is zero; the excess charge sits on the outer surface.

A charge +q sits at the centre of a neutral conducting shell: induced charges?

−q on the inner surface, +q on the outer surface.

How do fields of several sheets combine?

Add them as vectors region by region (E=σ/2ε0 for each sheet, directed away from positive, towards negative).

A charge q is placed near an infinite sheet: what kind of motion?

The force qσ/2ε0 is constant, so it moves with uniform acceleration; use kinematics or energy.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using σ/2ε0 for a conductor (it is σ/ε0).

• Counting charge outside the Gaussian surface.

• Adding magnitudes of the fields of two sheets instead of vectors.

• Forgetting that E is zero inside a shell but not inside a solid non-conducting sphere.

• Two or three point charges placed on a line, or on a string.

• Identical spheres 'brought in contact' or 'connected by a wire'; drops that merge.

2. Formulae and laws

Force between point charges in a medium of dielectric constant K

F=q1q24πε0Kr2

Identical conducting spheres touched and separated: charge on each

q1+q22

Two spheres of radii R1,R2 joined by a wire: q1:q2 and σ1:σ2

q1:q2=R1:R2 (equal potentials); σ1:σ2=R2:R1

n identical drops merge: new radius, charge, surface density

R=n1/3r; Q=nq; σnew=n1/3σold

A charge Q is split into q and Q−q: force is maximum when

q=Q2 (then Fmax=kQ24r2)

Third charge in equilibrium, distance x from q1 (separation d)

kq1x2=kq2(d−x)2 (zero-force point)

Tcosθ=mg; Tsinθ=F=kq2(2Lsinθ)2

Same angle in air and in a liquid (density ρl, constant K): relation

FK=mg(1−ρlρ), giving ρ=KρlK−1

3. Concepts and skills

Why does charge divide in the ratio of the radii when spheres are joined?

They reach the same potential; V=kq/R so q∝R.

Where is the zero-force position for like charges? For unlike charges?

Like: between the charges. Unlike: outside, on the side of the smaller charge.

What is conserved when conductors touch?

Total charge (not the charge on each).

How is the net force on one charge found among several?

Vector sum of the Coulomb forces from each other charge.

What changes in a liquid?

The Coulomb force is divided by K, and the weight is reduced by buoyancy: mg→mg(1−ρl/ρ).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Forgetting K when the charges are in a medium.

• Taking the ratio of charges as the ratio of surface densities.

• Using the wrong side of the line for the null point of unlike charges.

• Not resolving forces along the string and perpendicular to it.

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• The question asks for V, potential energy, work done in moving a charge, or a speed gained.

• Drops merge, spheres are connected, or a graph of V against r is shown.

• Charges at the corners of a square or triangle, or a ring or half-ring with the potential at its centre.

2. Formulae and laws

Potential of a point charge q at distance r

V=kqr (a scalar; sign of q included)

Potential energy of two point charges; of a system

U=kq1q2r; system: add over all pairs

Work done by an external agent moving q from A to B

W=q(VB−VA) (the field does −W)

Charged conducting sphere (Q, R): V on the surface, inside, outside

kQR on the surface and inside; kQr for r>R

Potential at the centre of a ring or arc of radius R, charge Q

kQR (every element is at distance R)

Potential on the axis of a ring at distance x

V=kQx2+R2

Relation between E and V

E=−dVdr (Ex=−∂V∂x); VA−VB=∫E⃗⋅dl⃗

n drops (potential v, energy u each) merge: V and U of the big drop

V=n2/3v; U=n5/3u

Two spheres joined: common potential

V=q1+q24πε0(R1+R2)

Self-energy of a thin shell; of a uniform solid sphere

kQ22R; 3kQ25R

3. Concepts and skills

Why can potentials be added directly?

Potential is a scalar: V=∑kqiri.

Work done along an equipotential line

Zero.

How is the speed of a charge found from V?

Energy conservation: 1/2mv2=qΔV (gain in kinetic energy = loss in potential energy).

Potential inside a conductor or a hollow shell

Constant and equal to the surface value.

How do you spot where V=0 for two charges?

Solve kq1r1+kq2r2=0; for unlike charges this happens at points between them and outside, nearer the smaller charge.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Adding potentials as vectors or ignoring signs.

• Using U=kq1q2/r for a single charge.

• Forgetting the 2/3 and 5/3 powers when drops merge.

• Confusing work done by the field with work done by the external agent.

1. How do I spot this type?

• Two equal and opposite charges a small distance apart, or the words 'dipole moment'.

• A dipole is placed in a uniform field, near a charge, a line charge or a sheet.

• 'Short dipole', 'axial', 'equatorial' or 'small oscillation' appear.

2. Formulae and laws

Dipole moment of charges ±q separated by 2a

p=q⋅2a, direction from − to +

Field of a short dipole on the axis, on the equatorial line

Axial: 2kpr3 (along p⃗); equatorial: kpr3 (opposite to p⃗)

Potential of a dipole at (r,θ)

V=kpcosθr2 (zero on the equatorial line)

Torque on a dipole in a uniform field

τ⃗=p⃗×E⃗, τ=pEsinθ

Potential energy of a dipole in a uniform field

U=−p⃗⋅E⃗=−pEcosθ

Work to turn a dipole from θ1 to θ2

W=pE(cosθ1−cosθ2); from parallel to antiparallel: 2pE

Time period of small oscillations about equilibrium

T=2πIpE

Net force on a dipole: in a uniform field, in a non-uniform field

Zero; F=pdEdx along the gradient

3. Concepts and skills

How does the dipole field fall with distance, and the potential?

E∝1/r3 and V∝1/r2 (faster than for a single charge).

Which orientation is stable equilibrium?

p⃗ parallel to E⃗ (θ=0); θ=180∘ is unstable.

How do you add the fields of two dipoles?

Vector sum of the axial or equatorial fields at the point, with correct directions.

A dipole near a line charge or a sheet: what changes?

The field is non-uniform: find the force on each charge (or use F=pdE/dx) and the torque separately.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking the equatorial field along p⃗ (it is opposite).

• Measuring r from the wrong charge instead of the centre.

• Using U=pEcosθ without the minus sign.

• Forgetting that a uniform field gives only torque, no net force.

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'Flux through a surface/face', a cube, cuboid, hemisphere or sphere is shown.

• A field is given as E⃗=(2î+4ĵ+6k̂) or E=4000x2î and an area is stated.

2. Formulae and laws

Flux through a flat surface in a uniform field

ϕ=E⃗⋅A⃗=EAcosθ (θ = angle between E⃗ and the normal)

Total flux through a closed surface

ϕ=qenclosedε0 (independent of the shape)

Charge at the centre of a cube: flux through the cube, through one face

Charge at a corner of a cube: flux through each face not touching it

q24ε0 (total through the cube q8ε0; faces meeting at the corner get 0)

Charge at the centre of a face of a cube: flux through the cube

q2ε0

Charge at the centre of the flat face of a closed hemisphere: flux through the flat face, curved face

0; q2ε0

Non-uniform field E(x) through a cube of side a

Charge from flux

q=ε0ϕnet

3. Concepts and skills

Sign convention for flux

Outward flux is positive; lines entering a closed surface give negative flux.

What does charge outside a closed surface contribute?

Zero net flux (lines enter and leave).

How do you use symmetry to share flux between faces?

Equal faces get equal flux; imagine completing the cube (or 8 cubes) around the charge.

Unit of flux

N m2C−1 (or V m).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the angle between E⃗ and the surface instead of the normal.

• Counting the faces that touch the charge (they get zero).

• Including the charge outside the surface.

• Forgetting to integrate when the field depends on x or y.

3. Concepts and skills

How does symmetry help?

Components that cancel in pairs are dropped; only the component along the axis of symmetry is added.

How is the field of a continuous distribution found?

Take a small element dq, write dE, resolve it, and integrate the surviving component.

A square with equal charges at the corners: field at the centre?

Zero for equal like charges; for mixed signs add the diagonal vectors.

Where is the field zero for unlike charges?

On the line outside, nearer to the smaller charge.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Adding field magnitudes instead of vectors.

• Using the full arc angle where half the angle is needed.

• Using total charge Q in place of the density λ (or the reverse).

• Missing a charge's direction (sign error).

1. How do I spot this type?

• An electron or ion enters or is released in a field between plates.

• A charged ball hangs from a string or a pendulum is placed in a horizontal electric field.

• A drop is 'held in balance' by a field.

2. Formulae and laws

Acceleration of a charge q, mass m in a uniform field

a=qEm (opposite to E for an electron)

Speed gained through a potential difference V

qV=1/2mv2

Particle enters plates of length L with speed v: time, deflection, angle

t=Lv; y=1/2at2; tanθ=atv

Drop held in balance in a vertical field

Charged pendulum in a horizontal field: equilibrium angle; effective g

T=2πLg′

3. Concepts and skills

How do you treat the motion between plates?

Like a projectile: uniform velocity along the plates, uniform acceleration across them.

Which method is faster when the path is complicated?

Work–energy: work done by the field =qΔV.

When does gravity matter?

For heavy drops and balls (mg comparable to qE); ignore it for electrons and ions unless told otherwise.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking the wrong sign of acceleration for an electron.

• Using g instead of g′ for the pendulum in a field.

• Forgetting that the horizontal velocity is constant between the plates.
