2. Formulae and laws

Refractive index

n=cv=λ0λ, frequency unchanged

Snell's law

n1sini=n2sinr

Index from ε,μ

n=μrεr

Apparent depth (normal view)

dapp=dn; layers: ∑dini

Shift of the object through a layer

t(1−1n)

Lateral shift of a slab

tsin(i−r)cosr

Reflected and refracted rays at 90∘

tani=n2n1

3. Concepts and skills

What stays the same when light enters a medium?

The frequency. The speed and the wavelength both become 1/n times.

How do I find the apparent depth under several layers?

Add d/n for each layer; each layer is viewed from above and the nearest layer is treated first.

What does the lateral shift depend on?

The thickness, the angle of incidence and the index; the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray.

A ray makes 45∘ with the surface: what is i?

The angle of incidence is measured from the normal, so i=90∘−45∘=45∘.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking the angle with the surface as the angle of incidence.

• Dividing the real depth by n twice for two layers instead of layer by layer.

• Using n1/n2 the wrong way round in Snell's law.

• Changing the frequency when the light enters the medium.

• Using the shift formula t(1−1/n) for a slanting ray.

2. Formulae and laws

Mirror formula

1v+1u=1f, f=R2

Magnification

m=−vu

Plane mirror

image distance = object distance; mirror moved by x: image moves by 2x

Two mirrors at angle θ

deviation =360∘−2θ (two reflections)

Speed of the image

vi=−m2vo along the axis

Number of images in two mirrors

360∘θ−1

3. Concepts and skills

Does a mirror's focal length change in a liquid?

No. It depends only on the radius of curvature, so f stays the same.

How do I use a given magnification?

Write v=−mu, put it in the mirror formula and solve for u or f.

What does the sign of f tell me?

With the usual convention f<0 for a concave mirror and f>0 for a convex mirror.

How is the error in f found from the scale?

Differentiate 1f=1u+1v to get Δf=f2(Δuu2+Δvv2), with Δu,Δv the least count.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the wrong sign for f of a convex mirror.

• Measuring the distance between a virtual image and the object as v−u without checking the sides.

• Forgetting that the speed of the image is m2 times the speed of the object.

• Taking Δf as the least count itself.

• Counting the images in two mirrors without checking that 360∘/θ is an integer.

3. Concepts and skills

How do I treat a real or virtual image distance?

Use the sign: v>0 on the outgoing side, v<0 on the incoming side; a virtual image has the opposite side to a real one.

What does the line 1/v=1/u+1/f look like?

A straight line of slope 1; the intercept on the 1/v axis gives 1/f.

How is the mirror formula different from the lens formula?

The signs: 1v+1u=1f for a mirror and 1v−1u=1f for a lens.

Where is the image of a concave lens?

Always virtual, erect and diminished, on the object side.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the mirror formula signs for a lens.

• Taking the radius of the surface positive when the centre is on the incoming side.

• Forgetting to convert the object distance to a negative number.

• Using x1x2=f2 with distances measured from the lens instead of the focus.

3. Concepts and skills

How does the focal length change in a liquid?

It increases; if nm>nL a convex lens turns into a diverging lens.

What happens when a lens is cut along or across the axis?

Cut along the axis: same focal length, half the aperture. Cut across the axis: each piece is plano-convex with twice the focal length.

How do I find the focal length when a liquid layer is added?

The liquid layer acts as another lens with its own radius; add the powers.

Which radius is positive?

The radius of a surface is positive when its centre is on the outgoing side.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using n instead of nL/nm when the lens is in a liquid.

• Forgetting the second pass of the light through the lens in a silvered lens.

• Adding focal lengths instead of powers for lenses in contact.

• Taking the half-lens of a cut lens as having half the focal length.

3. Concepts and skills

What is special at minimum deviation?

The ray inside is parallel to the base, and the incident and emergent angles are equal.

Which colour bends most?

Violet bends most and red least; the index increases as the wavelength decreases.

How does the primary rainbow look?

Red is on the outer (upper) edge and violet on the inner edge; the light is reflected once inside the drops.

What does the graph of deviation against incidence look like?

A curve with a single minimum (the angle of minimum deviation).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using δ=(n−1)A for a thick prism.

• Forgetting that r1=r2=A/2 only holds at minimum deviation.

• Taking the primary rainbow with red at the bottom.

• Mixing up the condition for no deviation and for no dispersion.

How do I improve the resolving power?

Use a shorter wavelength, a larger aperture or a medium of larger index.

What is the final image position in normal adjustment?

Which lens corrects which defect?

Short sight: concave lens; long sight: convex lens; astigmatism: cylindrical lens.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using m=(L/fo)(D/fe) when the image is at 25 cm.

• Taking the tube length as fo alone.

• Mixing up the limit of resolution and the resolving power (they are reciprocals).

3. Concepts and skills

How do I solve a lens with a mirror behind it?

Image from the lens, then reflection in the mirror, then the lens again.

When does the final image fall on the object?

When the beam returns along its own path, i.e. falls normally on the mirror or converges to its centre of curvature.

How do I handle a virtual object for the second lens?

If the first image lies beyond the second lens, take u>0 (virtual object).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Measuring the object distance for the second lens from the first lens.

• Forgetting that the reflected light goes back through the lens.

• Using the sign of the mirror's focal length wrongly.

Critical angle

3. Concepts and skills

When does total internal reflection occur?

The light goes from a denser to a rarer medium and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle.

Why is the ray in a prism sometimes not reflected for red?

The critical angle is larger for smaller indices: red (smallest n) has the largest C.

How do I find the number of reflections in a fibre?

Length divided by the advance per reflection, dcotr.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using n1/n2 instead of n2/n1 for sinC.

• Using the angle with the surface as the angle at the wall.

• Taking i=C as total reflection (it is the limiting case).
