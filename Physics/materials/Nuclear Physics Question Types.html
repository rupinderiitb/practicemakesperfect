JEE Main Physics – Nuclear Physics

Types of questions asked in JEE Main 2019–2025 (106 questions)

Key message: these 106 questions fall into only 7 repeating types. The three biggest types (Types 1, 2, 3) account for 65 questions (61%). The decay-law types (1, 3) are 43 questions (41%); the energy type (2) is 22 (21%). About 23% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Radioactive decay law: half-life, fraction left, decay constant and activity – 26 questions

What is asked: A radioactive sample decays; find the fraction or amount left, the time taken, the half-life, the decay constant, the mean life or the activity.

What to master: Whole numbers of half-lives, fractions such as 1/8 or 7/8 or 6.25 %, two activity or count readings, the activity of a mass of ¹⁹⁸Au or of 1 µg of a nuclide, and a discharging capacitor matched to the decay.

Forms that appear: 7/8 of a sample disintegrates in 15 minutes, activity falls from 6.4×10⁻⁴ Ci to 5×10⁻⁶ Ci with a 5-day half-life, 1600 to 100 counts per second in 8 s, the activity of 1.5 mg of ¹⁹⁸Au, activity A at t₁ and A/5 at t₂, a 200 µF capacitor discharged through R so that charge and activity keep a fixed ratio.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (2), 2021 (10), 2022 (8), 2023 (5)

Which form was asked:

Fraction or amount left after a given time: 6 questions

Time or half-life from a given fraction left or decayed: 11 questions

Activity of a given mass of a nuclide: 3 questions

Decay constant, mean life and time from the exponential law: 6 questions

Type 2: Binding energy, mass defect and energy released in fission and fusion – 22 questions

What is asked: Find the binding energy, the mass defect, the energy released or the energy needed in a nuclear reaction, or the energy from a given mass of fuel.

What to master: Binding energy per nucleon data for fission and fusion, atomic masses and the Q-value, the mass defect and binding energy of a nucleus, neutron separation energy, E = mc² for 1 g, and energy in MeV, J and kWh or power from a fuel.

Forms that appear: 220 → 105 + 115 with 5.6 and 6.4 MeV per nucleon, two deuterons fusing to helium, ²³⁵U → ¹⁴⁰Ce + ⁹⁴Zr + n from atomic masses, three ⁴He into ¹²C, the Q-value of the alpha decay of ²³⁸U, 20 g of ⁷Li converted in kWh, 2 kg of hydrogen fusing against 2 kg of ²³⁵U fissioning, a star powered by the triple alpha process.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (2), 2021 (1), 2022 (2), 2023 (5), 2024 (10), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Mass defect, binding energy and mass–energy conversion: 7 questions

Energy released from binding energy per nucleon: 6 questions

Energy released from masses: the Q-value of one reaction: 5 questions

Energy from a mass of fuel, power, and fusion against fission: 4 questions

Type 3: Two radioactive samples or two decay processes – 17 questions

What is asked: Two radioactive nuclei, or two decay routes of one nucleus, are compared: the ratio of the nuclei left, the time for a given ratio, a relation between the half-lives, or the effective half-life.

What to master: Different decay constants with equal initial numbers, the half-life of one equal to the mean life of the other, different masses, atomic weights or activities, and two decay processes in one sample.

Forms that appear: A and B with decay constants 25λ and 16λ and N_B/N_A becoming e, half-lives of 10 and 20 minutes after 60 minutes, equal activities with twice the number of nuclei, two 320 g samples with half-lives of 1 day and ½ day, half-lives of 5 minutes and 30 s acting together.

Asked in: 2019 (5), 2020 (1), 2021 (4), 2022 (3), 2023 (3), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Same number of nuclei, different decay constants or half-lives: 7 questions

Half-life of one equal to the mean life of the other: 2 questions

Different masses, atomic weights or activities: 3 questions

Two decay processes in one sample: effective half-life: 5 questions

Type 4: Nuclear size and density – 12 questions

What is asked: Use the radius–mass number law to find a radius, a mass number, a volume or a density ratio, or judge a statement about nuclear density.

What to master: R = R₀A^(1/3), volume proportional to A, the same density for all nuclei (about 10¹⁷ kg m⁻³), the density compared with water, and the graph of ln R against ln A.

Forms that appear: a radius half that of A = 192, the density ratio of oxygen and helium nuclei or of calcium and oxygen, the nuclear density compared with water, an assertion about copper and carbon nuclei, the graph of ln(R/R₀) against ln A.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2022 (2), 2023 (3), 2024 (3), 2025 (2)

Which form was asked:

Radius, volume and mass number: 4 questions

Nuclear density: independent of A, ratios, order of magnitude, assertion–reason: 8 questions

Type 5: Decay series and decay graphs – 10 questions

What is asked: Count the α and β particles in a decay chain and find the final nucleus, or choose or read a graph of radioactive decay.

What to master: Mass number and charge bookkeeping with α, β⁻, β⁺ and γ, ln R against t with its slope and intercept, activity lines of several substances, and the graph of the daughter nucleus in a chain.

Forms that appear: ²³⁸U to ²⁰⁶Pb, ²³²Th after 6 α and 4 β, a chain α β⁻ γ α β⁺ γ from ²¹⁸A, ln R against t with a 40 s intercept, three activity lines A, B and C, mass of P, Q and R against time, number of B atoms against time.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (3), 2022 (2), 2023 (2), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Alpha, beta and gamma emission: the daughter nucleus and the number of particles: 5 questions

Graphs of radioactive decay: 5 questions

Type 6: Nuclear reactions: conservation laws, Q-value and sharing of energy – 9 questions

What is asked: Check a nuclear reaction for conservation of mass number, charge and momentum; match fission and fusion; or find the kinetic energy and speed of the products.

What to master: Fission products and emitted particles, kinds of reaction and the sign of Q, which process is allowed, the kinetic energy of an α-particle, the speed of fission fragments, K_p + Q > 0, and the recoil in γ emission.

Forms that appear: the correct fragments of n + ²³⁵U, the particle R in ²³⁶X → ¹⁴¹Y + ⁹²Z + 3R, matching four reactions to chemical, fission and fusion, which of four processes is allowed by momentum and energy conservation, the kinetic energy of an α-particle from A = 184 with Q = 5.5 MeV, the loss of internal energy in γ emission.

Asked in: 2020 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (1), 2024 (4), 2025 (1)

Which form was asked:

Identify the products or the kind of reaction: 4 questions

Q-value, momentum and kinetic energy of the products: 5 questions

Type 7: Nuclear forces, stability and decay: statements – 9 questions

What is asked: Choose the correct statement or assertion–reason about nuclear forces, binding energy, the decay of the neutron and the proton, or radioactivity.

What to master: Properties of the nuclear force, the constancy of binding energy per nucleon, packing fraction, beta decay of the neutron and the proton, and general statements on radioactivity.

Forms that appear: binding energy per nucleon constant for 30 < A < 170 with a short-range or long-range force as the reason, five statements on nuclear forces and packing fraction, the correct beta decay of the neutron, why a free neutron decays but a free proton does not, the statements of the semi-empirical mass formula.

Asked in: 2021 (2), 2022 (1), 2023 (3), 2024 (1), 2025 (2)

6. Suggested study order for students

Start with the decay law (Type 1) and the two-sample and two-process questions (Type 3): 43 questions (41%). Half-life arithmetic and the exponential law settle almost all of them.

Then the energy questions (Type 2): 22 questions (21%). Practise binding energy per nucleon, atomic masses and the conversion between MeV, J and kWh.

Then nuclear size and density (Type 4) and decay series and graphs (Type 5): 22 questions.

Finish with reactions and conservation laws (Type 6) and the statements (Type 7): 18 questions, and the one-off atomic physics question.

7. A routine to follow for every question

Decide which family the question belongs to: the decay law (N, A, T, λ), an energy question (binding energy, Q-value), nuclear size and density, or a statement.

For decay questions find the number of half-lives t/T first; use e^(−λt) with ln 2 = 0.693 only when the ratio is not a power of 2.

For two samples subtract the decay constants in the exponent; for two processes in one sample add them.

For energy questions write the reaction with A and Z balanced, find the mass difference or the binding-energy difference, and convert with 931.5 MeV per u.

Check units (minutes to seconds, mg to g, MeV to J to kWh) and whether the answer is a mass, a number of atoms or an activity.
