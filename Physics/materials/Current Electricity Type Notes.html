Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'Equivalent resistance between A and B' of a drawn network, or 'the combination of resistors that gives a stated value'.

• A wire is bent into a triangle, square, polygon or circle, or twelve resistors form a cube.

• A battery is joined to a network and the current or potential difference in one branch is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Resistors in series: Req and what is common

Req=R1+R2+…; the same current flows through each

Resistors in parallel: Req and what is common

1Req=1R1+1R2+…; the same potential difference across each

Two resistors in parallel; n equal resistors R in parallel

Req=R1R2R1+R2; Rn

Current division between R1 and R2 in parallel (total current I)

I1=IR2R1+R2, I2=IR1R1+R2

Potential division across R1 and R2 in series (total V)

V1=VR1R1+R2

Balanced Wheatstone network PQ=RS: the middle resistor

Carries no current; remove it (or join its ends)

Wire of total resistance R bent into a circle: across a diameter; across points dividing it in the ratio x:(1−x)

R4; x(1−x)R

Wire of total resistance R bent into a regular polygon of n sides: between adjacent corners

Network joined to a battery E (no internal resistance): total current, current in a branch

3. Concepts and skills

How do you redraw a tangled circuit?

Name every junction with a letter (a wire joins points of the same letter), redraw the resistors between the letters, then look for series and parallel groups.

When can two points be joined or a resistor be removed?

When symmetry (or a balanced bridge) gives them equal potential: no current flows between them.

A resistor has both ends connected to the same point. What is its effect?

It is short-circuited: no current flows and it can be ignored.

Which values can three equal resistors R give?

3R, R3, 3R2 (two in parallel, one in series) and 2R3 (two in series, one in parallel).

How do you find the potential at a point inside the network?

Find the branch current, then subtract IR along the path from a point of known potential.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Adding resistors in series when they are really in parallel, because of how the figure is drawn.

• Removing a resistor that does carry current (the potentials are not actually equal).

• Using the total current in the formula for the branch instead of the current division.

• Mixing up R of the whole wire with R of one side for polygon and circle problems.

3. Concepts and skills

Why must an ammeter have a small resistance and a voltmeter a large one?

The ammeter is in series and should not change the current; the voltmeter is in parallel and should not draw current.

How do you find the reading of a real meter in a circuit?

Replace the meter by its resistance, solve the circuit, and read the current through it (ammeter) or the potential difference across it (voltmeter).

Does increasing the number of turns N always increase voltage sensitivity?

No. N raises current sensitivity, but G also rises roughly with N, so NABCG may stay the same.

What is the deflection if the current is a fraction of Ig?

The deflection is the same fraction of full scale, since θ∝I.

Why is a shunt made of a low-resistance wire such as manganin?

It must stay small and keep its resistance when it heats; its value must not change with temperature.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Putting the shunt in series or the voltage resistance in parallel.

• Using I instead of I−Ig in the shunt formula.

• Forgetting to subtract G when finding the series resistance of a voltmeter.

• Ignoring the meter's own resistance when the circuit resistances are of similar size.

3. Concepts and skills

What stays constant when a wire is stretched?

The volume, so Al is fixed: if l becomes nl, the area becomes An and R becomes n2R.

How does resistance change with temperature for metals, semiconductors and alloys?

Metals: increases (α>0). Semiconductors and insulators: decreases (α<0). Alloys such as manganin and constantan: almost constant.

What happens to the resistivity when the wire is stretched?

Nothing: ρ depends only on the material and temperature, not on the shape.

How is the resistance of a conductor of varying shape found?

Take a thin slice dx of area A(x), write dR=ρdx/A(x), and integrate (series for slices along the current, parallel for slices across it).

How do you read a resistor with a changed colour?

Replace that band's digit (or multiplier) and recalculate; one step in the multiplier band changes R by a factor of 10.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using R∝l for a stretched wire (it is l2 when the volume is fixed).

• Applying RT=R0(1+αΔT) with T in kelvin when α is given per ​∘C (use the difference only).

• Taking the resistance of the pieces in parallel as R/n (it is R/n2).

• Reading the tolerance band as a digit.

• A circuit with two or more loops or batteries and 'the current in the middle branch' or 'the potential difference between two points'.

3. Concepts and skills

How should the signs be chosen in the loop rule?

Choose a direction around the loop. Going through a resistor along the current is a drop (−IR); against it a rise. Going through a cell from − to + is a rise (+E).

How do you handle an unknown current direction?

Assume one; a negative answer means the current flows the other way.

When do two cells in series give the same current as the same two in parallel?

When the external resistance equals the internal resistance of one cell (R=r).

How do you find the potential difference between two points in a multi-loop circuit?

Find the branch currents first, then add the rises and drops along any path from one point to the other.

What are an ideal ammeter and an ideal voltmeter in a circuit?

Zero resistance (a wire) and infinite resistance (an open circuit).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the emf instead of the terminal voltage across the cell.

• Wrong sign for a cell in opposition.

• Writing the loop rule with inconsistent directions for different branches.

• Forgetting that no current flows in the capacitor branch in steady state.

3. Concepts and skills

Which of two bulbs in series glows brighter?

The one with the larger resistance, i.e. the lower power rating, since P=I2R.

Which of two bulbs in parallel glows brighter?

The one with the larger power rating (smaller resistance), since P=V2R.

A heating wire is cut to half its length and used on the same supply. What happens to the heat?

R halves, so H=V2tR doubles.

Why is electric power transmitted at a high voltage?

For a given P, the current PV is smaller and the loss I2RL falls as 1V2.

How do you handle a question with changing resistance during heating?

If the temperature change is stated to matter, use RT=R0(1+αΔT); otherwise treat R as constant.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using P=V2/R for bulbs in series (the voltage is not the rated value).

• Using the marked P instead of recalculating when the voltage is different.

• Confusing maximum power transfer (R=r) with maximum efficiency (large R).

• Mixing units: watts and joules, hours and seconds.

3. Concepts and skills

Why is the null point best near the middle of the wire?

The percentage error in X is least when l=50 cm, so choose S close to X.

Why is a high-resistance protection used with the galvanometer, and removed at the end?

To protect the galvanometer from a large current while finding the approximate null point, then it is shorted for a sensitive final balance.

Why is the jockey tapped and not slid along the wire?

Sliding scrapes the wire and changes its area and resistance.

How does adding a resistor in parallel with one arm change the null point?

The effective resistance of that arm falls, so the null point shifts towards the side of that arm.

How do you handle a bridge that is not balanced?

Apply Kirchhoff's laws or the potential-divider method to the two arms and take the potential difference across the galvanometer.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using l for the wrong side (which gap holds X).

• Forgetting to replace S by the combined resistance when a resistor is added in parallel.

• Putting l in metres in a formula that uses 100 cm.

• Applying the balance condition when the question says the bridge is not balanced.

3. Concepts and skills

Why is a potentiometer better than a voltmeter for measuring an emf?

At balance it draws no current from the cell, so it measures the true emf (an ideal voltmeter of infinite resistance).

How can the sensitivity of a potentiometer be increased?

Decrease the potential gradient k: use a longer wire or add a series resistance, so a small change in emf shifts the balance point more.

What happens to the balance length if the driver current is increased?

k increases, so the balance length for the same emf decreases.

Why must the driver cell's emf be larger than the emf to be measured?

The potential drop across the wire must cover the emf, otherwise no balance point exists on the wire.

How is the new balance length found when a resistance is added in series with the wire?

Find the new k, then l=Ek; or use the ratio of the gradients.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using l2 and l1 the wrong way round in r=Rl1−l2l2.

• Using the emf of the driver cell as the potential drop across the wire (the series resistance and the internal resistance take part).

• Taking the balance length in metres in one place and centimetres in another.

• Forgetting the shunt resistance R is across the cell being tested, not across the wire.

• A current such as I=3t2+4t3 is given and the charge in a time interval is asked, or a Q(t) is given.

• A copper or other wire with electron density n, area A and drift speed vd is described.

• 'Mobility', 'current density', 'relaxation time' or 'how does the drift velocity change' appear.

3. Concepts and skills

Typical size of the drift velocity?

About 10−4m s−1 for ordinary currents in copper, much smaller than the speed of the electrons between collisions.

What happens to vd when the temperature rises?

The relaxation time τ falls (more collisions), so vd falls for the same field.

Same current through a wire whose area changes along it: what changes?

I is the same; J=IA and vd=InAe are larger where the area is smaller.

How do you find the charge when the current is given as a function of time?

Integrate between the limits; for a maximum charge look for I=0 in Q(t) (dQdt=0).

How is the direction of current related to the motion of electrons?

Conventional current is opposite to the electron drift; J and E are in the same direction.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Forgetting that e is the magnitude of the electron charge when computing I.

• Using the cross-section A of the wire in a problem where A varies with r (integrate).

• Taking the thermal speed as the drift speed.

• Mixing up J (current per unit area) with I.

3. Concepts and skills

How do I attack a one-off question?

Identify the two ideas involved, write the standard formula of each, and connect them through the quantity they share.

Can a capacitor carry a current?

No charge crosses the gap, but a changing electric field gives a displacement current that equals the conduction current in the wires.
