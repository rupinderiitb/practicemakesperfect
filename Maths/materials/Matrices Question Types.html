JEE Main Maths – Matrices

Types of questions asked in JEE Main 2019–2025 (153 questions)

Key message: these 153 questions fall into only 7 repeating types. The three biggest types (Types 1, 2, 3) account for 94 questions (61%). Adjoint and determinant chains alone (Type 1) are 44 questions (29%). About 37% of the questions ask for a numerical (integer) answer, so there are no options to guess from.

How to read this: a “type” is the formula, law or technique that decides the answer. So every type below tells you what the question looks like and which formula or method to use.

3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Adjoint and determinant chains – 44 questions

What is asked: Find the determinant (or a power of it) of an expression built from adj, scalar multiples and inverses of a 3×3 matrix whose determinant is given, or find A from its adjoint.

What to master: |adj A| = |A|ⁿ⁻¹; |kA| = kⁿ|A|; adj(adj A) = |A|ⁿ⁻²A; |A⁻¹| = 1/|A|; adj(AB) = adj B · adj A; A·adj A = |A|I; prime factorisation of the result.

Forms that appear: |adj(adj(2A))| with |A| given, |3 adj(3A·A²)|, |adj(A adj(2A))| = 2ᵅ3ᵝ5ᵞ, adj A given and |A| asked, n times nested adjoint and a remainder, cofactors with logarithm entries.

Asked in: 2020 (2), 2021 (5), 2022 (9), 2023 (11), 2024 (8), 2025 (9)

Which form was asked:

Nested adjoint and scalar multiples: |adj(adj(kA))|, |k·adj(mA)| with |A| given: 27 questions

Adjoint or cofactor of an explicit matrix, and adjoint properties: 17 questions

Type 2: Powers of a matrix, Aⁿ – 29 questions

What is asked: Find Aⁿ, a sum A + A² + … + Aᵐ, or a sum of its entries, by splitting A = I + N or by finding the pattern or the period.

What to master: Binomial theorem for (I + N)ⁿ; nilpotent N; rotation matrices R(θ)ⁿ = R(nθ); periodicity (A³ = I, A⁴ = I); entries with ω and i.

Forms that appear: A = [[1,0],[1/2,1]] and P⁵⁰, A = [[1,a,a],[0,1,b],[0,0,1]] and Aⁿ given, A = [[cos θ, −sin θ],[sin θ, cos θ]] and A³², A²⁰²⁵ − A²⁰²⁰, sum of entries of A + A² + … + A²⁰.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (4), 2021 (7), 2022 (6), 2023 (5), 2024 (1), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Powers of a matrix: write A = I + N (N nilpotent), triangular and pattern matrices: 15 questions

Powers of rotation, cyclic and complex-entry matrices: 14 questions

Type 3: Inverse, orthogonality and matrix-equation problems – 21 questions

What is asked: Use a given relation such as AAᵀ = I, AB = B, A⁵ = B⁵ or AX = λX to find an unknown, a determinant or the inverse of an expression.

What to master: AAᵀ = I (rows are unit vectors); A⁻¹ = adj A/|A|; (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹; invertible ⇔ |A| ≠ 0; AB = B ⇒ (A − I)B = O; AX = λX for a non-zero X.

Forms that appear: AAᵀ = I₃ and |p| asked, B = adj A and |adj B|/|C|, A⁻⁵⁰ for a rotation matrix, f(x) with f(−x) = f(x)⁻¹, invertibility of a matrix with e^t, sin t, cos t entries, AX = λX with A = I − 2MMᵀ.

Asked in: 2019 (5), 2020 (2), 2021 (7), 2023 (2), 2024 (3), 2025 (2)

Type 4: Counting matrices with entries from a given set – 19 questions

What is asked: Count the matrices of a given order with entries from a small set that satisfy a condition on their entries (sum, determinant, A = A⁻¹, singular, symmetric).

What to master: Multiplication principle; counting solutions of a sum condition with restricted values; tr(AAᵀ) = Σaᵢⱼ²; the number of symmetric matrices of order n is kⁿ⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾ᐟ²; checking a determinant condition by cases.

Forms that appear: singular matrices of order 2 with entries from {2,3,6,9}, 3×3 matrices with entries from {−1,0,1} and tr(AᵀA) = 6, sum of all entries equal to a prime, matrices with det = 15, A = A⁻¹, AB = BA with entries from {1,…,5}.

Asked in: 2020 (1), 2021 (5), 2022 (7), 2023 (3), 2024 (1), 2025 (2)

Type 5: Cayley–Hamilton and matrix polynomial equations – 18 questions

What is asked: Use A² − (tr A)A + |A|I = O (or a given polynomial relation) to find unknown constants, a higher power or the inverse of A.

What to master: Cayley–Hamilton theorem; reducing Aⁿ with the characteristic relation; comparing coefficients; A⁻¹ = (1/|A|)((tr A)I − A) for a 2×2 matrix.

Forms that appear: A² = 3A + αI and A⁴ = 21A + βI, A⁻¹ = αA + βI, A³ = 4A² − A − 21I, A² − A + I = O type relations, A⁵ = αA² + βA + γI, (I + A)⁸ for a matrix with |A| = 0.

Asked in: 2020 (1), 2021 (3), 2022 (3), 2023 (5), 2024 (3), 2025 (3)

Type 6: Basic operations, trace and unknown entries – 15 questions

What is asked: Add, multiply and take the trace of matrices, apply an elementary operation, or find unknown entries from the given matrix relations.

What to master: Row-by-column multiplication; equality of matrices; tr(A) = sum of diagonal entries; tr(AB) = tr(BA); elementary row operations; |AB| = |A||B|.

Forms that appear: which matrix cannot be obtained by a single row operation, the values of a₂₃ from three matrix equations, A + 2B and 2A − B given and Tr(A) − Tr(B), 2×2 matrices with entries from {0,1}, XᵀAᵏX = 33, det X for C = ABAᵀ.

Asked in: 2020 (1), 2021 (3), 2022 (3), 2023 (1), 2024 (5), 2025 (2)

Type 7: Symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices – 7 questions

What is asked: Decide whether an expression built from a symmetric and a skew-symmetric matrix is symmetric or skew-symmetric, or split a matrix into its symmetric and skew-symmetric parts.

What to master: Aᵀ = A and Aᵀ = −A; (AB)ᵀ = BᵀAᵀ; A = ½(A + Aᵀ) + ½(A − Aᵀ); diagonal of a skew-symmetric matrix is zero; Aᵐ is symmetric or skew-symmetric according to m.

Forms that appear: A symmetric and B skew-symmetric with A¹³B²⁶ − B²⁶A¹³, which of AB − BA, AB + BA, A⁴ − B⁴ is not true, A + B given and AB asked, number of symmetric matrices of order 2 with Σaᵢⱼ² = 1.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (2), 2023 (2)

5. Suggested study order for students

Start with adjoint and determinant chains (Type 1): 44 questions (29%). Master |adj A|, |kA| and adj(adj A) for 3×3 matrices.

Then powers of a matrix and inverse or matrix-equation problems (Types 2 and 3): 50 questions (33%).

Then counting matrices, Cayley–Hamilton, basic operations and symmetric matrices (Types 4, 5, 6 and 7): 59 questions.

6. A routine to follow for every question

Read the order of the matrix (2×2 or 3×3) and write down what is given: |A|, a relation such as A² = αA + βI, or the set the entries come from.

Adjoint chains are the largest block: reduce them with |adj A| = |A|ⁿ⁻¹ and |kA| = kⁿ|A| from the inside outwards.

For powers of a matrix, look for A = I + N, a rotation or a period before multiplying out.

For 2×2 matrices think of the Cayley–Hamilton relation first; for counting questions fix the structure and count cases.

Check the answer against the options by taking the determinant or the trace, which are quick tests for most matrix identities.
