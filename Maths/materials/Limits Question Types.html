3. Type-by-type detail

Type 1: Exponential, logarithmic and 1^∞ limits, and limits at infinity – 23 questions

What is asked: A limit of the form 1^∞, an exponential or logarithmic expansion, or a limit as x or n → ∞ is to be evaluated.

What to master: (1 + u)^v → e^{lim uv}; (e^x − 1)/x → 1; ln(1 + x)/x → 1; divide by the highest power at infinity; rationalise radicals.

Forms that appear: (tan(π/4 + x))^{1/x}, (cos x)^{cot x}, (2 − cos x)/(cos 2x))^{(x+2)/x²}, lim (√(x² − x + 1) − ax) = b, sums and products as n → ∞.

Asked in: 2020 (4), 2021 (5), 2022 (1), 2023 (4), 2024 (5), 2025 (4)

Which form was asked:

1^∞ forms and exponential-power limits: 9 questions

Exponential, logarithmic and inverse-trigonometric limits by expansion: 5 questions

Limits at infinity: sums, products and radicals: 9 questions

Type 2: Standard trigonometric and inverse-trigonometric limits – 21 questions

What is asked: A ratio of trigonometric (or inverse-trigonometric) functions at a point is to be evaluated.

What to master: sin x/x, tan x/x → 1; (1 − cos x)/x² → ½; substitute x = a + h; use sin(2x) and cos(2x) identities; rationalise radicals.

Forms that appear: sin²(π cos⁴x)/x⁴, tan(π cos²θ)/sin(2π sin²θ), (cot³x − tan x)/cos(x + π/4) at π/4, (x² − 1) sin²(πx)/(x⁴ − 2x³ + 2x − 1).

Asked in: 2019 (4), 2020 (1), 2021 (5), 2022 (5), 2023 (1), 2024 (2), 2025 (3)

Which form was asked:

Direct trigonometric ratios and 1 − cos forms: 13 questions

Trigonometric limits with radicals and products: 5 questions

Mixed standard limits (sin, tan, log, e together): 3 questions

Type 3: Finding constants from a finite-limit condition – 18 questions

What is asked: A limit equal to a given number (or finite) fixes unknown constants a, b, c, α, β, γ, λ, μ; a combination of them is asked.

What to master: For a finite 0/0 limit, every coefficient of x^k below the order of the denominator must vanish; the leading one equals the given value.

Forms that appear: (cos 2x + a cos 4x − b)/x⁴ finite, (a x² eˣ − b ln(1 + x) + c x e⁻ˣ)/(x² sin x) = 1, (x−1)(6 + λ cos(x−1)) + μ sin(1−x) over (x−1)³ = −1.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (1), 2021 (4), 2022 (4), 2023 (2), 2024 (3), 2025 (3)

Type 4: Functional and derivative-based limits (f differentiable, integrals) – 18 questions

What is asked: A limit involves an unknown differentiable function f (values f(a), f′(a) given) or an integral with a variable limit.

What to master: f′(a) = lim (f(x) − f(a))/(x − a); L'Hôpital with Leibniz: d/dx ∫ g = g(h(x)) h′(x) − g(k(x)) k′(x).

Forms that appear: lim (x² f(2) − 4 f(x))/(x − 2) with f(2) = 4, f′(2) = 1; lim (x f(a) − a f(x))/(x − a); lim (1/(x − π/2)²) ∫ cos(t^{1/3}) dt; lim f(x)/x³ for f = ∫(t + sin(1 − eᵗ))dt.

Asked in: 2019 (1), 2020 (2), 2021 (3), 2022 (1), 2024 (9), 2025 (2)

Type 5: Greatest integer, fractional part, piecewise functions and existence of a limit – 15 questions

What is asked: A limit containing [x], {x} or a piecewise definition is to be found, or the values for which it exists are asked.

What to master: Evaluate [x] separately for x → a⁻ and x → a⁺; a limit exists only if both one-sided limits are equal.

Forms that appear: lim x([x] + x) sin[x]/x at 0⁻, the set of a for which lim ([x − 5] − [2x + 2]) = 0, integer a with lim (18 − [1 − x])/[x − 3a] at 7 existing.

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (1), 2021 (2), 2022 (3), 2023 (3), 2024 (2), 2025 (1)

Type 6: Algebraic limits: factorisation and rationalisation – 5 questions

What is asked: A ratio of powers, roots or polynomials gives 0/0 at a point and is evaluated by factorising or rationalising.

What to master: (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → n aⁿ⁻¹; multiply by the conjugate to remove a square root; use fractional powers as roots.

Forms that appear: (x⁴ − 1)/(x − 1) = (xᵏ³ − k³)/(x² − k²) to find k, ((a + 2x)^{1/3} − (3x)^{1/3})/((3a + x)^{1/3} − (4x)^{1/3}).

Asked in: 2019 (3), 2020 (1), 2024 (1)

5. Suggested study order for students

Start with the exponential, logarithmic and trigonometric limits (Types 1 and 2): 44 questions (44%). Master the standard limits and the e^{lim uv} rule.

Then finding constants and functional limits (Types 3 and 4): 36 questions (36%).

Then greatest-integer and algebraic limits (Types 5 and 6): 20 questions.

6. A routine to follow for every question

Substitute the limit point: if the form is 0/0, ∞/∞ or 1^∞, a standard method is needed.

Pick the method: factorise or rationalise, use a standard limit, expand in a series, or L'Hôpital.

For a 1^∞ form, write the limit as e^{lim uv}; for x → ∞, divide by the highest power.

With [x] or a piecewise definition, find the left and right limits separately.

Check the answer by plugging in a small value (for example x = 0.01) in your calculator.
