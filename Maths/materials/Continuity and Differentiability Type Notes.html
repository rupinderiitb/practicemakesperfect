Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'The number of points where f(x)=|x2−2x−3|e… (or max{x,x2}) is not differentiable'.

• The function mixes |⋅|, max, min or [⋅], sometimes with a composition such as f(f(x)).

• The answer is a number of points, or a set such as {5,10,15}.

2. Formulae and laws

Corner

f is not differentiable at a if f′(a−)≠f′(a+) (both finite)

Modulus

|g| has a corner where g=0 and g′≠0; no corner if g has a repeated root

Max / min

max{f,g} switches where f=g; a corner if f′≠g′ there

Greatest integer

[x] is constant between integers and jumps at each integer

3. Concepts and skills

How do I count the corners of a modulus function?

List the zeros of every modulus expression, write f on each interval and compare the one-sided slopes at each zero.

When do two modulus kinks cancel?

If their slope changes at a common zero are opposite (for example −3|x+2| and |(x+2)(x−1)| near −2), there is no corner.

How do I handle max, min or [x]?

Sketch the graphs and mark the crossing points; a jump of [x] is not differentiable, so count it as well.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Counting a zero of |g| where g has a double root.

• Forgetting that two modulus kinks can cancel.

• Counting points outside the open interval asked.

Completed notes – Type 2

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'If f is continuous at x=0, then a+2b (or 6a+b2, a2, k) is equal to'.

• The left branch is a ratio such as sin(a+2)x+sinxx and the right branch a power or radical.

• The value at the junction is a constant such as b, k or α.

2. Formulae and laws

Continuity

limx→a−f=limx→a+f=f(a)

Standard limits

sinxx→1,1−cosxx2→12,log(1+x)x→1,ex−1x→1

1∞ form

(1+kx)1/x→ek

Radicals

(1+h)n−1≈nh for small h

3. Concepts and skills

How do I set up the equations?

Compute the left limit, the right limit and f(a); equate them in pairs and solve for the constants.

What if a limit is 00?

Use the standard limits, a series expansion or rationalisation until the zero cancels.

How do I do a 1∞ limit?

Write (1+u)v=evln(1+u)≈euv with u→0 and take limuv.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using only one side of the junction.

• Expanding sin(a+2)x as sin(a+2)⋅x.

• Reading the denominator power wrongly (for example x4/3 against x1/3).

• Forgetting to equate with the value f(a).

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'The number of points of discontinuity of f(x)=[x22]−[x], x∈[0,4]'.

• A piecewise function with [4x2−1] or [x] in a branch and 'is discontinuous at exactly …'.

• A composition f∘g of two piecewise functions is discontinuous at …

2. Formulae and laws

Jump of [g]

[g(x)] jumps where g(x) is an integer and g crosses it

Flat touch

If g only touches an integer (a minimum), [g] has no jump there

Composition

(f∘g)(x)=f(g(x)): use the range of g on each piece

Value test

A removable break: f(a−)=f(a+)≠f(a) still counts as discontinuous

3. Concepts and skills

How do I find the jump points of [x2/2]?

Solve x22=k for integer k inside the interval and list the roots.

How do I handle a composite?

Find the range of g on each piece, plug into the right branch of f, then test the joins and the points where g hits a break point of f.

What about the junctions of the branches?

Compare the left limit, right limit and the value at every junction separately.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Counting the same point twice from two terms.

• Missing end points that lie in the closed interval.

• Treating a touching point of [g] as a jump.

• Using the wrong branch of the outer function.

Completed notes – Type 4

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'If f is differentiable on R, then 48(a+b) (or a−b, ∫f) is equal to'.

• A piecewise function has unknown a,b,c and a condition such as f′(0)+f′(2)=e.

• A linear g(x) in one branch is determined from continuity at 0 and an extra condition.

2. Formulae and laws

Continuity at a

f(a−)=f(a+)=f(a)

Differentiability at a

continuity and f′(a−)=f′(a+)

Two junctions

Write two equations at each junction and solve all together

Derivative of a branch

Differentiate each branch separately before matching

3. Concepts and skills

How many equations do I get?

One for continuity and one for the derivatives at every junction; count them against the number of unknowns.

Why match the derivatives of the branch formulas?

Differentiability needs the one-sided derivatives equal, and they equal the derivatives of the two branch formulas at the junction.

How do I use an extra condition like f′(1)=f(−1)?

Substitute the branch formulas at the stated points into the condition.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Matching only the values and forgetting the derivatives.

• Using the wrong branch for f′(2) or f(−1).

• Not checking the rejected root (for example a>1).

• Errors in differentiating e−x or 1x.

Completed notes – Type 5

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'Which of the following is true?': f(x)=∫0x[y]dy or x2sin1x at 0.

• 'Then f is continuous on R−{1} and differentiable on R−{−1,1}'.

• 'Which statement is NOT true' for a function with an integral branch.

2. Formulae and laws

Definition

f′(a)=limh→0f(a+h)−f(a)h (both sides)

Integral

ddx∫0xg=g(x) where g is continuous

Squeeze

|x2sin1x|≤x2, so x2sin1x→0

Absolute value

|x| is not differentiable at 0

3. Concepts and skills

How do I test an option?

Find the one-sided limits and derivatives at the stated points and compare them with the option.

How do I use an integral definition?

Differentiate under the integral: f′(x) is the integrand, so f′ is discontinuous where the integrand is.

How do I find increasing intervals?

Look at the sign of f′ on each branch separately.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Assuming continuous implies differentiable.

• Testing only the junction and not the open pieces.

• Reading 'NOT true' as 'true'.

• Using [x] at an integer without the one-sided values.

Completed notes – Type 6

1. How do I spot this type?

• A functional equation such as f(x+y)=f(x)f(y) is given with f′(0)=3 or f′(1)=2024.

• |f(x)−f(y)|≤k|x−y|p with p>1 and f(0) is given.

• A relation like f″=g″+6x or a limit-defined f with f(1)=1.

2. Formulae and laws

Derivative

f′(x)=limh→0f(x+h)−f(x)h

Exponential type

f(x+y)=f(x)f(y)⇒f′(x)=f′(0)f(x)

Lipschitz power

|f(x)−f(y)|≤k|x−y|p,p>1⇒f′≡0

Limit of an increment

limf(h)−f(0)h=f′(0)

3. Concepts and skills

How do I start a functional equation?

Put y=h, form the difference quotient and let h→0 to get f′ in terms of f.

Why is f constant for p>1?

Dividing by |x−y| gives |f′(x)|≤limk|h|p−1=0.

How do I use f″=g″+6x?

Integrate twice for f−g and fix the constants from the given values.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Assuming f(0)=0 for f(x+y)=f(x)f(y) without checking.

• Forgetting the case p>1 is needed for f′=0.

• Dropping a constant of integration.

• Using the wrong point for the given value.
