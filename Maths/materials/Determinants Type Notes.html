How to use these notes

1. Do these pages BEFORE the practice sheet, one type at a time.

2. Close all books and notes. Fill the blanks from memory; leave a gap if you cannot recall.

3. Then press “Show completed notes” under that type, correct your answers in a different colour (or your own notes), and note what you missed.

4. Now do that type's questions on the sheet. Come back to the notes after the sheet to add anything new.

5. Re-fill the same page a week later with the prompts covered – this is what makes it stick.

  • Tap a box and use the bar at the bottom of the screen.

Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• A 3×3 system with a parameter λ, μ, α or k is given and the question says 'no solution', 'unique solution', 'inconsistent' or 'consistent for all'.

• The options are statements such as 'infinitely many solutions if α=1,β=1' and you must pick the one that is NOT correct.

• The answer is the number of values of the parameter, or the set of values, for which the system fails.

2. Formulae and laws

Cramer's rule

x=ΔxΔ,y=ΔyΔ,z=ΔzΔ

Unique solution

Δ≠0

No solution

Δ=0 and at least one of Δx,Δy,Δz≠0 (or an inconsistent row after reduction)

Infinitely many solutions

Δ=Δx=Δy=Δz=0 and the equations are consistent (rank of A = rank of [A|B]<3)

Parallel planes

Two equations with proportional coefficients but different constants give no solution

3. Concepts and skills

How do I find the special values of the parameter?

Expand Δ as a function of the parameter and solve Δ=0; only those values need checking.

At a special value, how do I decide between no solution and infinitely many?

Reduce the augmented matrix: a row 0=c with c≠0 means no solution; if the third equation is a combination of the other two, there are infinitely many.

How do I handle a 'NOT correct' question?

Test each option by substituting its values into the system and reducing; the option that gives the opposite nature is the answer.

What if Δ=0 for every value of the parameter?

Then compare the ranks directly by row reduction instead of using Cramer's rule.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking Δ=0 as 'no solution' without checking Δx,Δy,Δz or the rank.

• Missing a special value of the parameter that makes a coefficient zero.

• Counting a value of the parameter that gives infinitely many solutions when the question asks for no solution.

• Sign errors while expanding Δ.

Completed notes – Type 2

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'If the system … has infinitely many solutions, then λ+μ (or 2a+3b, 13αβ) is equal to …'.

• Two unknown constants sit in the coefficients and the constants of the system.

• The answer is a number, an ordered pair (λ,μ) or a quadratic having α,β as roots.

2. Formulae and laws

Condition

Δ=0 and Δx=Δy=Δz=0

Combination method

Third equation =p×(first) +q×(second); compare coefficients and constants

Elimination

Eliminate one variable from two pairs of equations and force the two new equations to be identical

Rank

rank(A)= rank[A|B]<3

3. Concepts and skills

Which method is quickest?

If two equations have small coefficients, write the third as pE1+qE2 and match the three coefficients to find p,q, then the constant.

Why do I need Δ=0 and the constants too?

Δ=0 alone gives only the parameter in the coefficients; the constant must also fit for the system to be consistent.

How do I find a parameter that appears in the coefficient and the constant?

First use Δ=0 for the coefficient parameter, then use one of Δx,Δy,Δz=0 for the constant.

What if the question asks for roots of a quadratic?

Find the two parameters, then compute their sum and product.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using only Δ=0 and forgetting the condition on the constants.

• Matching coefficients with an arithmetic slip when the numbers are large.

• Evaluating the asked combination (such as 2a+3b) with the wrong signs.

• Confusing 'infinitely many' with 'no solution' when Δ=0.

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• All the right-hand sides are 0 and the system 'has a non-trivial (non-zero) solution'.

• A row vector times a matrix equals (000), or x(…)+y(…)+z(…)=(0,0,0) with xyz≠0.

• The entries involve sinα,cosα, sin3θ or a symmetric pattern, and the number of values or their sum is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Non-trivial solution

Δ=0

Trivial solution only

Δ≠0

Ratios

Solve two equations for x:y:z using cross products of the coefficients

Trig reduction

C1→C1+C2+C3 or Ri→Ri−Rj to factor out a common expression

3. Concepts and skills

Why is Δ=0 the condition?

A homogeneous system always has x=y=z=0; a non-zero solution exists only when the equations are dependent.

How do I find x:y:z?

Take any two equations and solve for two variables in terms of the third, then substitute in the asked expression.

How do I count the values of θ in an interval?

Simplify Δ=0 to a product of simple trigonometric factors and count the roots of each factor in the interval, without repeats.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Stopping at Δ=0 without checking the roots against the given interval.

• Counting a repeated root of two factors twice.

• Using the three equations that are dependent when solving for the ratios.

• Forgetting that xyz≠0 rules out some ratios.

Completed notes – Type 4

1. How do I spot this type?

• After the parameters are found, the question asks the distance of (λ,μ,…) from a plane or the radius of a circle centred at (λ,μ).

• A system is given as (A−kI)X=B, or A is described through AX=λX for given vectors.

• The question counts integer solutions with a bound such as 15≤x2+y2+z2≤150.

2. Formulae and laws

Distance from a plane

|ax1+by1+cz1+d|a2+b2+c2

Distance from a line

|ax1+by1+c|a2+b2 (radius of a circle touching the line)

Collinear points

∣x1y11x2y21x3y31∣=0

Eigen-type relation

AX=λX⇒(A−λI)X=O

3. Concepts and skills

What do I do first?

Solve the linear-system part to get the numbers, then switch to the geometry or counting part.

How are integer solutions counted?

Write the general solution with one parameter t, substitute in the inequality and count the integer values of t.

How do I decide the solutions of (A−kI)X=B?

If |A−kI|≠0 the solution is unique; if =0 check the consistency with B.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the wrong plane (for example the plane with the constant moved to the wrong side).

• Forgetting to square-root the denominator in the distance formula.

• Counting integer values of the parameter without checking the bound at both ends.

• Misreading the eigenvalue from AX=λX.

Completed notes – Type 5

1. How do I spot this type?

• A 3×3 determinant with entries in A.P., with x, a, b, c, or with ar=cos2rπ9+isin2rπ9 is given.

• The determinant equals a product such as (a+b+c)(x+a+b+c)2 and x is asked.

• 'The sum of the real roots of Δ=0' or 'a value of θ for which Δ=0'.

2. Formulae and laws

Row operation

Ri→Ri+kRj does not change Δ

Common factor

A common factor of a row or column can be taken outside Δ

Zero determinant

Two equal or proportional rows (or columns) give Δ=0

Factor theorem

If Δ=0 at x=a, then (x−a) is a factor

Roots of unity

ω3=1, 1+ω+ω2=0; sum of ar over a full cycle is 0

3. Concepts and skills

How do I simplify quickly?

Add all columns into one to take out a common factor like (a+b+c), then subtract rows to create zeros.

How do I find the factors without expanding?

Substitute values that make two rows equal; each such value gives a factor, and the degree fixes the constant.

What about determinants with ar in a cycle?

Use ar+9=ar and a1+a2+…+a9=0 to create zeros.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Changing the determinant while using Ri→kRi+Rj (the value is multiplied by k).

• Missing a sign when expanding along the second row.

• Forgetting a root, or counting a root outside the allowed interval.

• Treating the constant factor as 1 after the factor theorem.

Completed notes – Type 6

1. How do I spot this type?

• f(x) is a 3×3 determinant with entries like 1+sin2x, cos2x, sin2x, and its maximum and minimum are asked.

• f′(0), f(0) or a limit of a determinant is asked.

• A determinant Dk depends on k and ∑Dk is given.

2. Formulae and laws

Reduction

C1→C1+C2+C3 (or R2−R1, R3−R1) gives a simple function

Ranges

−1≤sinx≤1, sin2x=2sinxcosx, sin2x+cos2x=1

Derivative of a determinant

f′(x)= sum of the determinants with one row differentiated at a time

Summation

∑k=n(n+1)2, ∑k2=n(n+1)(2n+1)6

Cubic extremes

Local maximum and minimum at the roots of f′(x)=0

3. Concepts and skills

How do I find the maximum of a trigonometric determinant?

Reduce it to a simple function of sin2x or cos2x first, then use the range of that function on the given interval.

How do I find f′(0) without expanding fully?

Expand to a polynomial in x, or use the rule of differentiating one row at a time.

How are local extremes of a cubic found?

Solve f′(x)=0, check the sign change of f′, and substitute in f.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the range of sinx when the argument is restricted to an interval.

• Taking the maximum at an end of the interval without checking the turning point.

• Differentiating all rows together.

• Adding the global instead of the local extreme values.

Completed notes – Type 7

1. How do I spot this type?

• A matrix B is obtained from 2A by a row operation such as R2→2R2+5R3 and detB is asked.

• det(ABAT) and det(AB−1) are given and another determinant is asked.

• Entries are factorials p!,(p+1)!, greatest integers [x+1] or logarithms logea1ra2k.

2. Formulae and laws

Scalar multiple

|kA|=kn|A| (for 3×3: k3|A|)

Product and transpose

|AB|=|A||B|, |AT|=|A|, |A−1|=1|A|

Row operation

Ri→aRi+bRj multiplies Δ by a

Factorials

(p+1)!=(p+1)p!

Greatest integer

[x+n]=[x]+n for an integer n

3. Concepts and skills

How do I use the given determinants of products?

Write each as a product of |A| and |B|, solve for |A| and |B|, then evaluate the asked product.

How do I handle factorial entries?

Take p!, (p+1)! ... out of the rows to leave small integers, then factorise the result to see the powers of p and p+2.

How do I handle [x]?

Write [x+k]=[x]+k, let [x]=m and solve the determinant equation for m.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using |kA|=k|A|.

• Forgetting that R2→2R2+5R3 multiplies the determinant by 2.

• Taking |AT|=−|A|.

• Taking log(ab) as loga⋅logb.
