2. Close all books and notes. Fill the blanks from memory; leave a gap if you cannot recall.

3. Then press “Show completed notes” under that type, correct your answers in a different colour (or your own notes), and note what you missed.

5. Re-fill the same page a week later with the prompts covered – this is what makes it stick.

Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• The words local maximum, local minimum, critical point or point of extremum appear, or the maximum and minimum values on an interval [a,b] are asked.

• A function with |x|, [x] or a fractional power such as x2/3 is given and the number of extrema is asked.

• A parameter has to be found so that the function has a maximum or minimum at a given point.

2. Formulae and laws

Critical points

f′(c)=0 or f′(c) does not exist

First derivative test

f′ changes from + to − at c: maximum; from − to +: minimum

Second derivative test

f′(c)=0,f″(c)<0⇒ maximum; f″(c)>0⇒ minimum

Absolute extrema on [a,b]

compare f at the critical points inside (a,b) and at a and b

Cubic ax3+bx2+cx+d

extrema exist when b2−3ac>0

Greatest term of a sequence

treat n as a real variable x and find the maximum of f(x), then compare the nearest integers

3. Concepts and skills

What is the first step in every extremum question?

Differentiate and solve f′(x)=0; also list the points where f′ is not defined.

How do I find the extrema of a function with |x| or [x]?

Split the domain at the points where the expression inside changes sign or the integer part jumps, and study each piece.

How do I get the absolute maximum on a closed interval?

Evaluate f at every critical point inside the interval and at both end points; the largest is the absolute maximum.

A function has a maximum at x=a; how do I find the parameter?

Use f′(a)=0 and check the sign of f″(a) or the sign change of f′.

How do I find the greatest term of a sequence?

Differentiate the function of x obtained by writing n=x and compare the integers on either side of the critical point.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Ignoring the end points when asked for the absolute maximum or minimum.

• Forgetting points where f′ does not exist (such as x=0 for x2/3).

• Using the second derivative test when f″(c)=0.

• Taking a critical point that lies outside the given interval.

Completed notes – Type 2

1. How do I spot this type?

• The tangent or normal at a point on a curve is asked, or a point lying on it.

• The tangent is parallel or perpendicular to a given line, passes through a given point, or two curves cut at right angles.

• The area of the triangle formed by the tangent, the normal and an axis is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Slope of tangent

m=dydx at the point; for x=x(t),y=y(t): m=dy/dtdx/dt

Tangent

y−y1=m(x−x1)

Normal

y−y1=−1m(x−x1)

Tangent parallel to x-axis

dydx=0; parallel to y-axis: dxdy=0

Angle between two curves

tanθ=|m1−m21+m1m2|; orthogonal if m1m2=−1

Tangent from an outside point

let the point of contact be (t,f(t)) and put the given point in the tangent

3. Concepts and skills

How do I find the slope when the curve is implicit?

Differentiate both sides with respect to x and solve for dydx.

A tangent passes through a point that is not on the curve; what do I do?

Take the point of contact (t,f(t)), write the tangent there and substitute the given point to find t.

How do I find where the tangent meets the curve again?

Solve the curve and the tangent together; the point of contact is a repeated root.

How do I know that two curves cut at right angles?

Find the point of intersection and check that the product of the slopes is −1.

What if the tangent is parallel to the chord joining two points?

Equate dydx to the slope of the chord y2−y1x2−x1.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the slope of the tangent when the normal is asked (and the reverse).

• Substituting the given point in dydx when it does not lie on the curve.

• Dropping a sign in implicit differentiation.

• Not checking that the point of contact lies on the curve.

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• The question says that f is increasing or decreasing (strictly or non-strictly) in an interval, or asks for such an interval.

• A parameter a or λ is to be found so that the function is increasing for all real x.

• A function such as g(x)=f(x)+f(c−x) with f″>0 is given and its monotonic intervals are asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Increasing

f′(x)≥0 on the interval; strictly increasing if f′(x)>0

Decreasing

f′(x)≤0 on the interval

Quadratic ax2+bx+c non-negative for all x

a>0 and b2−4ac≤0

Composite function

ddxf(g(x))=f′(g(x))g′(x)

Symmetric function g(x)=f(x)+f(c−x)

g′(x)=f′(x)−f′(c−x), zero at x=c2

Convex function

f″>0⇒f′ is increasing

3. Concepts and skills

How do I find the interval of increase?

Factorise f′(x), mark its zeros on a number line and read the sign in each interval.

What does ‘increasing for all x’ mean for a parameter?

f′(x)≥0 for all x; use the discriminant of f′ or the minimum value of f′.

How do I handle |x| or piecewise functions?

Differentiate each piece, and check the behaviour at the joining point separately.

How do I treat the function f(x)=xx?

Take logarithms: lnf=xlnx, so f′f=lnx+1.

Why must I check the domain?

A function can only be increasing inside its domain; for ln or square roots exclude the points where it is not defined.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Reading the sign of f′ wrongly in the intervals around a repeated root.

• Forgetting the domain, for example x≠±1 for ln|x−1x+1|.

• Writing a closed interval where the function is not defined at the end.

• Using f′(x)>0 when the question allows f′(x)=0 at isolated points.

Completed notes – Type 4

1. How do I spot this type?

• The words Rolle's theorem or Lagrange's mean value theorem appear, or a tangent is parallel to the chord.

• A function is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b) and values or bounds on f′ are given.

• The minimum number of zeros of f′ or f″ is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Rolle's theorem

f(a)=f(b)⇒f′(c)=0 for some c∈(a,b)

Lagrange's mean value theorem

f′(c)=f(b)−f(a)b−a

Bound from f′(x)≤k

f(b)−f(a)≤k(b−a)

Zeros

n zeros of f give at least n−1 zeros of f′ and n−2 zeros of f″

Product rule form

(fg′)′=f′g′+fg″, (f3)′=3f2f′

3. Concepts and skills

How do I check that the theorem applies?

The function must be continuous on the closed interval and differentiable in the open interval; for Rolle also f(a)=f(b).

How do I find c?

Solve f′(c)=f(b)−f(a)b−a and keep the root that lies in (a,b).

How do I count zeros of f″?

Use Rolle between consecutive zeros of f to get zeros of f′, and again between consecutive zeros of f′.

What if the expression looks like a combination of f, f′, f″ and f‴?

Look for an expression whose derivative it is, then count the zeros of that expression using Rolle between the given zeros.

How do I bound f when only f′ is bounded?

Apply the mean value theorem between the known point and the point asked.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking c outside (a,b).

• Using Rolle without checking f(a)=f(b).

• Counting zeros of f′ at the end points.

• Assuming the result holds with ‘at every point’ when it holds for ‘some point’.

Completed notes – Type 5

1. How do I spot this type?

• A figure is inscribed in another (rectangle in a parabola, cylinder in a sphere, rectangle in a triangle) and the maximum area or volume is asked.

• A wire or sheet of fixed length is cut or folded to form shapes and the combined area is to be a maximum or minimum.

• An open box is made by cutting squares from the corners.

2. Formulae and laws

Rectangle in y=a−x2 below the parabola

area =2x(a−x2), maximum at x=a3

Cylinder in a sphere of radius R

h=2R3, Vmax=4πR333

Cone of slant height l

V=13πr2h with r2+h2=l2, maximum at h=l3

Box from a a×a sheet

V=x(a−2x)2, maximum at x=a6

Triangle in a circle of radius r

maximum area for the equilateral triangle =334r2

3. Concepts and skills

How do I reduce the area to one variable?

Use the constraint (the equation of the curve, or the fixed length) to eliminate one variable.

Do I maximise the area or its square?

Either; the square removes the roots and has the same critical point.

How do I check it is a maximum?

Use f″<0 or the sign change of f′, and compare with the end values.

What about the wire cut into two pieces?

Let the pieces be x and L−x, write the total area, differentiate and equate to zero.

Why is the answer sometimes at the end of the interval?

If no critical point lies inside the allowed range, the extreme value is at an end point.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Not eliminating one variable before differentiating.

• Using the wrong dimension (width 2x versus x for a symmetric figure).

• Giving the value of x when the area is asked.

• Not rejecting a critical point outside the domain.

Completed notes – Type 6

1. How do I spot this type?

• The question asks for the number of real roots or solutions of a polynomial or exponential equation.

• A parameter a is to be found so that the equation has three distinct real roots.

• The equation has a high power such as x7 and no direct factorisation.

2. Formulae and laws

Roots from extrema

for a cubic with local maximum M and minimum m: three roots if M>0>m

Monotonic function

f′(x)>0 for all x⇒ exactly one real root (for odd degree)

Descartes' sign rule

positive roots ≤ number of sign changes in f(x); negative roots: in f(−x)

Parameter form

5x3−15x=a: roots are the intersections of y=a with the graph of 5x3−15x

3. Concepts and skills

What is the standard method?

Let f(x) be the left side, find its local maximum and minimum and look at the signs of f at them.

How do I count roots of x7−7x−2=0?

Find f′(x)=7(x6−1), the extrema at x=±1, and check the signs of f(−1) and f(1) along with the behaviour at infinity.

How do I handle exponentials like e4x+2e3x−ex−6?

Study f′ or put ex=t>0 and count positive roots of the polynomial in t.

Why do I need the limits at ±∞?

They tell the sign of f far away, which fixes the number of crossings.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Counting a double root twice.

• Forgetting to check the signs of f at both extrema.

• Ignoring the condition t>0 after the substitution t=ex.

• Assuming a polynomial of odd degree has three roots.

Completed notes – Type 7

1. How do I spot this type?

• A polynomial of a given degree has stated critical points, maxima or minima and values, and a value or a set is asked.

• A limit such as limx→0f(x)x3=1 is given along with extrema.

• The question asks which statement about maxima, minima or symmetry is true.

2. Formulae and laws

Derivative from critical points

f′(x)=k(x−p)(x−q)⋯

Limit at zero

limx→0f(x)xn=c means f starts with cxn

Even and odd polynomials

critical points ±1,0 for a quartic suggest f(x)=ax4+bx2+c

Cubic with f′(a)=0 and f″(b)=0

use f′(x)=3p(x−a)(x−a′) and the condition on f″

3. Concepts and skills

How do I begin?

Write the polynomial with unknown coefficients, or write f′ using its known roots, and count the conditions.

What does the limit f(x)x3→1 tell me?

The constant term and the terms of degree 1 and 2 of f are zero, and the coefficient of x3 is 1.

How do I decide a local maximum or minimum from f′?

Look at the sign of f′ on both sides, or use f″.

How do I find the set f(x)=f(0)?

Factorise f(x)−f(0) using the structure of f (usually a perfect square times a quadratic).

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking too few conditions, so the polynomial is not completely determined.

• Mixing up the local minimum and local maximum at x=±1.

• Assuming the polynomial is even or odd when it is not given.

• Not rechecking the answer in the original conditions.

Completed notes – Type 8

1. How do I spot this type?

• The shortest distance between a line and a curve, or two curves, or a point and a curve is asked.

• A point moves along a curve and the nearest position is required.

• The sum of squares of distances to two fixed points is to be a minimum.

2. Formulae and laws

Distance squared

d2=(x−x1)2+(y−y1)2 with y=f(x)

Nearest point on a curve to a line

the tangent at that point is parallel to the line

Distance from a point to a line

|ax1+by1+c|a2+b2

Between two circles or a circle and a curve

distance between centres minus the radii

3. Concepts and skills

What is the quickest method for a curve and a line?

Find the point where the tangent to the curve is parallel to the line, then use the distance from the point to the line.

How do I find the shortest distance from a point to a curve?

Minimise d2; at the nearest point the normal passes through the given point.

What about two curves such as a parabola and a circle?

Reduce to the distance from the centre of the circle to the parabola and subtract the radius.

Why minimise d2 instead of d?

It removes the square root and has the same critical points.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Minimising the vertical distance instead of the shortest distance.

• Forgetting to subtract the radius for a circle.

• Using a point of the curve that does not satisfy the domain, such as x≥2 for y2=x−2.

• Leaving the answer as d2.

Completed notes – Type 9

1. How do I spot this type?

• A quantity (volume, area, length, radius, height) changes at a given rate and the rate of another quantity is asked at a certain instant.

• Water is poured into a cone, a balloon is inflated, ice melts on a sphere, or a ladder slides along a wall.

• The words ‘at the rate of’ appear with a time unit.

2. Formulae and laws

Chain rule

dVdt=dVdr⋅drdt

Sphere

V=43πr3,S=4πr2

Cone

V=13πr2h, and r=htanα for semi-vertical angle α

Cube

V=a3,S=6a2

Ladder

x2+y2=l2⇒xdxdt+ydydt=0

3. Concepts and skills

What is the first step?

Write the relation between the quantities and eliminate extra variables, for example r=htanα in a cone.

Why differentiate with respect to t?

Because all the given rates are with respect to time.

A layer of ice of thickness x melts on a ball of radius R; what is the volume?

V=43π((R+x)3−R3); differentiate with respect to t.

When do I substitute the instant values?

After differentiating, never before.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Substituting the instant value before differentiating.

• Wrong sign: a decreasing quantity has a negative rate.

• Mixing units (cm, m, minutes, seconds).

• Forgetting to remove a variable before differentiating.
