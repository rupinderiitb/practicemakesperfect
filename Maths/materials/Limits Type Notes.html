How to use these notes

1. Do these pages BEFORE the practice sheet, one type at a time.

2. Close all books and notes. Fill the blanks from memory; leave a gap if you cannot recall.

3. Then press “Show completed notes” under that type, correct your answers in a different colour (or your own notes), and note what you missed.

4. Now do that type's questions on the sheet. Come back to the notes after the sheet to add anything new.

5. Re-fill the same page a week later with the prompts covered – this is what makes it stick.

Typing in the notes boxes
  • Your answers are saved on this device only. Use “Save my notes to a file” to keep a copy or move to another device.
  • Tap a box and use the bar at the bottom of the screen.
  • Subscript or superscript: select the text and press x₂ or xⁿ, or press the button, type, and press it again.
  • Back to normal text: press Aa, or press the right arrow at the end of the subscript or superscript.
  • Fraction: press a/b, type the top, press Tab, type the bottom, press Tab again to continue.
  • You can also just type 1/2 or sqrt(x).
Keyboard shortcuts (computer keyboard)
  • Ctrl+H: superscript
  • Ctrl+L: subscript
  • Ctrl+Space: back to normal text
  • Ctrl+F: fraction (Tab moves from top to bottom to out)
  • Ctrl+I: integral ∫
  • Ctrl+S: sigma Σ
  • Ctrl+R: square root √
  • Ctrl+P: pi π
  • Ctrl+E: infinity ∞
  • Ctrl+> (Ctrl+Shift+. on most keyboards): arrow →; press again for the “implies” arrow ⇒
  • These work only while you are typing in a notes box. On a phone use the bar at the bottom.

Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'limx→0(cosx)cotx (or (tan(π4+x))1/x) is equal to'.

• A power whose base tends to 1 and whose exponent tends to ∞.

• x→∞ or n→∞ with radicals, sums or products of many terms.

2. Formulae and laws

1∞ form

lim(1+u)v=elimuv when u→0, v→∞

Standard

ex−1x→1,ln(1+x)x→1

Expansion

ex=1+x+x22+⋯,ln(1+x)=x−x22+⋯

At infinity

divide by the highest power; x2+bx+c−x→b2

3. Concepts and skills

How do I do a 1∞ limit?

Write base =1+u, find limuv and put it in the exponent of e.

What if the base is not near 1 in form?

Write it as 1+(base−1) and compute base −1 as a ratio first.

How do I handle x→∞ with radicals?

Rationalise, then divide numerator and denominator by the highest power of x.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Forgetting to subtract 1 from the base before multiplying by the exponent.

• Cancelling ∞−∞ without rationalising.

• Using ex≈1+x when the denominator needs x3 terms.

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'limx→0sin2(πcos4x)x4 (or limtan(πcos2θ)sin(2πsin2θ)) is equal to'.

• A ratio with sin, tan, cos or their inverses at 0, π4 or 1.

• A radical or product of cosines next to a trigonometric ratio.

2. Formulae and laws

Basic

sinxx→1,tanxx→1,1−cosxx2→12

Shift

put x=a+h and let h→0

Identities

1−cosx=2sin2x2,sin2x=2sinxcosx

Rationalise

A−B=A−BA+B

3. Concepts and skills

How do I use sinf(x)f(x)→1?

Check f(x)→0, then multiply and divide by f(x).

What if the limit point is not 0?

Substitute x=a+h, expand sin(a+h) and use h→0.

How do I treat cos4x inside sin(πcos4x)?

Use sin(π−θ)=sinθ with θ=π(1−cos4x), then reduce 1−cos4x.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Applying sinxx→1 when the argument does not tend to 0.

• Dropping the factor 2 in 1−cosx=2sin2x2.

• Forgetting to rationalise a radical before using the standard limit.

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'If limx→0cos2x+acos4x−bx4 is finite, then a+b is equal to'.

• The limit is given as a number (or 'exists') and a,b,c,α,β,γ,λ,μ are unknown.

• The answer is a combination such as a+b, 16(a2+b2+c2) or α+β+γ.

2. Formulae and laws

Finite 00

coefficients of all powers below the denominator's order are 0

Series

cosx=1−x22+x424,sinx=x−x36

More

ex=1+x+x22+x36,ln(1+x)=x−x22+x33

Matching

the leading coefficient equals the given limit

3. Concepts and skills

How do I get the equations?

Expand every term to the order of the denominator, collect powers of x, and set each lower coefficient to 0.

How many equations do I need?

One for each unknown: the vanishing coefficients plus the one equal to the limit.

What if the constants are linked to roots?

Use sum and product of roots to rewrite the expression, then reduce it with the standard limit.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Stopping the expansion one order too early.

• Sign slips in ln(1+x) and e−x expansions.

• Not using the 'finite' condition to kill the lower powers.

Completed notes – Type 4

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'Let f be differentiable with f(2)=4, f′(2)=1. Then limx→2x2f(2)−4f(x)x−2 is'.

• The limit contains f(x) with f unknown but f(a), f′(a) given.

• A definite integral with a variable limit divided by a power of x.

2. Formulae and laws

Definition

f′(a)=limx→af(x)−f(a)x−a

Leibniz

ddx∫g(x)h(x)F(t)dt=F(h)h′−F(g)g′

L'Hôpital

for 00 differentiate numerator and denominator

Add and subtract

insert ±af(a) to build f(x)−f(a)

3. Concepts and skills

How do I use f′(a) in a limit?

Add and subtract a term so the numerator becomes f(x)−f(a) times a constant, plus a term that cancels.

How do I do an integral limit?

Check it is 00, apply Leibniz to the numerator, and repeat until the denominator is nonzero.

What if f is only given by an equation?

Differentiate or use the limit definition to get a differential equation for f.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using f′(x) where only f′(a) is known.

• Missing h′(x) in Leibniz's rule.

• Using L'Hôpital when the form is not 00 or ∞∞.

• 'The set of values of a for which limx→a([x−5]−[2x+2])=0'.

Just left of 0

Just right of 0

Fractional part

3. Concepts and skills

How do I evaluate [x] in a limit?

Find the integer just left or right of the point and substitute it before simplifying.

How do I find the values where the limit exists?

Equate the left and right limits; only integer points of the argument can break it.

What if x→a and [g(x)] is constant near a?

The limit is that constant; no jump occurs there.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking [x]=0 for x→0−.

• Treating [2x+2] as 2[x]+2.

• Ignoring one-sided limits.

Completed notes – Type 6

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'limx→1x4−1x−1=limx→kx3−k3x2−k2, then k is'.

• A ratio of cube roots such as (a+2x)1/3−(3x)1/3(3a+x)1/3−(4x)1/3 at x=a.

• A 00 form with powers or roots and no trigonometric terms.

2. Formulae and laws

Power rule

xn−anx−a→nan−1

Conjugate

A−B=A−BA+B

Fractional

substitute x=tm to clear roots

Cube roots

a3−b3=(a−b)(a2+ab+b2)

3. Concepts and skills

How do I use the power rule?

Write numerator and denominator as differences of powers of x and a, then take nan−1 for each.

When do I rationalise?

When the form is 00 and a square root appears in the numerator or denominator.

How do I handle cube roots?

Use the a3−b3 identity or the power rule with exponent 13.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using the power rule with the wrong limit point a.

• Not multiplying the denominator by the conjugate too.

• Arithmetic slips with fractional exponents.
