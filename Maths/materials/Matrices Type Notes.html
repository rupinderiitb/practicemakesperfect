2. Close all books and notes. Fill the blanks from memory; leave a gap if you cannot recall.

3. Then press “Show completed notes” under that type, correct your answers in a different colour (or your own notes), and note what you missed.

4. Now do that type's questions on the sheet. Come back to the notes after the sheet to add anything new.

5. Re-fill the same page a week later with the prompts covered – this is what makes it stick.

Completed notes – Type 1

1. How do I spot this type?

• The question gives |A| (or A itself) and asks for the determinant of an expression such as adj(adj(2A)) or adj(3Aadj(2A)).

• The answer is written as 2m3n5p and a sum like m+n or 3m+2n is asked.

• adjA is given and |A|, |adj(adjA)| or |A−1adjA| is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Determinant of the adjoint

|adjA|=|A|n−1 (for 3×3: |A|2)

Scalar multiple

|kA|=kn|A| (for 3×3: k3|A|)

Adjoint of adjoint

adj(adjA)=|A|n−2A

Adjoint of a product and a scalar

adj(AB)=adjBadjA; adj(kA)=kn−1adjA

Key identity

AadjA=adjAA=|A|I; A−1=adjA|A|

Inverse

|A−1|=1|A|; adj(A−1)=(adjA)−1

3. Concepts and skills

How do I handle a nested expression like |adj(3Aadj(2A))|?

Work from the inside: |adj(2A)|=|2A|2, then multiply the determinants inside the outer adjoint and apply |adjX|=|X|2 again.

What does adj(adjA) become for 3×3?

|A|A, so |adj(adjA)|=|A|3|A|=|A|4.

How is |A| found when adjA is given?

Use |adjA|=|A|2; take the positive root unless the sign is fixed by the question.

How do I get 2m3n5p quickly?

Keep every factor as a power of a prime from the start (for example |3A|=33|A|) and add the exponents at the end.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using |kA|=k|A| instead of kn|A|.

• Applying |adjX|=|X|n−1 to the wrong matrix in a nested chain (the inner matrix is not A).

• Forgetting that adj(kA)=kn−1adjA and writing kadjA.

• Mixing up |adjA|=|A|n−1 with adjA=|A|n−1.

Completed notes – Type 2

1. How do I spot this type?

• A large power (A50, A2025, A100) or a sum A+A2+…+Am is asked.

• The matrix has cosθ,sinθ entries, or entries like ω, i, 12.

• A relation like An=An−2+A2−I or A2=AT is given to make the powers repeat.

2. Formulae and laws

Binomial for nilpotent N

(I+N)n=I+nN+(n2)N2 (if N3=O)

Rotation matrix

[cosθ−sinθsinθcosθ]n=[cosnθ−sinnθsinnθcosnθ]

Unit upper-triangular

[1a01]n=[1na01]

Period

If Ak=I then An=Anmodk

Complex entries

ω3=1, 1+ω+ω2=0, i2=−1

Sum of a series

A+A2+…+Am: find a pattern in Ak and add, or use I+N form

3. Concepts and skills

How do I know that A=I+N works?

Subtract I from A; if the result squares (or cubes) to the zero matrix, use the binomial theorem.

What if A is not of the form I+N?

Compute A2,A3 and look for a pattern (often A2=A, A3=I or a shifting entry), then prove it by induction.

How do I use a given relation like An=An−2+A2−I?

Use it to reduce the exponent step by step (for example A50=A48+A2−I) and add the pieces.

How are sums of the elements of An asked?

Take the vector of ones X and use XTAnX, or find An and add the entries.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Multiplying entry by entry instead of row by column.

• Using (A+B)n=∑(nk)An−kBk when A and B do not commute.

• Writing cos(nθ) as cosnθ.

• Forgetting the sign in A−n (rotation by −nθ).

Completed notes – Type 3

1. How do I spot this type?

• A relation such as AAT=I, AB=B or A5=B5 is given and a determinant, an entry or the inverse of an expression is asked.

• The question asks whether a matrix with et,sint,cost entries is invertible for all, some or no t.

• An equation AX=λX or PnQ=Q must hold for a non-zero X or for all a,b,c,d.

2. Formulae and laws

Orthogonal matrix

AAT=I⇒ rows are unit vectors, |A|=±1, A−1=AT

Inverse

A−1=adjA|A|; (AB)−1=B−1A−1

Eigen-type equation

AX=λX, X≠0⇒|A−λI|=0

Idempotent / projection

A=I−2MMT with MTM=1 gives A2=I, so λ=±1

3. Concepts and skills

How do I use AAT=I?

Multiply A by AT and equate with I: each row has sum of squares 1 and different rows are perpendicular.

How do I test invertibility for all real t?

Find |A| as a function of t; if it is a non-zero constant times a positive function like e−t, A is invertible for every t.

How do I use AB=B with B≠O?

Write (A−I)B=O; B is a non-zero vector, so |A−I|=0, and use the trace and determinant of A.

What to do when the question gives powers of A and B without saying they commute?

Check whether the given conditions force commutation; otherwise factorising A2−B2 is not allowed.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Assuming AB=BA when it is not given.

• Cancelling a matrix factor without checking that it is invertible.

• Taking |A|=1 for an orthogonal matrix (it can be −1).

• Using (AB)−1=A−1B−1.

Completed notes – Type 4

1. How do I spot this type?

• 'The number of matrices of order 2 or 3 with entries from {0,1,2,3}' (or {−1,0,1}) such that …

• The condition is on the sum of the entries, the trace of AAT, the determinant, singularity or A=A−1.

• The answer is a number of matrices (an integer such as 27, 204, 5376).

2. Formulae and laws

Multiplication principle

Number of matrices =kmn for an m×n matrix with k choices per entry

Symmetric matrices

Number of symmetric matrices of order n with k choices: kn(n+1)/2

Trace of AAT

tr(AAT)=∑aij2

Entries summing to S

Solutions of x1+…+xr=S: use stars and bars after fixing the allowed values

Determinant of 2×2

ad−bc=D: list the pairs (a,d) and (b,c) with the required products

3. Concepts and skills

How do I count matrices with tr(AAT)=6 and entries in {−1,0,1}?

This means exactly 6 of the 9 entries are non-zero: (96)⋅26.

How do I count 2×2 singular matrices?

Count the pairs with ad=bc for the given set, using the products of the pairs.

How do I count matrices whose entries sum to a prime?

List the primes in the range, count the ordered solutions for each prime and add.

What about A=A−1?

This means A2=I; use a2+bc=1, b(a+d)=0, c(a+d)=0, d2+bc=1 and count the cases.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Counting unordered selections instead of ordered matrices.

• Forgetting the sign choices (±1) for each non-zero entry.

• Missing the cases in which a+d=0 or b=c=0.

• Double counting matrices that satisfy two cases at once.

Completed notes – Type 5

1. How do I spot this type?

• A relation like A2=3A+αI, A3=4A2−A−21I or A−1=αA+βI is given.

• A power such as A4, A5 or (I+A)8 is to be written as pA+qI.

• The constants α,β,γ or a sum like α+β+γ is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Cayley–Hamilton (2×2)

A2−(trA)A+|A|I=O

Inverse from it

A−1=1|A|((trA)I−A)

Reducing powers

A2=pA+qI⇒An+1=pAn+qAn−1

3×3 form

A3−(trA)A2+(sum of minors)A−|A|I=O

Binomial with I

(I+A)n=∑(nk)Ak, since I commutes with A

3. Concepts and skills

How do I use A2+γA+18I=O for a 2×2 matrix?

Compare with A2−(trA)A+|A|I=O, so |A|=18.

How do I find A4 from A2=3A+αI?

Square the relation, replace A2 again and collect the coefficients of A and I.

What if the relation is given without the trace?

Use the Cayley–Hamilton relation of A to eliminate the unknown entries first, then compare coefficients.

How do I find A−1 in the form αA+βI?

Multiply the Cayley–Hamilton relation by A−1.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Using |A|=trA or mixing up the trace and the determinant in the relation.

• Writing A2=αA+β without the identity matrix.

• Applying the 2×2 relation to a 3×3 matrix.

• Comparing coefficients when A is a scalar multiple of I (the relation is then not unique).

Completed notes – Type 6

1. How do I spot this type?

• Two or three matrices are given and a trace, a determinant or one entry of a product is asked.

• A matrix equation such as A[10]=… determines the entries of A.

• A question on elementary row operations asks which matrix can not be obtained.

2. Formulae and laws

Product

(AB)ij=∑kaikbkj

Trace

tr(A+B)=trA+trB; tr(AB)=tr(BA)

Determinant of a product

|AB|=|A||B|; |AT|=|A|

Transpose of a product

(AB)T=BTAT

Row operation

Ri→Ri+kRj keeps the determinant; Ri→kRi multiplies it by k

3. Concepts and skills

How do I find unknown entries from AX=Y with several vectors?

Write each equation as a set of linear equations for the entries, or combine the vectors into one matrix equation.

How do I find trA−trB from A+2B and 2A−B?

Solve for A and B (or for their traces directly) as in a pair of simultaneous equations.

When is a matrix obtained by a single row operation?

Compare each row of the new matrix with the old one; exactly one row may change, by adding a multiple of another row or scaling.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Multiplying matrices in the wrong order (AB≠BA).

• Adding matrices of different orders.

• Forgetting that one row operation changes only one row.

• Reading a column vector as a row vector.

Completed notes – Type 7

1. How do I spot this type?

• A is symmetric and B skew-symmetric and the type of an expression like AB−BA, A13B26−B26A13 is asked.

• A matrix is written as P+Q with P symmetric and Q skew-symmetric, and |P| or |Q| is asked.

• A count of symmetric matrices with entries from a set is asked.

2. Formulae and laws

Definitions

AT=A (symmetric); AT=−A (skew-symmetric)

Decomposition

A=1/2(A+AT)+1/2(A−AT)

Transpose of a power

(Ak)T=(AT)k

Commutator tests

(AB−BA)T=BTAT−ATBT; for A symmetric and B skew: (AB+BA)T=−(AB+BA)

Skew-symmetric diagonal

Diagonal entries are 0; for odd order |B|=0

3. Concepts and skills

How do I decide whether AB−BA is symmetric or skew?

Take its transpose using (XY)T=YTXT and compare with the original.

What is the type of B2k and B2k+1 for skew-symmetric B?

Even powers are symmetric; odd powers are skew-symmetric.

How do I use A+B given with A symmetric and B skew-symmetric?

A=12(M+MT) and B=12(M−MT) where M=A+B.

4. Mistakes to avoid

• Taking (AB)T=ATBT.

• Forgetting that odd powers of a skew-symmetric matrix stay skew-symmetric.

• Assuming the product of two symmetric matrices is symmetric (it is only if they commute).

• Writing a non-zero diagonal for a skew-symmetric matrix.
